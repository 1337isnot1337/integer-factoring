# Complete profiles for Gaussian circuits with one-mode matrix factors

The complete Gaussian Smith theorem extends to products containing
arbitrary invertible two-by-two maps on individual occupation modes.
The maps may mix the vacant and occupied states, have rational
coefficients, and occur on any modes between Gaussian factors.
One auxiliary mode makes the whole product Gaussian by an integral
permutation. Thus a matrix of order \(2n+2\) determines the complete
\(2^n\)-dimensional Smith profile.

This is a uniform characteristic-two theorem. It extends
[the quadratic Clifford theorem](clifford-gaussian-smith-profiles.md),
whose original hypothesis requires preservation of the linear CAR span.
The new one-mode factors generally preserve only its affine extension.
No general symplectic-to-spin lifting assertion is needed.

## 1. Statement

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic two,
\(O=k[[t]]\), \(K=k((t))\), and
\(\mathcal F_n=\bigwedge K^n\), with its occupation basis.
Write \(C_i,A_i\) for creation and annihilation. They satisfy

\[
 C_i^2=A_i^2=0,\qquad C_iA_j+A_jC_i=\delta_{ij},
\]

and distinct-mode operators commute. A one-mode factor is any
\(X\in\mathrm{GL}_2(K)\) acting on the vacant/occupied pair of one
mode and as the identity on all other modes.

Let \(G\) be a product of invertible Gaussian factors, scalar factors,
and one-mode factors, in any order. The Gaussian factors may be
generated by exterior mode changes, creation-pair and annihilation-pair
shears, and particle-hole exchanges, as in the existing Clifford proof.

**Theorem.** There is an explicitly constructed Gaussian operator
\(\widetilde G\) on \(n+1\) modes, with Clifford matrix
\(\widetilde B\) of order \(2n+2\), such that:

1. every Smith order of \(G\) occurs exactly twice in \(\widetilde G\);
2. the signed Smith orders of \(\widetilde B\) are
   \(-a_{n+1},\ldots,-a_1,a_1,\ldots,a_{n+1}\), with \(a_i\ge0\);
3. at least one \(a_i\) is zero;
4. after removing one zero gap, the complete profile of \(G\) is

\[
 \boxed{\left\{\mu+\sum_{i\in S}a_i:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}},
 \qquad
 \mu=2^{-n}v_t(\det G)-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\in\mathbb Z.
 \tag{1}
\]

The statement is about the complete multiset, including its scalar
shift. An integral common clearing is allowed, but its valuation must
be retained in that shift. The same theorem applies at infinity after
reversing the parameter in the complete rational maps.

## 2. An integral auxiliary-mode permutation

Add one auxiliary mode and put

\[
 R=C_*+A_*,\qquad R^2=I.
\]

For an original occupation state \(S\), define

\[
 U\bigl(|S\rangle\otimes v\bigr)
   =|S\rangle\otimes R^{|S|\bmod2}v.
 \tag{2}
\]

This exchanges the two auxiliary basis states precisely when \(S\)
has odd occupation. Hence \(U\) is a constant permutation matrix,
\(U^2=I\), and both \(U\) and its inverse are unimodular over \(O\).
Directly on occupation states,

\[
 U(C_i\otimes I)U=C_iR,\qquad
 U(A_i\otimes I)U=A_iR.
 \tag{3}
\]

For example, an annihilation changes occupation parity by one, so the
two powers of \(R\) contributed by (2) have product \(R\).
The same calculation applies to creation.

Set

\[
 \widetilde G=U(G\otimes I_2)U.
 \tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) proves the duplication of Smith orders at once. It also
shows that the construction is multiplicative: a single auxiliary
mode works for an entire product, regardless of which original modes
the factors act on.

## 3. Every lifted factor is Gaussian

A product of two original CAR operators acquires two commuting \(R\)
factors under (3), which cancel. Thus creation-pair, annihilation-pair,
and mixed quadratic shears are unchanged. Exterior mode changes
preserve occupation parity and also commute with \(U\).
A particle-hole exchange becomes

\[
 (C_i+A_i)R,
\]

a product of two invertible Clifford vectors. Its conjugation preserves
the enlarged linear Clifford span, so it too is Gaussian.

For a one-mode upper or lower shear, (3) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I+bA_iR&=(I+bA_iC_*)(I+bA_iA_*),\\
 I+cC_iR&=(I+cC_iC_*)(I+cC_iA_*).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]

The cross term in either product is zero because \(A_i^2=C_i^2=0\).
Every factor on the right is quadratic Gaussian. A diagonal one-mode
matrix is a scalar times an exterior scaling, and is unchanged by \(U\).

Every invertible two-by-two matrix is a product of these shears,
diagonal matrices, and a swap. Explicitly, if
\(X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\), \(a\ne0\), and
\(D=ad+bc\), then

\[
 X=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\c/a&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&D/a\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&b/a\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{6}
\]

If \(a=0\), an initial swap makes the upper-left entry nonzero.
The decomposition takes place over \(K\); no regularity assumption is
made on its individual factors. Equations (5)--(6) prove that every
lifted one-mode factor is Gaussian.

Consequently \(\widetilde G\) meets the quadratic hypothesis of the
existing Clifford theorem. That theorem is stated for arbitrary mode
counts; it does not require \(n+1\) to be a power of two.

## 4. Removing the auxiliary multiplicity and keeping the mean

Apply the quadratic Clifford theorem to \(\widetilde G\). Its profile
is a scalar shift plus subset sums of \(n+1\) nonnegative gaps.
If exactly \(z\) gaps vanish, the smallest order has multiplicity
\(2^z\). By (4), every multiplicity is even. Thus \(z\ge1\).
Removing one zero gap divides every multiplicity by two and recovers
exactly the profile of \(G\).

Furthermore,

\[
 \det\widetilde G=(\det G)^2,\qquad
 2^{-(n+1)}v_t(\det\widetilde G)=2^{-n}v_t(\det G).
 \tag{7}
\]

The mean is unchanged. Removing a zero gap does not change its sum.
The scalar formula (1) therefore follows directly from the existing
theorem, including the integrality of \(\mu\).

In particular, a complete counting function is

\[
 Z_G(z)=z^\mu\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+z^{a_i}).
 \tag{8}
\]

This identity is a structural classification, not a pressure lower
bound. A physical construction still has its own alphabet weights,
endpoint conditions, polynomial degrees, and scalar valuations.
Changing the gaps can change its pressure, and those costs remain
part of any transfer calculation.

## 5. A direct four-dimensional update

The auxiliary lift does not require expanding a Fock matrix.
For a canonical pair \(u,v\) and auxiliary generators \(e,f\), use
coordinate order \((u,v,e,f)\). The lifted action of
\(X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\), with \(D=ad+bc\ne0\),
has Clifford matrix

\[
 B_X=\frac1D
 \begin{pmatrix}
 d^2&c^2&cd&cd\\
 b^2&a^2&ab&ab\\
 bd&ac&ad&bc\\
 bd&ac&bc&ad
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{9}
\]

This follows by conjugating the four generators by the explicit
four-dimensional operator \(U(X\otimes I_2)U\). Multiplication by
its adjugate gives the displayed numerator. In particular, \(e+f\)
is fixed, and (9) preserves the split quadratic form
\(x_1x_2+x_3x_4\).

The formula applies to a Gaussian-transported canonical pair as well.
If \(T\) is the inclusion of \((u,v,e,f)\) into the enlarged Clifford
space and \(T^\vee\) is its polar-form left inverse, its full action is

\[
 I+T(B_X-I_4)T^\vee.
 \tag{10}
\]

Thus each one-mode factor gives a rank-at-most-four rational update.
One common auxiliary mode is retained throughout all updates.

For rational polynomial input, one may keep a common denominator:
if \(\widetilde B=N/d\), its signed Smith orders are the polynomial
Smith orders of \(N\), each shifted by \(-v_t(d)\). The denominator
is never discarded. A polynomial Smith computation on \(2n+2\) rows
and the determinant mean suffice to recover (8). This gives a compact
complete profile in polynomial time in the usual dense-polynomial
input model. Writing all \(2^n\) orders individually is unnecessary.
Sparse exponents specified in binary do not make their coefficient
expansion free.

## 6. Exact implementation for the literal full-tail family

[tensor_affine_clifford_profiles.py](../tools/tensor_affine_clifford_profiles.py)
applies (9)--(10) to the eight active modes in
[tensor-literal-full-tail-core.md](tensor-literal-full-tail-core.md).
The resulting Clifford matrix has order 18. This replaces the
256-dimensional active Fock Smith calculation for full-rank one-mode
frames. Both places use the previously fixed physical gauges, including
parameter reversal in every map at infinity.

Let \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_8\) be the active Gaussian diagonal
orders. Suppose the four weighted local matrices are cleared to
\(X_1,\ldots,X_4\) with total clearing power \(C\). Their exact mean is

\[
 2\bar\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{8}\lambda_i
                +\sum_{j=1}^{4}v_t(\det X_j)-2C.
 \tag{11}
\]

Indeed, on \(n\) occupation modes, a one-mode factor has determinant
\((\det X_j)^{2^{n-1}}\). Gaussian conjugation of that factor does not
change its determinant. Each common clearing contributes its scalar
valuation once to the mean. Equation (11) includes all these terms.
The program checks the signed pairing of all 18 Clifford orders,
the auxiliary zero pair, and the integrality of (1).

The coefficient implementation uses F4 polynomial arithmetic.
The theorem itself does not restrict the coefficient field to F4.
Its generic scope also permits one-mode factors on many different modes
interleaved with Gaussian operations; the present full-tail search
interface specializes to the lowest-mode physical frames.

The native series window must resolve the full promised rank 18.
Failure to resolve that rank raises an error. No capped Smith values
are accepted in this evaluator.

## 7. Independent exact controls

The [checker](../tools/check_tensor_affine_clifford_profiles.py) and its
[receipt](../evidence/tensor-affine-clifford-controls.json) cover three
separate parts of the construction.

First, the auxiliary permutation is checked on 252 states, with 2,568
creation/annihilation state comparisons. The local formula (9) is checked
as a universal degree-two polynomial identity: substitution
\(a=t,b=t^3,c=t^9,d=t^{27}\) separates every monomial of degree at most
two in each variable. All 64 conjugation entries and 16 adjugate entries
agree exactly.

Second, all 131,072 complete active orders in the earlier
[binary affine census](../evidence/tensor-literal-full-tail-affine-m16-census.json)
are reconstructed from the 18-dimensional calculation and compared
individually. This tests all 512 cuts, including the determinant mean
and Laurent clearing corrections.

Third, two nonbinary polynomial frame pairs are contracted directly
against the original raw Laurent Pfaffian kernel. They use polynomial
degrees two and three and assigned low heights one and two.
All 262,144 raw coefficient entries and endpoints pass.
All 1,024 complete literal Fock Smith orders agree with the smaller
calculation. A separate restriction-of-scalars binary elimination checks
32 initial F4 Toeplitz ranks. These prefix ranks are additional controls;
the complete-order comparison uses the full literal matrices.

~~~sh
python tools/check_tensor_affine_clifford_profiles.py \
  --output /tmp/tensor-affine-clifford-controls.json
~~~

## 8. Larger degree and coefficient search

The [search driver](../tools/tensor_affine_clifford_search.py) retains
the complete binomial profile and scalar shift of every completed case.
Its pressure fits include the full Gaussian spectator products and the
physical alphabet. All coefficient degrees remain charged.

The first [completed F4 census](../evidence/tensor-affine-clifford-f4-nilpotent-m16-profiles.json)
uses \(M=16\), physical lowest-mode heights \((0,1)\), and

\[
 L(t)=I+(1+t^d)A,\qquad
 P(t)=I+(1+t^d)B,
\]

where \(A^2=B^2=0\). It tests all 16 nilpotent two-by-two matrices over
F4 on each side, including zero, for each
\(d\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64\}\).
There are 1,792 completed cases and 3,584 complete cut profiles,
encoding 917,504 active orders without capped tails.
Both determinants are identically one in this family, but the
polynomial degree is still part of the physical map.

The best observed numerical fit at every tested degree is the known
Gaussian control,
\(\rho=0.7083157559380195\). No strict improvement was found.
The coefficient catalogue and Smith profiles are exact; the
one-variable numerical minima are not globally certified.
This finite census does not exclude arbitrary polynomial frames.

~~~sh
python tools/tensor_affine_clifford_search.py \
  --modes 16 --degrees 1 2 4 8 16 32 64 --workers 4 \
  --output /tmp/tensor-affine-clifford-f4-nilpotent-m16-profiles.json
~~~

For larger F4 catalogues, optional Frobenius reduction identifies a
matrix pair with its coefficientwise square. The kernel and assigned
height exponents are defined over F2, so this field automorphism
preserves both complete Smith profiles. The driver records both the
representative count and the number of normalized matrix pairs covered.

The new uniform result is the complete-profile reduction with arbitrary
invertible one-mode factors. Rank-deficient physical compression and
general multi-mode endomorphisms remain outside this theorem. The
structural result and completed search do not improve the factoring
exponent by themselves.
