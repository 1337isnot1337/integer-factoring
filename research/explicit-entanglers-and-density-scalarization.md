# Explicit entanglers and the distinction between two scalarization tests

## Results and scope

The matrix-valued seed transfer motivates changes between two full tensor-factor structures on an auxiliary space. The earlier [generic nonscalarization argument](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/integer-factoring/blob/bb3bf763ed054f6fdaf882649c037ad810e16b97/research/generic-full-factor-nonscalarization.md) proves existence of structures without a normalized common product functional. The concrete certificates here address two different tests that must not be identified.

1. An explicit matrix `W_2 in GL_16(F_2)` has **no nonzero product vector whose image is a product vector**, over any extension of `F_2`. A `400`-by-`400` full-rank minor of a degree-`(3,3)` polynomial coefficient matrix proves this.
2. Two further explicit matrices have the same product-vector exclusion over every extension, but admit normalized functionals factorizing across both full factor structures over `F_4`. One of these changes of basis is itself binary. Thus the first property does **not** establish the generic note's stronger nonscalarization conclusion.

Both are finite constructive algebraic results. Neither matrix comes with an improving crossing identity or rank-sum profile. The factoring exponent remains `1/2`.

For `W_2`, a separate exhaustive finite-field computation also finds no normalized common product-density functional **over `F_2`**. That finite check does not exclude such a functional over an extension field.

## 1. The two tests

Write the auxiliary vector space as `V=F^4 tensor F^4`. Its original full factor algebras are

```math
\operatorname{Mat}_4(F)\otimes I_4,
\qquad I_4\otimes\operatorname{Mat}_4(F).
```

A change of basis `W` conjugates these into a second full factor pair. A common product ket is a nonzero vector `v` such that both `v` and `Wv` are product vectors. Replacing `W` with its inverse gives the equivalent convention used in the earlier transfer note.

A normalized common product functional has the form

```math
\ell(T)=\operatorname{Tr}(DT),\qquad
D=A\otimes B,\qquad
W^{-1}DW=C\otimes H,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(D)=1.       \tag{1}
```

The trace pairing on matrices is nondegenerate, including in characteristic two. Thus (1) is exactly the full-factor scalarization condition, not merely a sufficient special case. A functional from a bra and ket, `ell(T)=phi T psi`, has a rank-one density matrix. The common-product-ket test detects an obstruction to such a rank-one construction. It need not detect a higher-rank density in (1).

## 2. An exact polynomial certificate excluding every product ket

Use variables `u_0,...,u_3` and `v_0,...,v_3`, and set

```math
Y_{kl}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{3}W_{4k+l,4i+j}u_i v_j.
```

The matrix `Y` is the `4`-by-`4` reshape of `W(u tensor v)`. It has rank at most one precisely when its thirty-six `2`-by-`2` minors vanish. Denote these bihomogeneous polynomials of degree `(2,2)` by `F_e`.

Multiply every `F_e` by each of the sixteen monomials `u_i v_j`. This gives `576` polynomials of degree `(3,3)`. The space of all polynomials of that bidegree has dimension

```math
\binom{6}{3}^2=20^2=400.
```

The supplied certificate selects `400` of the `576` rows whose coefficient matrix is invertible over the stated field. Therefore the ideal generated by the minors contains **every** degree-`(3,3)` monomial, in particular every `u_i^3 v_j^3`.

Suppose nonzero vectors `u,v` over any extension field made all minors vanish. Choose indices with `u_i!=0` and `v_j!=0`. Evaluating the ideal identity for `u_i^3 v_j^3` would give zero, while `u_i^3 v_j^3!=0`. This contradiction proves the exclusion. Since `W` is invertible, the image of a nonzero product vector is nonzero, so the rank-at-most-one condition does not introduce a zero-image exception.

All equations, monomial orders, selected row indices, and field arithmetic are reconstructed by the replay. Invertibility of a finite matrix over the base field remains valid over every extension, which is why this certificate is stronger than enumerating product vectors over a few finite fields.

## 3. An explicit characteristic-two matrix

The first matrix is given by these sixteen binary row masks, with bit `j` representing column `j` and basis index `4i+j` representing `e_i tensor e_j`:

```text
16669, 37713, 29213, 55025, 12094, 39882, 4857, 53562,
35306, 23446, 20077, 45287, 25564, 14040, 53253, 54155.
```

The certificate is in [`../evidence/universal-entangler16.json`](../evidence/universal-entangler16.json). Its selected degree-`(3,3)` rows have rank `400`. The replay separately verifies that the original `16`-by-`16` matrix is invertible.

A supplied-channel construction using this `W_2` cannot be dismissed by finding a common product ket over a larger field. A normalized product-density scalarization, or scalarization on only the actual smaller factor spans, remains a separate question.

### The finite normalized-density screen

A product density of trace one over `F_2` must have `Tr(A)=Tr(B)=1`. Each `4`-by-`4` factor has fifteen free bits, so there are exactly `2^30=1,073,741,824` ordered factor pairs. Product densities are unique under their factors over `F_2`, since the only nonzero scalar is one.

The native checker enumerates all these pairs. It forms the realignment of `W_2^{-1}(A tensor B)W_2`; realignment places entry `(4i+k,4j+l)` at row `(i,j)`, column `(k,l)`. A nonzero matrix is a product precisely when this realignment has rank one. Over `F_2`, that is equivalent to all its nonzero rows being the same nonzero row.

Binary Gray order updates one free factor bit at a time. When the changed bit is diagonal, the distinguished diagonal bit changes with it, preserving trace one. Conjugation and realignment are linear in each factor, so their updates are exact XOR operations. Every factor pair is checked once. The recorded run finds zero product densities.

This is a complete result over the base field only. It supplies no extension-field exclusion for arbitrary densities.

## 4. An explicit counterexample to identifying the two tests

Let `F_4=F_2[omega]/(omega^2+omega+1)`. The data file encodes `0,1,omega,omega+1` by `0,1,2,3`.

Start with the diagonal matrices

```math
A_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\omega,\omega^2),\qquad
B=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^2,\omega^2,\omega,1).
```

The product `A_0 tensor B` has three eigenspaces of dimensions `5,5,6`. Choose the explicit block-preserving change of basis `W_4` recorded in [`../evidence/entangler-density-control.json`](../evidence/entangler-density-control.json). It commutes with this product matrix. Its own degree-`(3,3)` coefficient matrix also has a certified invertible `400`-row submatrix. Therefore `W_4` has no common product ket over the algebraic closure.

Nevertheless, set `A=omega A_0` and `D=A tensor B`. Then

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\omega,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(B)=\omega^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(D)=1,\qquad W_4^{-1}DW_4=D.             \tag{2}
```

Consequently `ell(T)=Tr(DT)` normalizes the identity and factorizes across both tensor structures:

```math
\ell((P\otimes I)(I\otimes Q))
 =\operatorname{Tr}(AP)\operatorname{Tr}(BQ),
```

and conjugation by `W_4` leaves the same expression unchanged. This is a complete normalized common product functional. The density is invertible of rank sixteen. It is not a rank-one bra-ket density.

The replay checks (2), all `512` matrix-unit factorization identities for the two structures, and the product-ket exclusion independently. Thus no implication from absence of a common product ket to absence of every normalized product functional is available, even in dimension sixteen and characteristic two.

## 5. A binary change of basis with an extension-field density

The explicit density control can be strengthened so that the change of
basis itself has entries in `F_2`. The normalized product density still uses
`F_4`. The following matrix has no product ket over any field extension, yet a
normalized product functional exists over `F_4`.

Let `W` be the binary matrix with row masks

```
32801, 35, 14400, 17160, 1042, 1026, 4100, 128,
16776, 17280, 33841, 6212, 10244, 14404, 256, 33793.
```

The basis order is `(i,j) -> 4i+j`, and bit `j` of a row mask is column `j`.
The record [`../evidence/binary-entangler-extension-control.json`](../evidence/binary-entangler-extension-control.json) contains the full matrix,
its 400 selected degree-(3,3) Macaulay rows, and the following density factors.
In `F_4 = F_2[ω]/(ω²+ω+1)`, set

```math
A=\operatorname{diag}(\omega,\omega,\omega^2,1),\qquad
B=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^2,\omega^2,\omega,1).
```

Then `Tr(A)=ω`, `Tr(B)=ω²`, and `Tr(A⊗B)=1`. Direct computation gives
`W⁻¹(A⊗B)W=A⊗B`. Thus `ell(T)=Tr((A⊗B)T)` factorizes across both full tensor
factor structures. This was checked on all 256 matrix-unit pairs in each
structure, giving 512 exact identities.

The 400 selected rows have rank 400 over `F_2`. As in the original explicit
entangler argument, they span every bidegree-(3,3) monomial, so the product-ket
exclusion holds over the algebraic closure and every extension field. The
certificate is independently replayed by the existing checker with this file
supplied through `--control`.

This record makes no assertion about the existence or absence of normalized
product densities over the base field `F_2`. It provides no improving crossing
seed. Its purpose is to separate product-ket exclusion from product-functional
exclusion even when the change of factor structures is binary.

## 6. Reproduction and remaining construction problem

The standard-library replay of the first two algebraic certificates is:

```sh
python3 tools/check_explicit_entanglers.py \
  --binary evidence/universal-entangler16.json \
  --control evidence/entangler-density-control.json \
  --output evidence/explicit-entangler-audit.json
```

Replay the binary extension-field control with:

```sh
python3 tools/check_explicit_entanglers.py \
  --binary evidence/universal-entangler16.json \
  --control evidence/binary-entangler-extension-control.json \
  --output evidence/binary-entangler-control-audit.json
```

The optional complete base-field density screen requires C++20:

```sh
python3 tools/check_density16_certificate.py --output evidence/density16-full.json
```

This wrapper compiles `tools/check_density16_native.cpp`, checks the exact
enumeration size and matrix against the algebraic certificate, and records
the source and executable hashes. The native calculation also checks
96 independent ordinary matrix conjugations and 67 realignment-rank
controls. These checks are included in `make verify-native`.

The generators used to find the displayed matrices are exploratory. The fixed data and independent replay are sufficient to check the stated certificates; no random-search assumption enters the proofs.

The larger matrix-valued interface still needs an actual crossing family with low supplied channels and verified global factor conditions. These matrices provide concrete coefficient structures and a necessary scalarization control. They do not reduce a channel count or supply a stronger factoring algorithm. The construction, certificate search, and replay used substantial OpenAI assistant support.
