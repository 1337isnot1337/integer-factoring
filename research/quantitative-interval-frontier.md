# How the interval family approaches the half-rate threshold

The explicit one-species interval family has exact finite certificates
very close to the half-rate boundary. It also has a sharp asymptotic
description of the remaining gap. Both statements concern the displayed
gauges and independent mode occupations; they do not optimize over all
physical gauges or all crossings.

The best complete factoring exponent remains `alpha = 1/2`.

## 1. Exact finite witnesses

The [interval construction](schur-one-species-interval.md) and the
[Pascal-complement theorem](schur-pascal-complement.md) give, for every
integer \(n\ge1\),

\[
B_n(s)=s^{n^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(1+s^{-(2j-1)})^4,
\qquad
Z_{n,R}(s)=\prod_{a=0}^{n-1}
 (1+s^{-[8a-4(n-1)]/R}).
\]

The strict inequality \(B_n(s)<Z_{n,R}(s)^R\) certifies
\(\rho<R/4\) for this typed crossing construction.

The following witnesses are checked with outward integer arithmetic.
In the last two columns, \(s=r^a\) and \(R=a/b\).

| Modes | Certified upper bound on \(\rho\) | \(R=a/b\) | Rational root \(r\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 63 | \(17/24\) | \(17/6\) | \(177/176\) |
| 511 | \(99/140\) | \(99/35\) | \(5756/5755\) |
| 4095 | \(577/816\) | \(577/204\) | \(204681/204680\) |
| 16383 | \(3363/4756\) | \(3363/1189\) | \(4111597/4111596\) |

In particular,

\[
\rho<\frac{3363}{4756}=0.7071068124474348\ldots.
\]

This upper bound is still larger than
\(1/\sqrt2=0.7071067811865475\ldots\). It therefore does not improve the
factoring exponent. The previous analytic floor proves that no choice of
\(s\) in this displayed interval formula crosses that boundary.

The checker stores the exact rational mode probabilities compactly:

\[
\pi_i=
\frac{r^{-b(8i-4(n-1))}}{1+r^{-b(8i-4(n-1))}}.
\]

All these probabilities are rational. Their common denominators can be
used to realize the required fixed-mode types.

## 2. Integer arithmetic with a separate exponent

[check_large_interval_pressure.py](../tools/check_large_interval_pressure.py)
represents a positive real interval as

\[
[L,U]2^e,
\]

where \(L,U\) are integers of about 256 bits and \(e\) is an unrestricted
integer. Addition, multiplication, inversion, and integer powers round
the lower endpoint down and the upper endpoint up. Thus products with
millions of binary exponent bits do not require million-bit mantissas.
No floating-point comparison is used to certify a witness.

For odd \(n=2k+1\), cancelling the paired positive and negative mode
weights gives the exact ratio

\[
\frac{B_n(r^a)^b}{Z_{n,a/b}(r^a)^a}
=
\frac{r^{ab}\prod_{j=1}^n(1+r^{-a(2j-1)})^{4b}}
 {2^a\prod_{j=1}^k(1+r^{-8bj})^{2a}}.                 \tag{1}
\]

For even \(n\), the ratio is

\[
\frac{\prod_{j=1}^n(1+r^{-a(2j-1)})^{4b}}
 {\prod_{j=1}^{n/2}(1+r^{-4b(2j-1)})^{2a}}.           \tag{2}
\]

Successive factors are generated by interval multiplication by a fixed
ratio. This avoids separately raising the root to every large exponent.

For the 16383-mode witness, the exact interval lies below one; its value
is approximately \(0.96827970221325579\). The evidence contains both
integer endpoints and a strictly positive rational lower bound on the
gap from one. The checker also performs 158 independent `Fraction`
arithmetic controls and compares (1)--(2) with the original unfactored
pressure expression at six small sizes, including both parities.

## 3. Sharp asymptotic gap

Write \(t=\log s\), and let \(R_n(t)\) be the unique solution of

\[
D_n(t,R):=\log B_n(e^t)-R\log Z_{n,R}(e^t)=0.
\]

Uniqueness follows because the derivative of
\(R\log Z_{n,R}\) with respect to \(R\) is the sum of the positive
binary entropies of its Gibbs occupations. Define

\[
\rho_n=\frac14\inf_{t>0}R_n(t),\qquad R_0=2\sqrt2.
\]

Let \(W(x)\) denote the positive solution of \(W e^W=x\), and put
\(y_n=W(12n^2)\).

**Theorem.** For either parity of \(n\), as \(n\to\infty\),

\[
\boxed{
\rho_n=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 +\frac{\sqrt2}{8\pi^2 n^2}(y_n^2+2y_n)
 +o(n^{-2}).}
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

Every minimizing choice \(t_n\) satisfies

\[
t_n=\frac{y_n}{\sqrt2 n}+o(n^{-1}).                    \tag{4}
\]

A minimizer exists for all sufficiently large \(n\): the endpoint
exclusions below confine any near-minimizing sequence to a compact
subinterval of \((0,\infty)\), where \(R_n\) is continuous.

In particular,

\[
\rho_n-\frac1{\sqrt2}
\sim\frac{(\log n)^2}{\sqrt2\pi^2n^2}.                \tag{5}
\]

### Infinite products and finite tails

Set \(L(x)=\log(1+e^{-x})\). The paired form of the pressure is

\[
D_n(t,R)=
\begin{cases}
t+4\sum_{j=1}^{n}L((2j-1)t)
 -R\log2-2R\sum_{j=1}^{(n-1)/2}L(8jt/R),&n\text{ odd},\\
4\sum_{j=1}^{n}L((2j-1)t)
 -2R\sum_{j=1}^{n/2}L(4(2j-1)t/R),&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{6}
\]

The eta-product transformation used in
[the interval floor proof](schur-one-species-interval.md) gives, for
either infinite-product continuation of (6), uniformly for \(R\) in
a fixed neighborhood of \(R_0\),

\[
D_\infty(t,R)
=\frac{(8-R^2)\pi^2}{48t}+\frac t6+O(e^{-c/t})         \tag{7}
\]

as \(t\downarrow0\), for some fixed \(c>0\).
The exponentially small remainder differs between the two parities;
the two displayed terms agree.

When \(nt\to\infty\), the estimate \(L(x)=e^{-x}+O(e^{-2x})\)
and geometric summation of the omitted terms give

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_n(t,R)=D_\infty(t,R)
&+\frac{R^2}{4t}e^{-4nt/R}
       \bigl(1+O(t+e^{-4nt/R})\bigr)\\
&-\frac2t e^{-2nt}
       \bigl(1+O(t+e^{-2nt})\bigr).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{8}
\]

The offsets of the first omitted index only change the \(O(t)\)
factor. This is why both parities have the same asymptotic expansion.

### Locating the minimizing scale

Taking \(t=y_n/(\sqrt2n)\) in (7)--(8) first gives
\(\rho_n-1/\sqrt2=O((\log n)^2/n^2)\). The known strict floor
gives the opposite inequality \(\rho_n>1/\sqrt2\).

For a minimizing sequence, \(R_n(t_n)\to R_0\) forces \(t_n\to0\).
Indeed, on any compact interval bounded away from zero, (6) converges
uniformly to its infinite continuation, which is strictly positive at
\(R_0\). Large \(t\) cannot supply an exception: for even \(n\) and
\(R\le3\), the first positive term has order \(e^{-t}\), whereas all
negative terms together are \(O(e^{-4t/3})\); for odd \(n\), the
positive term \(t\) dominates the negative terms. These estimates are
uniform in \(n\).

Also \(nt_n\to\infty\). If \(nt_n\to c\in(0,\infty)\), the
Riemann-sum limit of \(t_nD_n(t_n,R_0)\) is

\[
2\int_{\sqrt2c}^{2c}L(x)\,dx>0.
\]

If \(nt_n\to0\), instead \(D_n(t_n,R)/n\to(4-R)\log2\).
Both limits exclude a root tending to \(R_0\). The same arguments
apply to bounded subsequences of \(nt_n\).

Multiply (8) by \(t\), put \(R=R_0+\delta\), and use the upper
bound \(\delta=O((\log n)^2/n^2)\). The negative tail is smaller
than the positive tail because \(2>\sqrt2\). Consequently

\[
t_n=O(\log n/n),\qquad
e^{-\sqrt2nt_n}=O((\log n)^2/n^2).                    \tag{9}
\]

In this range the errors in (8), after multiplication by \(t\), are
\(o(n^{-2})\). For example, the negative tail is at most
\(O(((\log n)^2/n^2)^{\sqrt2})\), and the relative \(O(t)\)
error in the positive tail is \(O((\log n)^3/n^3)\).
Changing \(R_0\) to \(R_0+\delta\) in its exponent gives a still
smaller error, since \(nt\delta=O((\log n)^3/n^2)\).

Expanding the quadratic term in (7) now yields, uniformly throughout
the possible minimizing range,

\[
\frac\delta4
=\frac{\sqrt2}{4\pi^2}t^2
 +\frac{3\sqrt2}{\pi^2}e^{-\sqrt2nt}
 +o(n^{-2}).                                         \tag{10}
\]

The displayed function of \(t\) is strictly convex. Its derivative
vanishes exactly when

\[
(\sqrt2nt)e^{\sqrt2nt}=12n^2.
\]

Substituting this minimizer into (10) proves (3). Its second derivative
is bounded below by a positive constant, so the uniform \(o(n^{-2})\)
error gives (4). Finally \(W(12n^2)\sim2\log n\), proving (5).

## 4. Reproduction and scope

Run:

~~~sh
python tools/check_large_interval_pressure.py \
  --output evidence/large-interval-pressure.json
~~~

The [evidence](../evidence/large-interval-pressure.json) proves the finite
strict inequalities using integers. The asymptotic statement is an
ordinary mathematical proof using the exact profiles and the analytic
identities already recorded in the interval note. No Lean formalization
was added.

Increasing the number of interval modes therefore gives a controlled
approach to the known boundary, with the gap quantified by (3). A
strictly stronger factoring result needs a construction or a gauge
outside this displayed pressure family.
