# A finite limiting problem for bounded rectangular defects

## Result and scope

The fixed active cores admit a useful second limit: retain the formal
variables \(z\) and \(Z=z^M\) separately. A matrix of fixed size over
\(K(z,Z)\) has eventually affine Smith orders after this substitution.
This is a theorem about rational matrices, proved below. It also applies
to row and column gauges, and reduces the limiting pressure to the
lowest-order coefficient matrix over \(K(z)\).

The rectangular concentration formulas explain why such a representation
is available for power-of-two intervals. A complete exported bivariate
matrix and its exact lowest-order ranks have not yet been computed.
The numerical profile fit in Section 5 is finite evidence, separate from
the general stabilization theorem. It indicates a positive natural-gauge
defect excess, not a stronger factoring bound.

## 1. Eventual affine orders

Let \(A(z,Z)\) be a fixed finite matrix over \(K(z,Z)\), of rank
\(r\). Choose a common denominator and write \(A=P/Q\), where the
entries of \(P\) and \(Q\) are Laurent polynomials. Let \(w_P\) be
the difference between the largest and smallest exponent of \(z\)
appearing anywhere in \(P\). Define \(w_Q\) similarly.

**Stabilization theorem.** For every integer

~~~math
M>\max(rw_P,w_Q),                                      \tag{1}
~~~

the nonzero minors of \(A(z,z^M)\) have the same ranks as those of
\(A\), and every Smith order is an affine integer function of \(M\).
Their affine coefficients can be computed over the ordered valuation
whose value on \(z^a Z^b\) is \((b,a)\), compared lexicographically.

**Proof.** For a nonzero Laurent polynomial
\(f=\sum c_{ab}z^aZ^b\), combine terms with identical exponent pairs.
Let \((b_0,a_0)\) be the least pair in lexicographic order. If
\(M>w_f\), every term with \(b>b_0\) has
\(bM+a>b_0M+a_0\). Among terms with \(b=b_0\), the least exponent
is \(a_0\), and its coefficient is nonzero. Therefore

~~~math
\mathrm{ord}_z f(z,z^M)=b_0M+a_0.                     \tag{2}
~~~

A nonzero \(k\)-minor of \(P\) has \(z\)-exponent width at most
\(kw_P\). Equation (1) applies simultaneously to all these minors
and to \(Q\). Its valuation after substitution is thus the affine
value obtained from its lexicographic valuation, minus
\(k\,\mathrm{ord}(Q)\). The minimum valuation of the nonzero
\(k\)-minors gives the \(k\)-th Smith prefix. Because every competing
minor obeys the same width bound, its minimum is also the least
lexicographic value. Differences of consecutive prefixes prove the
claim. The same argument prevents a nonzero minor or denominator from
specializing to zero. \(\square\)

The bound (1) is conservative, but it is explicit and does not require
enumerating all minors merely to establish eventual stabilization.
For rational affine monomial gauges, clear their denominators by a
fixed ramification and apply the same argument. The required bound
then depends on the selected gauge.

## 2. Why the rectangular cores have this form

The [seven/nine-mode concentration](rectangular-defect-factorization.md)
and [ten-mode extension](rectangular-full-core-factorization.md) use
creation and annihilation sequences generated by coordinate vectors,
\(1\), \(s^a\), and \(s^{-a}\). Here \(s\) is one of the fixed
rational functions \(z^2\) and \(z^2/(1+z)^2\). Their coefficients
also contain fixed powers of \(z\) and \(1+z\), with exponents
affine in \(M\).

For \(M=2^k\) in characteristic two,

~~~math
(1+z)^M=1+Z,\qquad
\sum_{j=a}^{M-b}s^j
=\frac{s^a-s^{M-b+1}}{1-s},\qquad Z=z^M.               \tag{3}
~~~

The same identity holds with any fixed rational \(s\in\mathbb F_2(z)\),
since \(s(z)^M=s(Z)\). The special case \(s=1\) is the number of
summands reduced modulo two, which is constant on this sequence for
large \(M\).

Each concentration sweep has fixed pivot coordinates, at fixed distance
from either endpoint. For a variable eliminated coordinate, its entries
are finite sums of geometric sequences with coefficients in
\(\mathbb F_2(z,Z)\). A sweep either changes those coefficients or
adds a finite geometric sum to a pivot. Thus (3), applied successively
to the fixed number of sweeps, expresses every retained operator image
in \(\mathbb F_2(z,Z)\). The constant repair coefficients can then be
adjoined in \(\mathbb F_4\).

The CAR vacuum projector is a product of a fixed number of these
operators. Its coordinate matrices have fixed dimension. Each possible
vacuum minor is a rational function of \((z,Z)\). By (2), its rank
and its residue modulo \(z\), when integral, eventually stabilize.
The existing unit-minor construction therefore eventually uses fixed
pivot sets. It produces a fixed rational core matrix over
\(\mathbb F_4(z,Z)\). The physical input, repair, and deletion maps
also have fixed size. Their composition stays in this field.

This establishes the structural applicability of Section 1. Computing
the actual rational entries, an explicit specialization threshold, and
their leading minors remains a concrete certificate task. The observed
affine fit alone does not perform that task.

## 3. Two gauge scales and the leading matrix

Set \(\ell_M=\log s_M\) and \(\tau_M=M\ell_M\). Choose

~~~math
\tau_M=c\log M,\qquad c>1/\sqrt2,\qquad
\theta_j(M)=A_j\tau_M+B_j,                             \tag{4}
~~~

where \(\theta_j\) is a raw logarithmic gauge, as in the
[critical-pressure analyzer](rectangular-critical-pressure.md).
The fixed \(A_j\) cancel macroscopic extreme-mode energies; the
fixed \(B_j\) tune the remaining finite probabilities.

For a nonzero matrix entry, write its eventual ungauged valuation as
\(a_{ij}M+b_{ij}\). Let \(A_i^L,A_j^R,B_i^L,B_j^R\) be the
induced row and column gauges. Its logarithmic order is

~~~math
(a_{ij}+A_i^L+A_j^R)\tau_M
 +(B_i^L+B_j^R)+b_{ij}\ell_M.                          \tag{5}
~~~

The smallest slope is

~~~math
\lambda=\min_{A_{ij}\ne0}(a_{ij}+A_i^L+A_j^R).         \tag{6}
~~~

After removing \(Z^\lambda\), retain the entries attaining (6)
and take their leading coefficient in \(Z\). The result is a
matrix \(L_A(z)\) over \(K(z)\). It is the lowest-order coefficient
matrix for this gauge face. Its rank \(d\) is the multiplicity of
the smallest Smith slope.

For \(k\le d\), define

~~~math
m_k=\min_{\det L_A[I,J]\ne0}
 \left(\sum_{i\in I}B_i^L+\sum_{j\in J}B_j^R\right),
\qquad m_0=0,\qquad \mu_k=m_k-m_{k-1}.                \tag{7}
~~~

The first \(d\) weighted Smith orders, multiplied by \(\ell_M\),
are \(\lambda\tau_M+\mu_k+o(1)\). All remaining orders have a
strictly larger slope. Consequently the complete active profile
partition has the limit

~~~math
\log W_A=-\lambda\tau_M+
 \log\sum_{k=1}^{d}e^{-\mu_k}+o(1).                   \tag{8}
~~~

To prove this, apply the minor characterization to (5). A minor with a
larger slope loses by a positive multiple of \(\tau_M\), which tends
to infinity. On the least slope, its finite \(z\)-order is multiplied
by \(\ell_M\to0\), leaving exactly (7). Nonzero leading minors are
precisely the nonzero minors of \(L_A\). This also shows that real
gauges cause no difficulty in the limiting optimization. A final strict
finite pressure witness can be approximated by rational gauges.

Only entry valuations are needed for (6). Only the rank and weighted
minor structure of \(L_A(z)\) are needed for (8). Higher Smith slopes
do not contribute to this limit. At \(B=0\), the finite term is just
\(\log d\).

## 4. The finite critical-pressure discriminator

Keep the aggregate Gaussian bulk charges zero at both places:
\(c_\infty=c_0=0\). Opposite row and column charge gauges can still
act on the defect if their sums vanish. Compare the defect with the
Gaussian control from the critical-pressure note. Their spectator
factors and interior physical partition factors cancel exactly.

Every remaining term is a logarithm of a finite sum. If its energies
are \(a_i\tau_M+b_i+o(1)\), write

~~~math
a_{\min}=\min_i a_i,\qquad
K=\sum_{i:a_i=a_{\min}}e^{-b_i}.
~~~

Its expansion is \(-a_{\min}\tau_M+\log K+o(1)\). Apply this rule
to each active profile and to the two physical extreme cores, both
for the defect and its Gaussian control. For a physical partition,
the energies in this rule are the actual
physical energies divided by \(R_*\). Thus the leading physical
contribution below is the actual minimum energy slope: the outside
factor \(R_*\) cancels this division.

With \(R_*=\sqrt2\), the defect excess becomes

~~~math
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_M&=C(A)\tau_M+D(A,B)+o(1),\\
C&=\sum_{e\in\{0,\infty\}}(a_e^G-a_e)
 +R_*\sum_{P\in\{X,Y\}}(a_P-a_P^G),\\
D&=\sum_{e\in\{0,\infty\}}\log(K_e/K_e^G)
 -R_*\sum_{P\in\{X,Y\}}\log(K_P/K_P^G).              \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
~~~

Here the active-profile \(a_e,K_e\) are supplied by (6)--(8).
The physical cores have only four and three letters, so their terms
are immediate from their energy lists. The leading problem is a finite
piecewise-linear optimization of \(C\), followed on a selected face
by a finite log-sum-exp optimization of \(D\). It is not established
here that \(C\) is always nonnegative.

The [interval asymptotic](quantitative-interval-frontier.md) gives
at the critical point

~~~math
F_G=\frac{\ell_M}{6}
 +O\!\left(\frac{e^{-\sqrt2\tau_M}}{\ell_M}
           +\frac{e^{-2\tau_M}}{\ell_M}
           +e^{-c'/\ell_M}\right)=o(1)                \tag{10}
~~~

under (4). The rate condition in (4) is necessary for this stated
argument: \(\tau_M\to\infty\) by itself does not make the finite
tails vanish. Therefore either \(C<0\), or \(C=0\) and \(D<0\),
would imply negative critical pressure for all sufficiently large
power-of-two \(M\). A particular sufficiently large \(M\) would
then give a fixed finite crossing seed; the existing transfer theorem
would apply after a strict exact pressure certificate. No such negative
point is supplied by this note.

## 5. The natural-gauge profile fit

The [frozen inputs](../evidence/bivariate-core-pressure-inputs.json)
contain the previously computed natural profiles at \(M=8,16,32,64\).
The [analyzer](../tools/analyze_bivariate_core_pressure.py) fits affine
orders using 16 and 32, then checks the sorted multisets at 8 and 64.
All 480 orders at each size agree exactly with the same affine list.
This verifies a finite fit, not its eventual validity.

| Place | Slope | Multiplicity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Zero | 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 | 26, 98, 140, 92, 26, 2 |
| Infinity | -4, -2, 0, 2, 4 | 8, 28, 36, 20, 4 |

The smallest infinity slope is \(-4\), with offsets
\(3,4,4,5,6,7,7,8\). Its multiplicity is eight. The seven active
Gaussian infinity modes have the same smallest slope and multiplicity.
At zero, the smallest slope is zero, with multiplicity 26. The nine
active Gaussian zero modes have smallest-slope multiplicity 16.

At natural gauges, both physical extreme cores concentrate on the low
state, in both constructions. Their leading terms agree. If the fitted
affine profiles continue, equation (9) therefore gives

~~~math
C=0,\qquad D=\log(26/16)=\log(13/8)>0.                \tag{11}
~~~

This directs the next search toward changed leading faces and finite
offset gauges. Increasing the interval length with the natural gauges
does not remove the positive candidate limit in (11).

Run the finite controls and the profile fit with:

```sh
python tools/analyze_bivariate_core_pressure.py \
  --output evidence/bivariate-core-pressure-limit.json
```

The tool also checks (2) on Laurent polynomials with cancellations and
checks the minor formulation on small rational matrices. These are
finite controls for the implementation; the all-size statement is the
proof in Section 1.
