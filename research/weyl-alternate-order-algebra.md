# The constant alternate-order Weyl algebra

## Result and scope

For the binary weighted Weyl crossing at `h=2^R>=2`, write
`A=P(0)` as a row matrix. Let `B` be its crossing-preserving
`y,u` relabeling, also read as a row matrix, and put `N=A+I`.
The following identities hold over every characteristic-two field:

```math
A^2=I,\qquad N^2=B^2=0.
\tag{1}
```

The full algebra generated by `N,B`, with arbitrary word lengths
and arbitrary linear recombinations, has an exact central normal
form. Its crossing decompositions still have asymptotic parameter
at least `rho=3/4`. This closes the constant-generator route,
including the affine coset `A+J`, where `J` is the two-sided ideal
of words containing `B`.

The larger algebra generated by **all** coefficients of `P(z)` and
its opposite orientation is not covered. The two-parameter product
of those curves is an effective, different family; its first raw
diagonal specializations do not improve the rank sum. No improved
factoring exponent is claimed here.

## 1. An elementary all-block involution

Use external order `(alpha,y,u,beta)` and row order
`(alpha,y)|(beta,u)`. At `z=0`, the curve becomes

```math
A(\alpha,y,u,\beta)
=[\alpha+u=\beta+y]\prod_j
  \binom{\alpha_j+u_j}{\alpha_j}.
\tag{2}
```

Fix a row difference `delta=alpha-y=beta-u`. An intermediate
row pair is `(gamma,v)` with `v=gamma-delta`. Its contribution
to `A^2` is

```math
\prod_j\binom{y_j+\gamma_j}{\alpha_j}
             \binom{\gamma_j+u_j}{\gamma_j}.
```

We can sum over all nonnegative `gamma` of weighted energy `h`:
if some `v_j` is negative, the first binomial coefficient already
vanishes. Write `w_j=2^j`. The required entry is therefore

```math
[t^h x^\alpha]
 \prod_{j=0}^R
 \frac{(1+x_j)^{y_j}}
      {(1-t^{w_j}(1+x_j))^{u_j+1}}.
\tag{3}
```

In characteristic two, factor each denominator using
`1+t^w=(1+t)^w`. Since
`sum_j w_j(u_j+1)=3h-1`, expansion of a multiindex `k`, of
weighted energy `e=sum_j w_j k_j<=h`, gives the scalar

```math
[t^{h-e}](1+t)^{-3h+1-e}
=\binom{4h-2}{h-e}\pmod2.
\tag{4}
```

Every nonzero term has even `e`. Indeed, `alpha_0,y_0` are even,
because their weighted energies are the even integer `h` and all
other weights are even. The numerator coefficient
`binom(y_0,alpha_0-k_0)` vanishes unless `alpha_0-k_0` is even,
so `k_0`, and hence `e`, is even.

The integer `4h-2` has all binary digits below `4h` equal to one
except the lowest digit. Thus the binomial in (4) equals one on
every remaining term. Removing this scalar leaves

```math
[x^\alpha]\prod_j(1+x_j)^{y_j-u_j-1}
=\prod_j\binom{\alpha_j-\beta_j-1}{\alpha_j}
=\prod_j\binom{\beta_j}{\alpha_j}\pmod2.
\tag{5}
```

The last equality is the integer identity
`binom(alpha-beta-1,alpha)=(-1)^alpha binom(beta,alpha)`.
Its product vanishes unless `alpha<=beta` coordinatewise.
Equal positive weighted energies then force `alpha=beta`, and
the fixed difference forces `y=u`. This proves `A^2=I` in every
row block. Consequently `N^2=0`.

## 2. The opposite orientation is a square-zero Hopf operator

Let `F_h` be the homogeneous degree-`h` part of the polynomial
algebra with variables of weights `1,2,4,...,h`. Let `mu_h` multiply
two degree-`h` polynomials, and let `Delta_h` be the degree
`(h,h)` component of the standard additive coproduct. Then

```math
B=\Delta_h\mu_h.
\tag{6}
```

For a monomial of weighted degree `2h`, the coefficient of
`mu_h Delta_h` is

```math
[t^h]\prod_j(1+t^{w_j})^{\sigma_j}
=[t^h](1+t)^{2h}=\binom{2h}{h}=0\pmod2.
```

Thus `B^2=0`. Its rank is

```math
k=b(2h)-R-2.
\tag{7}
```

This is the same nonzero-sum-block count as in the
[row-filtration theorem](weighted-weyl-row-filtration.md).
There are `b(2h)-1` sums using modes through `h`; exactly `R+1`
single-bit sums have zero coproduct. All other dyadic bit sums
split into two degree-`h` groups without carries.

Restrict the intermediate sum basis to these `k` nonzero
channels. Write `B=UV`, where `U=Delta_h` and `V=mu_h` on the
retained channels. Then `U` has full column rank, `V` has full row
rank, and `VU=0`.

## 3. A central normal form and a smaller exact core

Define

```math
C=NB+BN.
```

The square-zero relations show directly that `C` commutes with
both `N` and `B`. Every word reduces to

```math
p(C)I+q(C)N+r(C)B+s(C)NB.
\tag{8}
```

For example, `BN=C+NB`, `NBN=CN`, and `BNB=CB` reduce all
alternating words. No commutation assumption between `N,B` is
being made.

Let `J` be the two-sided ideal generated by `B`. Every such word
has image in

```math
W=\operatorname{im}U+\operatorname{im}(NU),
\qquad \dim W\le2k.
\tag{9}
```

The space is invariant: `N^2U=0`, `BU=0`, and
`BNU=U(VNU)`. Hence every algebra element has the form
`T=aI+bN+K`, `K in J`, with `rank_row K<=2k`.

There is also a concrete small spectral calculation. Put

```math
D=VNU=VAU,\quad
X=[NU,U],\quad Y=\begin{bmatrix}V\\VN\end{bmatrix}.
```

Then

```math
C=XY,\qquad
YX=\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\0&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C^m=X\operatorname{diag}(D^{m-1},D^{m-1})Y
\quad(m\ge1).
\tag{10}
```

Thus `x f(x)` annihilates `C` whenever `f` annihilates the
`k` by `k` matrix `D`. Nonzero spectral work reduces to that
core. This is a dimension reduction, not a promise of a small
minimal-polynomial degree: the exact degrees for `D` at energies
8, 16, and 32 are respectively 4, 18, and 352. The last case has
32 parity blocks, each of dimension at most 196, and is computed
in approximately 1.5 seconds by the supplied exploration script.
The resulting valid upper bound on the word-algebra dimension is
1412 at energy 32.

## 4. A fixed column minor on the affine coset

Every vector in `W` is zero on row coordinates `(alpha,alpha)`.
For `U`, this follows from the central binomial coefficient at
positive degree. The matrix `A` preserves integer differences, so
`AU` is also zero on the entire difference-zero row block. Hence
the same holds for `NU` and for every matrix in `J`.

Let `alpha_*` be the label consisting of the single part of
weight `h`. In the column flattening, take rows `(alpha_*,u)`
and columns `(beta,alpha_*)`. For
`T=aI+bN+K`, this submatrix is diagonal, with entries

```math
\begin{cases}
a+b,&\beta=\alpha_*,\\
b,&\beta\ne\alpha_*.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
```

Indeed, the `J` part is zero there; the difference-zero block
of `A` is the Lucas disjointness matrix, and `alpha_*` is
disjoint from every label except itself.

If `b!=0`, equation (11) gives
`rank_col T>=d-1`. In particular, it proves this bound for every
tensor in the full affine coset `A+J`, with no restriction on word
length or field coefficients. It also shows that the coefficients
`a,b` in the quotient by `J` are intrinsic: a linear combination
`aI+bN` in `J` must have `a=b=0`.

## 5. The quotient rank budget handles the remaining coset

We reuse the quotient budget from
[border-and-nonunit-seed-transfer.md](border-and-nonunit-seed-transfer.md).
The short operator proof is included to specify its use here.
The following statement is valid over any field.
Suppose a tensor `T` has column rank `r`, while
`rank_row(T-aI)<=m` for a nonzero scalar `a`. Then, for every
integer `1<=g<=d`,

```math
m\ge g(d-rg).
\tag{12}
```

To prove it, a column factorization writes the row operator on
`d` by `d` matrices as

```math
T(X)=\sum_{\ell=1}^r L_\ell X^{\mathsf T}R_\ell.
```

Restrict `X` to matrices supported on any fixed `g` columns.
The column space of every `T(X)` then lies in the sum of the
corresponding `g` columns of the `L_ell`, a space of dimension
at most `rg`. Quotienting the output column space by that sum
kills `T(X)`, but leaves an image of `aX` of dimension at least
`g(d-rg)`. This proves (12).

If `r<=d/2`, choose `g=floor(d/(2r))`; then
`g>=d/(4r)` and `d-rg>=d/2`, giving

```math
r\ge\frac{d^2}{8m}.
\tag{13}
```

For `T=aI+K`, `K in J`, use `m<=2k`. Together with the
alternative `r>d/2`, this is a column lower bound of order
`d^2/k`.

## 6. Full-algebra crossing decompositions

The scalar `a` is a linear functional on the algebra modulo `J`
and takes value one on `I`. In every decomposition of the
crossing `I`, some summand has `a!=0`.

For that summand, `aI+bN` is invertible, with inverse
`a^(-1)I+b a^(-2)N`. Therefore

```math
\operatorname{rank}_{\rm row}T\ge d^2-2k.
\tag{14}
```

If `b!=0`, use (11). If `b=0`, use (12)--(13). Since
`k>=d` for `R>=2`, the following uniform, deliberately coarse
bound covers both cases:

```math
\operatorname{rank}_{\rm col}T\ge\frac{d^2}{16k}.
\tag{15}
```

The elementary recurrence
`b(2h)=sum_(j=0)^h b(j)<=(h+1)b(h)`, together with
`log d=Omega(R^2)` and `log h=O(R)`, gives
`k=d^(1+o(1))`. Equations
(14)--(15) force the same summand to have ranks at least
`d^(2-o(1))` and `d^(1-o(1))`. Its rank-sum contribution is
`d^(3/2-o(1))`, proving the full-algebra obstruction
`rho>=3/4`.

The finite bound in (15) is much weaker than (11). At energy
32, the affine coset `A+J` already has row rank at least
`37162` and column rank at least `201`, so no element of that
coset alone passes the finite half threshold. The other detected
coset is closed asymptotically by (12), without a corresponding
sharp finite minimum-rank claim.

## 7. The parameterized product remains a different family

Let `Q(w)` be the opposite-orientation image of `P(w)`. As row
matrices, `P(z)Q(w)` has the crossing endpoint at `(1,1)`.
For external labels, its intermediate pair is uniquely determined:

```math
\gamma=(\alpha-y+\beta+u)/2,\qquad
v=(-\alpha+y+\beta+u)/2.
\tag{16}
```

If these are not nonnegative integer occupations, the entry is
zero. Otherwise their energies are automatically `h`, and the
entry is the product of the two original binomial kernels. This
gives an explicit parity/carry family with no intermediate sum.
It is outside the linear coefficient/symmetry span studied earlier.

The diagonal specialization `P(z)Q(z)` gives the following exact
raw profiles, summarized by their rank sums:

| Energy | Labels | Raw rank sum, decimal | Half threshold, decimal |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 10 | 285.4323 | 25.9546 |
| 16 | 36 | 6278.8022 | 158.8342 |

The central coefficient ranks are `(96,92)` and `(1276,1274)`.
These failures concern those two raw decompositions. The
full coefficient algebra involving nonconstant parameters is not
excluded by the constant-generator theorem above.

## Verification and finite exploration

```sh
python3 tools/check_weyl_alternate_algebra.py --output evidence/weyl-alternate-order-algebra.json
```

This standard-library replay constructs the tensors independently
at energies 2, 4, and 8. It checks the involution and both
square-zero identities, the exact coproduct/core factorizations,
all 254 words of lengths one through seven in each shell, and
768 affine-coset and quotient-budget cases. The exact generated
word-algebra dimensions are 3, 12, and 18. The default replay takes
approximately 0.2 seconds and needs no coefficient dumps.

The separate `explore_weyl_central_core.py` reads the exact block
dumps produced by `explore_weighted_binomial_crossing.cpp`. Its
energy-32 result records the full degree-352 minimal polynomial
and the minimal polynomials of all parity blocks. These are finite
spectral data, not an asymptotic degree bound.

To reproduce the optional core calculation, keep the binary dumps outside
the repository:

```sh
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 tools/explore_weighted_binomial_crossing.cpp -o /tmp/weighted-weyl
/tmp/weighted-weyl 32 --dump-prefix /tmp/weyl-32
python3 tools/explore_weyl_central_core.py /tmp/weyl-32
```

Replace 32 by 8 or 16 for the smaller committed core profiles.
The parameterized-product profiles have a separate native reproducer:

```sh
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 tools/explore_weyl_composition.cpp -o /tmp/weyl-composition
/tmp/weyl-composition 8 1
/tmp/weyl-composition 16 1
```

The second argument is the positive dilation in `P(z)Q(z^dilation)`.
The committed finite files are `evidence/weyl-central-core-h8.json`,
`evidence/weyl-central-core-h16.json`,
`evidence/weyl-central-core-h32.json`, and the corresponding
`evidence/weyl-composition-h8.json` and `h16` profile.

A necessary projection control also fails to give a smaller rank
sum. At energy 8, the central idempotent `C^4` has ranks `(24,73)`;
its complement has ranks `(76,87)`. Multiplying the complement by
`A` gives ranks `(76,82)`, compared with `(100,31)` for `A` itself.
Thus isolating a small row spectral sector does not by itself
preserve the useful column rank.
