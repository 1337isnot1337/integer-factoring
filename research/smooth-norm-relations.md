# Smooth represented norms suffice for relation rows

The arithmetic relation construction does not need the two small forms
returned by the [bounded smooth-pair interface](bounded-smooth-norm.md).
A supplied smooth represented norm already gives the required short word
in the factor base. Its prime factorization and the transformed middle
coefficient determine that word in polynomial bit time. Exact balanced
divisor search can therefore be removed from the relation stage entirely.

This is a weaker arithmetic search requirement, with an explicit example
where the new requirement succeeds and the old pair requirement fails.
It does not implement fast smooth-norm search or prove a stronger factoring
bound. The complete score remains `1/2`.

## 1. The supplied-norm word theorem

Let `Q=(A,B,C)` be a primitive positive definite binary quadratic form of
discriminant `Delta < 0`. Suppose a primitive integer vector `(x,y)` gives

```math
n=Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2>0,\qquad \gcd(n,\Delta)=1.
```

If `n` is `Y`-smooth, then `[Q]` has an explicitly constructible word in the
canonical prime forms at primes at most `Y`, with word length at most
`floor(log_2 n)`. The complete bit cost, including smoothness recognition
by trial division, is `Y*poly(b)` when the arithmetic inputs have `O(b)`
bits. A supplied prime factorization removes the factor `Y` from this
conversion cost.

Choose `u,v` with `xv-yu=1`. The properly equivalent form obtained by this
change of variables has first coefficient `n` and middle coefficient

```math
B'=2Axu+B(xv+yu)+2Cyv,
\qquad B'^2\equiv\Delta\pmod{4n}.
```

For each prime `p` dividing `n`, let `r_p` be the residue of `B'` in
`[0,2p)`. Put

```math
c_p=\min(r_p,2p-r_p),\qquad
\epsilon_p=
\begin{cases}
1,&r_p=c_p,\\
-1,&r_p\ne c_p.
\end{cases}
```

The canonical prime form is the reduced representative of

```math
P_p=\left(p,c_p,\frac{c_p^2-\Delta}{4p}\right).
```

The supplied congruence proves integrality. The condition
`gcd(n,Delta)=1` excludes ramification and conductor primes. At an odd
prime the two roots give conjugate prime ideals; the same convention also
handles `p=2` when it occurs, since then `Delta == 1 mod 8`. Prime-ideal
factorization of the proper norm-`n` ideal gives

```math
[Q]=\prod_{p\mid n}[P_p]^{\epsilon_p v_p(n)}.
```

This is the same standard ideal factorization used in
[the previous conversion proof](bounded-smooth-norm.md), attributed there
to Lenstra--Pomerance Section 2.8. The useful change is to retain the word
itself instead of requiring a balanced split into two reduced forms.

Since every prime is at least two,

```math
\sum_{p\mid n}|\epsilon_p v_p(n)|
=\sum_{p\mid n}v_p(n)
\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor.
```

The roots come from `B'`; no modular square-root search is needed in this
converter. Extended gcd, reduction of the prime forms, and the signed
exponents have polynomial bit cost. The implementation can first divide
the vector by `gcd(x,y)` and apply the theorem to the resulting primitive
norm. No primality claim is inferred from a composite item in the
factorization: the trial-division routine returns the actual prime list.

## 2. The relation construction with a norm oracle

Let `H` be the subgroup generated by the chosen canonical prime forms
`g_1,...,g_m`. Let `mu` be an explicitly supplied distribution on `H`, each
of whose support elements has a known integer exponent word of l1 norm at
most `W`. Let `X >= 1` be an allowed represented-norm bound and put

```math
L=\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor,\qquad K=W+L+1.
```

For each generator `g_i`, an arithmetic extractor is asked to find:

- a sample `u` from the support of `mu`;
- a reduced form `Q` in the class `g_i^K u`;
- a primitive vector with `Q(x,y)=n <= X`, where `n` is `Y`-smooth and
  coprime to `Delta`.

There is no balanced-divisor condition and no requirement to output two
forms. Let `w_u` be the sample word and let `v_n` be the word from Section
1. Then

```math
r_i=K e_i+w_u-v_n
```

is a relation in the factor base, and

```math
\|r_i-Ke_i\|_1\le W+L<K.
```

Consequently its diagonal coefficient is positive and exceeds the sum of
the absolute values of the other row coefficients. The matrix of these
rows is strictly diagonally dominant and nonsingular by the maximum-entry
argument in the [positive relation kernel](positive-relation-kernel.md).
Its absolute determinant is a multiple of `|H|` and annihilates `H`.
The [density/index transfer](arithmetic-density-lift.md) can still correct
that determinant to a multiple of the full class number.

The distinction between `W` and `bits(W)` is unchanged. The sample words
may have large l1 norms but succinct binary exponents. Powers such as
`g_i^K` are computed by binary powering, and relation coefficients are
written in binary. This change creates no loop of length `K`.

### Why the existing Fourier premise still gives existence

Take `S_R` to be the small smooth reduced forms already used by the
positive kernel, with `4R^2 < |Delta|`, and choose `X=R^2`. If the bias
condition holds, the Fourier lemma supplies a sample `u` with

```math
g_i^K u=a b^{-1},\qquad a,b\in S_R.
```

The [bounded-norm equivalence](bounded-smooth-norm.md) then supplies a
primitive smooth represented norm at most `R^2`. It happens to have a
balanced divisor, but the new extractor need not certify or preserve that
extra property. It may return any valid smooth represented norm at most
`X`. Section 1 turns every such output into a sufficiently short word.

Thus the existence argument is inherited at exactly the same radius and
with exactly the same density premise. No shrinking of `S_R`, unproved
inner-radius density estimate, or `sqrt(Y)` radius loss is introduced.
The polynomial [radius-gap pair converter](smooth-norm-gap.md) remains
useful when an application actually needs two explicit small forms; the
relation matrix does not need that output format.

The bound `X` can also be enlarged while replacing `L` by `floor(log_2 X)`.
For example a bound with polynomially many bits still yields polynomial
word-processing cost. This is permission for a weaker future extractor,
not an assertion that enlarged search regions can already be searched
efficiently.

## 3. The new oracle is strictly weaker

Consider

```math
Q=(24,1,50),\qquad \Delta=-4799,\qquad R=5,\qquad Y=3.
```

It primitively represents `24=2^3*3` at `(1,0)`, so the new oracle accepts
that witness. Its prime-form word is

```math
[Q]=[(2,1,600)]^3\,[(3,1,400)].
```

But `Q` is not in `S_5/S_5`. Indeed,

```math
Q(x,y)=24\left(x+\frac y{48}\right)^2
       +\frac{4799}{96}y^2.
```

For `y != 0`, this is greater than `25`. When `y=0`, primitivity forces
`x=+1` or `x=-1`, giving `24`. There is no divisor `d` of `24` for which
both `d` and `24/d` are at most `5`. The bounded-norm equivalence therefore
excludes an `S_5/S_5` representation.

The finite class group in this example has `63` classes. The `3`-smooth
factor base generates all of it. The quotient set `S_5/S_5` contains `21`
classes, while smooth represented norms at most `25` yield short words
for `29` classes. The extra eight classes are an independently computed
finite control; the displayed form already proves strictness without that
enumeration.

## 4. Cost and the remaining open step

Under the existing arithmetic assumptions, constructing prime forms up to
`Y`, generating the low-bias word list, and converting a supplied norm
have the stated deterministic costs. If `Y=exp(O(sqrt(b log b)))`, the
conversion stays on that arithmetic scale. It no longer adds the exact
balanced-divisor term `2^O(b/log b)`.

The remaining arithmetic search requirement is now precise: for each
target and its known finite biased sample family, find a primitive,
coprime, smooth value of one of the resulting quadratic forms within the
chosen norm bound. The Fourier argument proves that a suitable witness
exists for the applicable radius and density premises; it does not find
the witness. Exhaustive coordinate enumeration still has exponential
worst-case cost in the discriminant's bit length.

The separate missing-direction issue also remains. For `Delta=-348` and
`Y=7`, the small-prime subgroup has order three inside a class group of
order six. The corrected class-number multiple is valid, but two-torsion
extraction from those generators cannot recover the missing ambiguous
form. Removing balancing does not supply full generators or solve that
coverage problem.

## 5. Implementation and evidence

The converter and row builder are
[smooth_norm_relation.py](../tools/smooth_norm_relation.py). Run:

```sh
python3 tools/check_smooth_norm_relation.py
```

The [record](../evidence/smooth-norm-relations.json) checks eleven
discriminants, including the strict example above. It contains:

- `2,942` exact represented-norm word identities, with independent
  class-group multiplication and scaled-coordinate controls;
- `368` accepted norms with no balanced split at the requested radius;
- `22` strictly diagonally dominant relation rows, `19` of which use
  unbalanced norm witnesses;
- relation determinants divisible by the independently enumerated
  subgroup orders, and density-corrected multiples divisible by the full
  class numbers;
- exact recovery of each factor-base subgroup's two-torsion, while
  retaining the `-348` missing-global-direction counterexample.

The checker enumerates small class groups, coordinate regions, and sample
words. Those are finite control procedures, not the missing fast extractor
or a claimed asymptotic factoring algorithm. The ordinary proofs above
give the conversion and relation guarantees. The work starts from
repository commit `a5e05feb756b4ad35ae1877d54a5f2e4eb387c19`, uses substantial
OpenAI assistant support, and adds no Lean formalization.
