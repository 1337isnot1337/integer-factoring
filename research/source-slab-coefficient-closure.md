# Frobenius closure of polynomial slab directions

## Result and exact finite family

Polynomial reparametrization and rational-span saturation recover every
constant coefficient tensor of a finite source slab. This gives a
canonical family broader than removing scalar factors from individual
slabs or taking their available fourth roots.

For the certified weight-16 source, the coefficient space has dimension
**22**. Free insertion at the first physical label gives 110 constant
tensor directions. After a factor `1+w`, these are degree-one
endpoint-zero perturbations. Their polynomial multiples of degree at
most 18 form a space of dimension **1,980**. Closing the constant
tensors under the eight permutations that preserve the crossing pairs
gives dimension **367**, hence 6,606 endpoint-zero directions through
degree 18.

These dimensions are exact over every characteristic-two field. They
describe a candidate space; they do not assert a useful pressure or an
improved factoring exponent.

## 1. Coefficient recovery from Frobenius reparametrizations

Let `K` be a characteristic-two field and write a polynomial vector as

\[
 G(w)=\sum_{j=1}^{s} C_j w^{k_j},
 \qquad C_j\in K^n,
\]

where the nonnegative exponents `k_j` are distinct. Then

\[
 \operatorname{span}_{K(w)}
   \{G(w),G(w^2),\ldots,G(w^{2^{s-1}})\}
 =\operatorname{span}_{K(w)}\{C_1,\ldots,C_s\}.        \tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** Set `t_j=w^(k_j)`. The coefficient matrix on the left is
the square Moore matrix `(t_j^(2^e))`, with `0<=e<s`. Suppose its rows
were linearly dependent over `K(w)`. There would be coefficients `a_e`,
not all zero, such that the nonzero additive polynomial

\[
 L(T)=\sum_{e=0}^{s-1}a_e T^{2^e}
\]

vanished at every `t_j`. Additivity forces it to vanish at all
`F_2`-linear combinations of these monomials. Distinct monomials are
`F_2`-linearly independent, so these are `2^s` distinct roots in
`K(w)`. But `L` has degree at most `2^(s-1)`, a contradiction.
The matrix is invertible, proving (1).

For finitely many generators, let `W` be the constant vector space
spanned by all their coefficients. Applying (1) to each generator
shows that finitely many Frobenius reparametrizations have rational
span `W tensor_K K(w)`. Consequently their full polynomial saturation
is exactly

\[
 W\otimes_K K[w].                                      \tag{2}
\]

To verify the intersection in (2), choose constant linear functionals
whose common kernel is `W`. A polynomial vector lies in the rational
span only if all these functionals annihilate it; equality of
polynomial coefficients then puts each coefficient in `W`.

If the initial generators have binary coefficients, reparametrization
is also literal entrywise Frobenius powering. Equation (1) itself
only requires the indicated substitution of the polynomial variable.

## 2. Restoring the endpoint exactly

The vectors in the polynomial saturation need not vanish at one.
The complete endpoint-zero submodule of (2) is

\[
 (1+w)\bigl(W\otimes_K K[w]\bigr).                     \tag{3}
\]

Indeed every coordinate of an endpoint-zero polynomial is divisible
by `1+w`; division preserves all constant annihilating functionals
of `W`. Conversely, each vector in (3) has zero endpoint.

Each slab has 125 coordinates, indexed by `y+5u+25v`. Placing it at
one of five first-label values gives a tensor with dense index
`x+5y+25u+125v`. Different first-label values have disjoint supports.
Thus the 22-dimensional coefficient space gives 110 independent
constant tensors. If `C_1,...,C_r` are independent constant tensors,
then

\[
 \{w^j(1+w)C_i:1\le i\le r,\ 0\le j<D\}
\]

is independent: divide a relation by `1+w` and compare powers of `w`.
Its dimension is `rD`, yielding 1,980 at `D=18`.

The raw bounded-source slabs and their previously saturated module
have the same constant coefficient span. Polynomial combinations
preserve that span. Rational saturation does too, by the constant
annihilator argument used for (2). The exact checker verifies this
equality directly for the retained records.

## 3. Permutations of the crossing pairs

The literal crossing is `[x=v][y=u]`. The permutations generated by
swapping `x` with `v`, swapping `y` with `u`, and exchanging the two
pairs form a group of order eight. Each preserves the crossing.
Permuting any endpoint-zero tensor also keeps its endpoint zero.

Apply this group to the 110 independent constant tensors and eliminate
the resulting 880 binary vectors of length 625. The exact rank is
367. The exported record retains a literal independent basis, each
with its original coefficient vector, first-label placement, and
permutation. Formula (3) then gives dimension `367D`, or 6,606 at
degree 18.

This is a specified finite permutation group. It is not closure under
arbitrary changes of coordinates on all four physical spaces.

## 4. Two rank-one coefficient tensors

The coefficient basis retains particularly small directions. Number
the original saturated module generators from zero. The coefficient
of `w^8` in generator 5 is

\[
 e_0(y)\otimes(e_2+e_3)(u)
   \otimes(e_1+e_2+e_3+e_4)(v).                         \tag{4}
\]

The coefficient of `w^8` in generator 9 is

\[
 (e_2+e_4)(y)\otimes(e_2+e_4)(u)
   \otimes(e_2+e_4)(v).                                \tag{5}
\]

Both have eight nonzero scalar entries. Inserting a fixed first-label
vector makes each a pure four-tensor, so its flattening has rank one
in both cuts. Multiplication by `1+w` gives exact endpoint-zero
rank-one tensor perturbations. This alone says nothing about the
profiles of their sum with a reference tensor, which still require
complete evaluation.
