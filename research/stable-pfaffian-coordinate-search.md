# Complete coordinate changes in the stable Pfaffian evaluator

The stable module now supports complete univariate pencils and complete
bivariate coordinate charts. The implementation retains every coefficient
and computes both flattenings. It also uses ancillary entries that are
monomials, in addition to constant entries.

## Polynomial block and ancillary shift

For a stable block \(\Delta^dB_m(g)\otimes B_n(h)\), with arbitrary row and
column weight slices, the exact entry is

\[
 (p+r)^d
 r^{m-u_r-u_c+w}
 p^{u_c-w}
 q^{v_r-z}
 (p+q+r)^z.
 \tag{1}
\]

All five factors are included before extracting a coefficient or taking a
rank. In particular, a nonmonomial determinant \(p+r\) cannot be treated
as a coefficient shift.

If a fixed ancillary entry is \(t^e\), the original coefficient of degree
\(k\) is a literal submatrix of the augmented coefficient of degree
\(k+e\). For a bivariate monomial \(x^e y^f\), use the shift
\((a,b)\mapsto(a+e,b+f)\).
The corresponding ancillary row and column grades determine the
rectangular slices through the formulas in
[the stable-module theorem](stable-pfaffian-modules.md).
The minimum over all available monomial entries remains a valid bound.

The second cut exchanges \(p,q\) and complements state modes. Full
fixed-grade domains are closed under that complementation. Both
evaluations are performed independently, so no coefficient symmetry is
assumed for a general coordinate change.

## Endpoint-preserving families

For a univariate pencil, encode a polynomial by its binary coefficient
mask. The three nonzero even-parity masks in the span of \(1,t,t^2\) are
\(3,5,6\). Choose distinct \(p,q\) from these masks and choose
\(r\in\{1,2,4,7\}\). There are twenty-four ordered triples. Every one has

\[
 p(1)=q(1)=0,\qquad r(1)=1.
\]

Hence summing all of its coefficient tensors gives the crossing tensor.
Exchanging \(p,q\) only exchanges the two cut costs, so twelve unordered
choices suffice for the recorded cost grid.

For bivariate charts, interpret the same masks in the basis \(1,x,y\).
The same twenty-four triples are linearly independent coordinate systems
with crossing at \(x=y=1\). Every coefficient of total degree at most
\(L\) is included.

The standard chart is
\[
 p=1+x,\quad q=1+y,\quad r=1.
\]
The alternate chart through the common singular point is
\[
 p=1+x,\quad q=1+y,\quad r=1+x+y.
\]
In the latter, \(p+q+r=1\); its complete cost is still evaluated rather
than inferred from that simplification.

## Bounded results

The checker compares actual signed-charge coefficient ranks with the
stable bounds for every one of the twelve unordered parameter choices at
\((P,L)=(2,2)\) and \((3,3)\), in both the univariate and bivariate
interpretations.

The recorded grids then evaluate all choices at
\((P,L)=(4,2),(8,4),(16,8),(32,15)\).
The original source pencil, up to exchanging cuts, remains the best
univariate choice in these grids. The standard centered chart and its
equivalent coordinate permutations remain the best bivariate choices.

| \(P\) | \(L\) | Best pencil upper \(\rho\) | Best chart upper \(\rho\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 0.9102244582 | 0.9196689746 |
| 8 | 4 | 0.8646704563 | 0.8654156586 |
| 16 | 8 | 0.8258428768 | 0.8260138157 |
| 32 | 15 | 0.8001570925 | 0.8001000625 |

None meets the sufficient finite-seed threshold. This bounded result does
not exclude other fields, nonlinear pencils, different coefficient
decompositions, or restricted codes.

## Reproduce

~~~bash
python3 tools/check_stable_pfaffian_coordinates.py \
  --output evidence/stable-pfaffian-coordinates.json
~~~

The general evaluators are
[stable_pfaffian_pencils.py](../tools/stable_pfaffian_pencils.py) and
[stable_pfaffian_charts.py](../tools/stable_pfaffian_charts.py).
The evidence includes every tested parameter triple, exact integer cost,
and a hash of each complete profile. The profiles can be regenerated by
the supplied functions.
