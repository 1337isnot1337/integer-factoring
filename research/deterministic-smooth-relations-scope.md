## Scope

The question is whether smooth norms, and so relations, can be produced
**deterministically and unconditionally** fast enough for a deterministic
L[1/2] factoring bound, or anything with exponent below the repository's
proved deterministic score of 1/2. This note does not improve that score.
It proves two structural lemmas that pin down **where** the unproved
inputs sit:

* B1 (genus invariance): the coverage hypothesis C, over the repository's
  own factor base and multiplier list, implies a joint least-quadratic-
  nonresidue statement at scale q^{o(1)}. No choice of multipliers or
  discriminant family can supply C without an analytic input of that strength.
  The same argument shows that unconditional JMV-type expansion with small
  generators would imply a Vinogradov-type bound.
* B2 (p-adic lattice chain): the repository's leading-power window
  Q_{p,k}=red(g_p^k) is exactly the sequence of normalized first minima of a
  descending index-p lattice chain fixed by the p-adic expansion of
  sqrt(Delta). It is the p-adic analogue of the CFRAC denominators, so a
  uniform window bound is at least as hard as a rigorous CFRAC-type
  smoothness theorem.

Labels: **PROVEN** (proved here, elementary), **VERIFIED** (finite exact
computation, script below), **KNOWN** (literature, cited), **CONJECTURE**
(heuristic or open). Nothing here is claimed as a new algorithm.

## 1. Setting

Following smooth-norm-localization.md, Section 1: N is an odd composite
non-prime-power, b=bits(N), Delta=-dN for multipliers d<=12, and H is the
subgroup of Cl(Delta) generated by prime forms at unramified split primes
<=Y_b, where Y_b=2^{ceil(s_b/2)} and s_b=ceil(sqrt(b ell_b)). Coverage
hypothesis C requires that, for some good d, H[2] has nontrivial image in the
coprime factorization group of N.

## 2. Lemma B1: genus invariance (PROVEN)

**Lemma B1.** Let N=q1 q2 with q1, q2 distinct odd primes such that
(q1/q2)=(q2/q1)=-1. Let s>=1 be coprime to N, with Delta=-sN a discriminant,
and suppose every prime factor of s is a quadratic residue modulo q1 and
modulo q2. Let S be a set of primitive forms of discriminant Delta. Suppose
each form in S represents some positive integer coprime to N that is a
quadratic residue modulo q1 and q2. Then no element of order <=2 in
H=<S> gives a nontrivial splitting of N.

*Proof.* For an odd prime q dividing Delta, the genus character
chi_q(f)=(m/q), where m is any integer coprime to q represented by f, is
well-defined and is a homomorphism Cl(Delta)->{+-1} (KNOWN: Gauss's genus
theory; Cox, *Primes of the form x^2+ny^2*, Section 3; Buell, *Binary
Quadratic Forms*, Chapter 4). By hypothesis chi_{q1} and chi_{q2} are +1 on S,
so H lies in ker chi_{q1} intersect ker chi_{q2}. Each class of order <=2
contains a reduced ambiguous form (a,b,c) with b=0, b=a or a=c. Such a form
represents m=a, a or 2a-b respectively, and m divides |Delta|. This is the
standard correspondence used in Lenstra--Pomerance Theorem 2.5. The
splitting of N given by the form is gcd(m,N). Suppose it is nontrivial, say
q1|m and q2 does not divide m. Then m=q1 m' with m' | s, and m is coprime to
q2. So

    chi_{q2}(f) = (q1/q2) * prod_{r | m'} (r/q2) = (-1)(+1) = -1,

which places f outside H. The case with q1 and q2 swapped is symmetric.
(Signs: m>0 because the forms are positive definite. A factor 2 of m' is
covered because every prime factor of s, 2 included, is a residue.) QED

**Corollary B1a (PROVEN).** Suppose hypothesis C holds for all preprocessed
inputs of b bits, using the repository's base (split primes <=Y_b) and
multipliers d<=12. Then there is no pair of primes q1, q2 with q1 q2 of b
bits, q1≡q2≡1 mod 8, (q1/q2)=-1 and least quadratic nonresidues n(q1), n(q2) > Y_b.

*Proof.* Since qi≡1 mod 4, reciprocity gives (q2/q1)=(q1/q2)=-1, and
N≡1 mod 8, so -dN is a discriminant for every d in the repository's list.
If n(qi) > Y_b >= 12, every prime <=Y_b is a residue mod qi. That covers
every prime factor of every d<=12 and every base prime. A prime form of norm
p represents p. By B1, H[2] has trivial image in the factorizations of N
for every multiplier, so C fails for N=q1 q2. QED

*Strength of the conclusion.* Y_b = q^{o(1)} (indeed exp(O(sqrt(log q log log q)))).
The best unconditional bound is Burgess's n(q) << q^{1/(4 sqrt e)+eps}
(KNOWN, Burgess 1957). Under GRH n(q) <= 2 log^2 q (KNOWN, Ankeny 1952;
Bach 1990), so pairs as in B1a do not exist for large b. They also do not
exist under the repository's pinned quasi-RH input. The joint statement in B1a
is therefore implied by the pinned analytic input but is not known
unconditionally (CONJECTURE that it holds; open unconditionally).
Consequence for Level 2: averaging over the multiplier and discriminant
family cannot replace the analytic input for C. Every d<=12 sees the same
genus obstruction. Lenstra--Pomerance avoid it through randomness: they use
random prime forms with norms up to exp(c (log|Delta|)^2), and some of those
are nonresidues (KNOWN: Lenstra--Pomerance 1992, Sections 8--10).

**Corollary B1b (PROVEN).** Suppose Cay(Cl(Delta), prime forms of norm <=T)
is connected (the generators generate Cl) for every Delta=-q q' with q' chosen
so that (q'/q)=-1 and q q' ≡ 3 mod 4. Then n(q) <= T for every odd prime q
admitting such a q'. *Proof:* if n(q)>T, every split prime p<=T has
chi_q = (p/q) = +1, so the generated subgroup lies in ker chi_q. That kernel is
proper, because the form representing q' has chi_q = (q'/q) = -1. QED.
At T=|Delta|^{o(1)} this is a Vinogradov-type bound n(q)<=q^{o(1)}, open
unconditionally (KNOWN status: best is Burgess). Consequence for Level 3:
unconditional JMV-style expansion (Jao--Miller--Venkatesan, JNT 2009,
arXiv 0811.0647, which assumes GRH) with q^{o(1)}-norm generators is at least
as hard as Vinogradov's conjecture.

*Scope of B1.* It obstructs only 2-torsion extraction from H. A lucky
gcd(A,N)>1 met during the search escapes it but is not guaranteed. B1 says
nothing against U_beta: U_beta's targets lie in H, inside the principal
genus intersection, where genus characters carry no information.

**VERIFIED** (`check_genus_invariance.py`): checks the ambiguous-form
divisor enumeration against brute force for 1999 discriminants |D|<4000,
and genus-character multiplicativity on 4000 products. It runs explicit
pairs (q1,q2) = (8089,13729), (87481,336361), (515761,1083289), with
n(qi)=17,17 / 29,29 / 37,41 and Y=13/23/31, across 54/68/73 multipliers
s<200. In every case all base characters are +1 and every splitting
ambiguous form has a -1 character. A full BFS of H finds 0 splitting

the non-splitting ambiguous forms do lie in H. Control: for the same N and

## 3. Lemma B2: the window is a p-adic lattice chain (PROVEN)

Let p be an odd prime with (Delta/p)=1 and p not dividing Delta. Let b_k be
the Hensel lift of a fixed square root of Delta to Z/2p^kZ with b_k ≡ Delta
mod 2. Let c_k=(b_k^2-Delta)/(4p^k), and

    L_k = {(u,y) in Z^2 : u ≡ b_k y (mod 2p^k)}.

**Lemma B2.** (i) g_p^k is equivalent to (p^k,b_k,c_k) (KNOWN: standard
composition of a split prime form with itself; Buell Chapter 4, Cohen
Algorithm 5.4.7). (ii) (x,y) -> (2p^k x + b_k y, y) is a bijection Z^2 -> L_k,
and 4p^k (p^k x^2 + b_k xy + c_k y^2) = u^2 + |Delta| y^2. (iii) So the
reduced leading coefficient is

    A_{p,k} = min_{0 != (u,y) in L_k} (u^2 + |Delta| y^2) / (4p^k),

because the leading coefficient of the reduced form is the form's minimum.
(iv) b_{k+1} ≡ b_k mod 2p^k, so L_{k+1} is a sublattice of L_k of index p.

So the window {A_{p,k}: K_p <= k < K_p+W} is the sequence of normalized
first minima of a descending index-p chain. The p-adic expansion of
sqrt(Delta) fixes the chain. Equivalently, A_{p,k} is the norm of the
p-adically best approximation of sqrt(Delta) at level k. This is the p-adic
mirror of CFRAC, whose Q_k are norms of the real best approximations to
sqrt(N).

*Consequence (CONJECTURE-level analogy, stated as such).* CFRAC has no
rigorous running-time analysis (KNOWN: Pomerance 1982, "Analysis and
comparison of some integer factoring algorithms"; Crandall--Pomerance,
*Prime Numbers*, CFRAC discussion). The repository's "uniform bound on the
required power window" is a deterministic smoothness statement about
first minima along one explicit arithmetic sequence. That is the same kind
of statement CFRAC lacks. We found no p-adic CFRAC factoring analysis (see
the literature list). B2 does not prove the window bound hard. It
identifies it as an open problem of known type.

**VERIFIED** (`check_padic_window.py`): [1] composition equals Gauss
reduction of (p^k,b_k,c_k), with chain inclusion, in 7575 (N,p,k) cases
(40 random N in [1e9,1e11], odd p<=60, k=K..K+24). It also equals the
brute-force lattice minimum in 909 cases (k=K..K+2). [2] Finite heuristic
data only: for 6 N in [1e13,1e14] with Y=200, the 200-smooth rate in the
window (8 generators x 400 steps) tracks the rate for leading coefficients
of random classes: 0.090/0.080, 0.163/0.180, 0.116/0.117, 0.099/0.105,
0.043/0.044, 0.134/0.141. The largest first-hit offset was 103.
This is consistent with "the window behaves like random classes". It is
evidence, not a bound.

## 5. The wall and the missing lemma

C reduces to the pinned analytic input: B1a shows it needs that strength,
and the pinned quasi-RH already implies the needed nonresidue statements. So
the deterministic path is walled at **U_beta alone**:

> **Missing lemma (unproved; equivalent target to U_beta).** There are
> effective C, beta>0 such that for every good Delta of b bits and every
> generator g_i, some target class Q_{i,u} in H contains a primitive vector
> (x,y) with |x|,|y| <= 2^{Cb/ell_b^beta} whose value is <=X, coprime to
> Delta and Y_b-smooth.

This is a smooth-value localization theorem at norm |Delta|^{1/2+o(1)} for
individually prescribed classes of a positive-definite form. It would be
needed even under the pinned quasi-RH, because the L-infinity character bound
from Re s > 31/32 only localizes at polynomially larger norm. The Dirichlet
analogue at that strength is beyond Harman's theorem (Section 4).
Equivalently, on the Level 1 route: a uniform bound W=2^{O(b/ell_b^beta)}
on the p-adic CFRAC window of Lemma B2.
