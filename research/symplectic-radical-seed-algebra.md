# The coefficient algebra of the 26-label radical seed

The 26-label symplectic radical crossing has a small exact coefficient
algebra in both matrix cuts. The row algebra has dimension 45 and split
semisimple quotient `F₂³`; the column algebra has dimension 11 and quotient
`F₂²`. Their representations on the 676-dimensional cut spaces have short
radical filtrations. This note records the exact algebras and extracts a
rank-cost obstruction for literal common-parameter coefficient expansions.

All algebraic identities extend to every field of characteristic two.
The obstruction concerns the stated coefficient expansions. It does not
exclude arbitrary recombinations in the coefficient space of a tensor
power, scalar-weighted rational parameter curves, or other label codes.

The later [common-character theorem](symplectic-seed-character-barrier.md)
settles the first two extensions for this seed: the entire six-generator
span is excluded over every characteristic-two field at every tensor
power. The algebra calculations here supply part of that stronger proof.

## 1. Normalization and matrix convention

Use the seed and coefficient basis in
[the finite-profile note](symplectic-boundary-code-profiles.md). Put
\[
 u=q+r,\quad v=p+q,\quad s=qr.
\]
The exact tensor is
\[
 G=u^4E+u^3vA+u^2v^2B+uv^3C+s u^2D+suvF.
 \tag{1}
\]
After taking out the scalar `u⁴`, the normalized family is
\[
 R(t,z)=E+tA+t^2B+t^3C+zD+ztF,
 \qquad t=v/u,\quad z=s/u^2.
 \tag{2}
\]
In each cut, `E` is the 676 by 676 identity. Both matrices are block
diagonal in eight syndrome blocks of sizes
\[
 100,80,80,80,80,80,80,96.
\]
The row cut is `(x,y)|(v,u)` and the column cut is `(x,u)|(v,y)`.
All algebra products below are ordinary matrix products within a single
cut. The two cut algebras need not be isomorphic.

The durable coefficient export selects the monomials
`r⁴,pr³,p²r²,p³r,qr³,pqr²`. If their matrices are `B₀,…,B₅`, the
normalization uses
\[
 (E,A,B,C,D,F)=(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3,B_1+B_4,B_1+B_5).
\]
The checker verifies (1) directly from the exported scalar coefficient
polynomials before constructing either algebra.

## 2. Exact algebras and their radicals

Let `𝒜` be the unital algebra generated by `A,B,C,D,F`. The checker
constructs a basis of actual matrices, closes it under left multiplication
by each generator, and then reconstructs every product of two basis
matrices. The resulting multiplication tables have 2025 and 121 products.

Let `𝒥` be the two-sided ideal generated by all generator commutators,
\[
 A^2+A,\quad B^2+B,\quad C^2+C,\quad D,\quad F.
 \tag{3}
\]
The exact dimensions are:

| Cut | `dim 𝒜` | `dim 𝒥` | `dim 𝒥²` | `dim 𝒥³` | `dim 𝒥⁴` | Semisimple quotient |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Row | 45 | 42 | 26 | 6 | 0 | `F₂³` |
| Column | 11 | 9 | 4 | 0 | 0 | `F₂²` |

The ideal generated by `D,F` has dimension 22 in the row algebra and 5
in the column algebra. It squares to zero in each cut.

These data identify the exact Jacobson radical. Indeed, (3) makes the
quotient commutative and generated by idempotents. The character maps
listed below are independent and their number equals the quotient
dimension. They identify that quotient with a product of copies of `F₂`.
The proposed ideal is nilpotent by the complete multiplication table,
so it is precisely the Jacobson radical. After extending scalars to a
characteristic-two field `K`, the same proof gives quotients `K³` and
`K²` and the same radical-power dimensions.

The characters send `E` to 1 and `D,F` to 0. Their values on `A,B,C` and
their multiplicities in the actual cut representation are:

| Cut | Character `(A,B,C)` | Multiplicity | Scalar value of `R(t,z)` |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Row | `(0,1,0)` | 436 | `(1+t)²` |
| Row | `(1,0,0)` | 40 | `1+t` |
| Row | `(1,1,1)` | 200 | `(1+t)³` |
| Column | `(0,0,0)` | 668 | `1` |
| Column | `(1,1,0)` | 8 | `1+t+t²` |

In particular,
\[
 \det R_{\rm row}(t,z)=(1+t)^{1512},\qquad
 \det R_{\rm col}(t,z)=(1+t+t^2)^8.
 \tag{4}
\]
The row matrix `R(1,z)` belongs to the radical after adjoining `z`, so
\[
 R_{\rm row}(1,z)^4=0.
 \tag{5}
\]
The column matrix `R(1,z)` is invertible. These statements hold for
every `z`, including values in extension fields.

\[
 V\supseteq\mathcal JV\supseteq\mathcal J^2V\supseteq\cdots.

## 4. Character minors in tensor-power coefficients

The adapted matrices give lower bounds as well as possible upper-bound
tools. A triangular matrix has rank at least the number of its nonzero
diagonal entries: select their row and column indices to obtain an
invertible triangular principal submatrix.

Tensor products preserve the constant triangular bases. Consequently,
each polynomial coefficient of a tensor power has the same triangular
structure, and its nonzero diagonal character values give exact minors.

First consider
\[
 R(1+x,z(x))^{\otimes n}
       =\sum_k T_kx^k,
\]
for an arbitrary polynomial `z(x)`. The row diagonal characters are
`x,x²,x³`, with multiplicities 40, 436, and 200. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\rm row}T_k
 \ \ge\ [X^k]\bigl(40X+436X^2+200X^3\bigr)^n,
 \tag{6}
\]
where the coefficient on the right is an ordinary nonnegative integer.
There is no reduction modulo two in this count: it counts distinct
diagonal positions whose scalar coefficient is 1.

For the column cut, choose exactly `j` tensor factors from the
eight-dimensional nontrivial character space. Their scalar polynomial
is `(1+x+x²)^j`, with leading coefficient 1 at degree `2j`. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\rm col}T_{2j}
 \ \ge\ \binom nj668^{n-j}8^j.
 \tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7) gives a lower bound on the cost of even a single
coefficient piece. Optimizing its asymptotic exponent gives the
numerical lower base 107.276832. That numerical optimization is not
needed to separate the construction from the seed threshold.

For a simple exact separation, take row character counts `6n/10` of
degree 1 and `4n/10` of degree 2, and column nontrivial count `7n/10`.
Both refer to coefficient degree `14n/10`. When `10` divides `n`,
\[
 \begin{split}
 S_n&=\sum_k\sqrt{
        \operatorname{rank}_{\rm row}T_k
        \operatorname{rank}_{\rm col}T_k}\\
 &\ge\sqrt{
   \binom n{4n/10}40^{6n/10}436^{4n/10}
   \binom n{7n/10}668^{3n/10}8^{7n/10}}
 \ge \frac{B^n}{n+1},
 \end{split}
 \tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 B^{20}=
 \frac{10^{20}40^6 436^4 668^3 8^7}
      {6^6 4^4 7^7 3^3},
 \qquad B=106.4396369\ldots.
 \tag{9}
\]
The last inequality is the elementary binomial type bound. Rounding
the two type counts, with the matching parity, gives the same limiting
exponential lower bound for all `n`.

The checker proves with integer arithmetic that
\[
 B>26^{99/70}>26^{\sqrt2}=100.2477189\ldots.
 \tag{10}
\]
It also verifies that (8) already exceeds the seed threshold for every
`n` divisible by 10 with `n≥80`. For the centered family, using every
nonzero diagonal coefficient rather than just the leading terms gives
the following finite lower bounds:

| Tensor power `n` | `n`th root of a certified integer cost lower bound | Above `26^{√2}`? |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 76.0000 | No conclusion |
| 2 | 88.1930 | No conclusion |
| 4 | 99.7343 | No conclusion |
| 8 | 103.3134 | Yes |
| 16 | 105.3071 | Yes |
| 32 | 106.3028 | Yes |
| 64 | 106.7917 | Yes |
| 128 | 107.0347 | Yes |

These lower bounds use actual coefficient minors. They are not upper
envelopes inferred from the semisimple quotient.

## 5. Scope of the polynomial-curve obstruction

The asymptotic lower bound (9) holds more generally for
\[
 R(t(x),z(x))^{\otimes n}
\]
whenever `t(x)` is a nonconstant polynomial. Let `h=deg t`. A row
character type with total exponent `κ` has leading degree `hκ` in
`(1+t(x))^κ`. A column type with `j` nontrivial factors has leading
degree `2hj` in `(1+t(x)+t(x)²)^j`. Choosing `κ=2j` reproduces (8) at
degree `2hj`. The leading scalar coefficients are nonzero over every
characteristic-two field. The polynomial `z(x)` does not affect either
diagonal character.

This settles literal coefficient expansions of normalized polynomial
curves, including nonlinear choices of `t` and `z`. It does not settle
arbitrary linear recombination of the coefficients. Such a
recombination can annihilate the scalar character blocks while leaving
a nonzero radical operator. It also does not settle expansions with a
nonconstant scalar prefactor, as in the homogeneous rational
parametrization `u(x)⁴R(v(x)/u(x),s(x)/u(x)²)`. Those operations require
separate two-cut rank analysis. That analysis is now supplied by the
[all-power common-character theorem](symplectic-seed-character-barrier.md).
