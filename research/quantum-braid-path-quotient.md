# Exact path quotient and phase descent for the block braid

The [ambient block-braid compiler](quantum-block-braid-crossings.md),
at its default order-five parameter, has an exact height-three path
model. The model's local matrices lie in `F4`. An explicit periodic
coefficient weighting then gives a strict crossing over `F4`, with
degree at most `n^2`, and omits one residue class of coefficients.
Each retained piece has the same two actual ranks as its phase-scaled
ambient counterpart. A favorable uniform rank bound remains open.

For background on Jones quotients and their Fibonacci dimensions,
see [Iohara, Lehrer and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08128).
The argument below uses explicit local identities in the fixed fields
at hand and does not import a characteristic-zero classification.
Earlier local F5 path-relation work is credited in the ambient note.

## 1. Orthogonal frames and the negligible wall

Fix `F16=F2[a]/(a^4+a+1)`, `q=a^3`, and
`delta=q+q^-1`. Thus `[5]_q=0`, `[1]_q=[4]_q=1`,
`[2]_q=[3]_q=delta`, and `delta^2=delta+1`.
Identify `F4=F2[alpha]/(alpha^2+alpha+1)` by `alpha=delta`.

The paired encoder is orthogonal for the ordinary bilinear word
pairing. More precisely, a return path `P` has norm

```math
\nu_P=\prod_{\text{down from }h}q[h]_q[h+1]_q\ne0,
\qquad D_P=F_P^T/\nu_P.                         \tag{1}
```

Here is a local proof rather than an assumption about the dual.
Let `g_(h,r)` be the magnetic norms in the ambient note. For the
splitting matrix `U_h`, direct substitution gives

```math
U_h^T(\operatorname{diag}(g_{h,r})\otimes I)U_h
 =\operatorname{diag}\bigl(g_{h+1,r},
                         q[h]_q[h+1]_qg_{h-1,r}\bigr).
```

The mixed blocks vanish. Induction on the prefix proves orthogonality
between different paths and magnetic states. At final height zero,
`g_(0,0)=1`, giving (1). The inverse-dual recursion therefore agrees
with these norm duals at every length.

Let `f_h` be the small symmetrizers from the ambient proof. They kill
all adjacent cup generators on their `h` strands. Their fusion
resolution is

```math
f_h\otimes I=f_{h+1}
 +\frac{[h]_q}{[h+1]_q}
   (f_h\otimes I)e_{h-1}(f_h\otimes I).         \tag{2}
```

The second term is the down-channel projector. Every denominator
in (2), through the height-three wall, is a unit.

The relevant diagram closure is the quantum trace

```math
\operatorname{tr}_q(T)
 =\sum_w q^{h-2|w|}T_{ww}.
```

The ordinary projector `f_4` has rank five. Its partial quantum trace
over the last strand is zero: the general expression is
`[5]_q/[4]_q f_3`. Also `f_4 e_i=e_i f_4=0` for its three internal
generators. These identities prove that any closed planar cup diagram
containing `f_4` evaluates to zero. To see this, remove that coupon
and follow the noncrossing matching around it. A turnback is killed
by `f_4`; the remaining through closures reduce by partial trace.
Closed loops elsewhere only contribute powers of `delta`.

Consequently the ideal generated by `f_4` is killed by every vacuum
cup pairing. Equation (1) makes the implemented duals such pairings,
up to nonzero scalar factors. This explains precisely why a term may
be discarded at the wall even when its ambient vector is nonzero.

Iterate (2) from left to right, using the wall rule `f_4=0` in that
ideal quotient. This expands every vacuum cup state into height-three
fusion paths. A positive final height is killed by the closing cups,
so the surviving labels end at zero. Their Gram norms (1) are units,
which makes this spanning set a basis of the vacuum quotient.
Every Temperley--Lieb word can therefore be evaluated in this basis
and gives exactly its ambient matrix coefficients against `D_Q,F_P`.
This argument applies coefficientwise to polynomial words as well.

## 2. The actual local path matrices

At a local return from height `h`, let `A=UD` and `B=DU` be the two
branches. Recoupling gives, with columns indexed by the input branch,

```math
\rho(e_i)=\frac1{[h+1]_q}
\begin{pmatrix}[h+2]_q&[h]_q\\[h+2]_q&[h]_q\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{3}
```

At height zero only `A` survives. At height three the `A` branch
contains `f_4`, so only `B` is retained; `[5]_q=0` makes its quotient
action consistent. In the encoding `alpha=2`, the scalar multiplying
both the unchanged and swapped path is

| Prefix height | Input `UD` | Input `DU` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | alpha | absent |
| 1 | 1 | alpha+1 |
| 2 | alpha+1 | 1 |
| 3 | absent | alpha |

The swap is omitted when its intermediate height would leave `0..3`.
Equal consecutive steps have zero action. This is the implementation
in [`quantum_braid_path.py`](../tools/quantum_braid_path.py).

The local square and adjacent TL relations can be checked on the
sixteen length-three walks with starting heights `0..3`; distant
generators commute because swapping an opposite pair does not change
the prefix height seen by a distant pair. The implementation also
checks `f_4=0` on all bounded four-step local modules. The comparison
to the ambient matrices is supplied by section 1 and the local
recoupling identity, including every parent magnetic state.

For the block braid, use the full path space at length `2n` and take
its rows and columns indexed by concatenated return paths `PQ`.
This middle-zero block is the desired four-leg tensor. It has
`L_(2n)=Fibonacci_(2n-1)` intermediate states, instead of the ambient
binary word space. The boundary block is taken after the full word
has been applied; it is not asserted to be an invariant subspace.

## 3. A strict F4 crossing with periodic weights

Put `D=n^2` and let `A_j` be the middle-zero coefficients of
`product(I+s rho(e_i))`, using the same block-braid word. All `A_j`
lie in `F4`. If `C_j` denotes the ambient phase-normalized coefficient
before its final affine scalar, section 1 gives the exact identity

```math
C_j=q^{D/2-j}A_j.                              \tag{4}
```

The ambient endpoint proves `E=sum_j q^(D/2-j) A_j`.
Use the `F4`-basis `(1,q)` of `F16` and a linear map
`pi_tau(u+vq)=u+tau v`. It fixes one. The coordinate pairs of
`1,q,q^2,q^3,q^4` are

```text
(1,0), (0,1), (1,alpha), (alpha,alpha), (alpha,1).
```

Choose `tau=(0,1,0,alpha,alpha+1)[D/2 mod 5]` and define

```math
b_j=\pi_\tau(q^{D/2-j}),\qquad B_j=b_j A_j.
```

Then `b_0=1` and `sum B_j=E`. Since `A_0` is the identity tensor,
`B(t)=sum B_j t^j` has both strict endpoints: identity at zero and
crossing at one. The weights are explicit and periodic with period
five. Exactly one residue class has zero weight. This projection is
used on the completed coefficient identity; its multiplicativity is
not assumed in the gate product.

For each retained coefficient, extension from `F4` to `F16` and the
nonzero scalars in (4) leave both matrix ranks unchanged. Thus this
descent has no extension-degree rank multiplier on the retained pieces.
The deleted pieces are simply absent from the new exact decomposition.

Single-entry construction costs `poly(n)*phi^(2n)` field operations
with the path recurrence. For binary-selected labels `n=O(lambda)`,
it fits the existing `2^O(lambda)` construction budget. The optional
NumPy batch computes all boundary entries at once. Its extra factor
is the number of input path pairs, which is still exponential of
order `2^O(lambda)`.

The degree and coefficient count remain `O(lambda^2)`. An effective
favorable recombination, or a favorable bound on the explicit pieces,
is still required for an improved factoring score.

## Evidence and scope

Run `make verify-braid-path`. The standard-library
[receipt](../evidence/quantum-braid-path.json) checks the metric identity,
the nonzero wall projector and its zero partial quantum trace, local
recoupling on every magnetic component, local path relations, norm
duals through length ten, complete small endpoints, comparison with
the ambient coefficient algorithm, and independently computed phase
coordinates. The all-length comparison follows from the ideal and
fusion-resolution argument above, rather than from extending a finite
numerical match. Favorable two-cut rank growth is not established.

The optional NumPy replay `make verify-braid-path-profiles` gives the
[complete finite profiles](../evidence/quantum-braid-path-profiles.json):

| Sites | Labels | Intermediate paths | Unprojected span | Strict F4 span | Central rank pair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 2 | 13 | 5 | 5 | (4,4) |
| 6 | 5 | 89 | 33 | 29 | (24,24) |
| 8 | 13 | 610 | 62 | 51 | (169,169) |

All 641 four- and six-site entries are compared coefficientwise with
the ambient algorithm, for 24,038 coefficient controls. Complete
endpoints and both coefficient-cut profiles are checked through
eight sites, with packed F4 ranks controlled against a separate
elementary field implementation.

The central coefficient always has weight `b_(D/2)=1`. At eight sites
it is full rank in both cuts, which is a bottleneck for that explicit
finite coefficient list. The projector evaluation `A(alpha^-1)` has
rank pair `(5,24)` at six sites and `(13,168)` at eight sites; the
reciprocal evaluation exchanges the cuts. These are further finite
structural observations, not uniform formulas or an optimized span
decomposition. As with the earlier probes, all these alphabet sizes
fall below the universal 95-label strict rank-sum exclusion. The
remaining useful targets are effective recombinations and weighted
constructions, with the actual asymptotic rank obligations intact.

The complete factoring score stays `1/2`. These arguments and tools
used substantial Codex AI assistance.
