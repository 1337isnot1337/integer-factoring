# An explicit degree-sixteen affine reflection crossing

## Constructive result

There is an explicit four-letter polynomial tensor over `F4`, of degree
sixteen, with the strict crossing endpoint and both paired traces

```math
\sum_x S_{xyux}(w)=(1+w^{16})\delta_{yu},\qquad
\sum_y S_{xyyv}(w)=(1+w^{16})\delta_{xv}.            \tag{1}
```

The complete 256 polynomial entries are in
[`degree16-reflection-crossing.json`](../evidence/degree16-reflection-crossing.json).
This attains the degree floor of the
[uniform paired-trace theorem](reflection-paired-trace-invariant.md)
at reflection interval four. The original reflection tensor has degree
twenty-four.

This is a degree improvement within the affine family generated by
independent paired physical frames. It is not an improved pressure
certificate or factoring exponent. The first unconstrained representative
has poor natural weighted profiles. Further searches must combine the
degree constraints with appropriate zero and finite-place jet conditions.

## 1. The explicit tensor and its exact proof

Write `F4=F2[omega]/(omega^2+omega+1)`. The entry at array position

```math
x+4y+16u+64v
```

is a pair of nonnegative integers `[a,b]`. Their binary digits encode the
coefficients of `a(w)+omega b(w)`. The tensor digest is

```text
4ac27b8fa50b35c1211bd220ca2aac67593154b9afc3776ac4e237d5bb078ae7
```

The [exact checker](../tools/check_degree16_reflection_crossing.py)
performs the following finite coefficient identities:

- every entry has degree at most sixteen, and an entry has degree sixteen;
- at `w=1`, every entry equals `delta_(x,v) delta_(y,u)`;
- all 32 polynomial entries in the two contractions equal (1).

These identities provide a complete ordinary proof for the displayed
finite tensor. No numerical optimization or local-profile heuristic is
used to establish them.

Equation (1) contains a nonzero coefficient of degree sixteen. Therefore
every polynomial tensor with these same paired traces has degree at least
sixteen. The explicit tensor attains that bound. This is sharpness within
the stated trace constraints; arbitrary crossing tensors need not satisfy
those constraints.

The tensor also has scalar polynomial content one. Any common factor
would divide `1+w^16=(1+w)^16`, while its strict endpoint contains entries
equal to one.

## 2. Why independent paired frames matter

The coupled Bell frame applies the same matrix on two physical legs:

```math
(A,A^{-T},A,A^{-T}).
```

It preserves the additional double contraction

```math
\sum_{x,u} T^{\rm Bell}_{x x u u}(w).
```

For the original four-letter reflection seed, this polynomial is exactly

```math
w^8+w^{12}+w^{20}+w^{24}.                          \tag{2}
```

The degree-twenty-four term in (2) survives every coupled rational frame
and every affine combination whose coefficients sum to one. Thus coupled
constant companions alone cannot lower the degree. The exact complete
companion-span search confirms this obstruction.

Independent paired actions

```math
(A_x,B_y,B^{-T}_u,A^{-T}_v)
```

preserve (1), but need not preserve (2). They provide the additional
directions used by the degree-sixteen construction.

## 3. The constructive span calculation

The initial exact search reconstructed all 21,528 constant companions
from the [completed companion catalogue](constant-companion-reflection-search.md).
Their polynomial affine differences span a 1,911-dimensional space over
`F4`. The high-degree coefficients, from degrees seventeen through
twenty-four, span a space of dimension 1,112. No affine target of degree
at most twenty-three is feasible in that coupled span.

The [span solver](../tools/solve_companion_affine_degree.py) preserves the
complete polynomial basis, rather than only its evaluations at a few
points. Its [record](../evidence/companion-affine-degree-search.json)
and [compressed basis](../evidence/companion-affine-polynomial-span.json.gz)
bind every reconstructed tensor to the published companion archive.

To enlarge the space, take every elementary matrix `E_ij`, `i!=j`,
on either physical pair `x/v` or `y/u`. The two operators

```math
L=E_{\rm left}+E^T_{\rm right},\qquad
Q=E_{\rm left}E^T_{\rm right}                      \tag{3}
```

satisfy

```math
R_{I+cE}-I=cL+c^2Q.
```

Using `c=1,omega`, the coefficient matrix is invertible over `F4`.
Consequently the images under `L,Q` are linear combinations of
constant paired-frame differences. Both operators annihilate the
crossing endpoint. Applying them to already known affine differences
therefore gives further valid directions.

There are 48 operators in (3): two physical pairs, twelve ordered
off-diagonal positions, and the two displayed operators. The
[closure program](../tools/close_independent_paired_affine_span.py)
uses exact polynomial bitplanes and finite-field elimination.
After processing thirty basis vectors, its space has dimension 3,035
and contains a degree-sixteen target. The explicit target uses 1,168
of these basis vectors.

This is a prefix of the independent-paired closure. It is not a claim
that the full invariant module has dimension 3,035. The
[prefix record](../evidence/independent-paired-affine-degree-prefix.json)
and [basis](../evidence/independent-paired-affine-degree-basis.json.gz)
are preserved for exact higher-jet and rank constraints.

## 4. Independent verification and remaining work

The checker reconstructs the displayed tensor from the basis witness.
It checks that all 3,035 basis vectors have zero crossing endpoint and
zero paired traces. Separate exponent dictionaries and finite-field
arithmetic verify 2,048 complete coefficient entries of the packed
physical operators, using both directions of the index shifts and both
physical pairs. The [evidence](../evidence/degree16-reflection-crossing-check.json)
records these checks and source hashes.

```sh
python3 tools/check_degree16_reflection_crossing.py \
  --output /tmp/degree16-reflection-crossing-check.json
```

The first degree-sixteen representative does not preserve the original
constant tensor. In this recorded prefix, the target that also preserves
all coefficients through degree zero is infeasible. That is a statement
about this finite prefix, not a universal obstruction to protected degree
reduction. The broader closure and weighted low-jet constraints remain
separate search tasks.

Degree reduction alone does not control either complete weighted cut.
Every resulting tensor must still be charged using its actual local
Smith profiles, one common pair of physical types, and the full
interpolation degree.
