# A three-CNOT crossing path and its tensor modules

## Construction and scope

This note constructs a row-involutive polynomial crossing in every binary
label size. The two-label instance has thin coefficients in both cuts and
differs from the earlier nilpotent-flag path. Exact constant changes of basis
expose its row and column modules.

The module structure proves that ordinary univariate coefficient grouping of
every tensor power misses the strict target. This conclusion does not address
arbitrary linear recombinations of coefficient tensors, nonsymmetric tensor
spans, or extensions of the coefficient field. It does not improve the
factoring exponent.

## 1. Three controlled additions

Let the labels form `F2^r`, so `q=2^r`. On pairs of labels define permutation
matrices

\[
 A(u,v)=(u+v,v),\qquad B(u,v)=(u,u+v).
\]

Both are involutions, and `ABA` is the flip `E`. Over characteristic two put

\[
 A(z)=I+z(I+A),\qquad B(z)=I+z(I+B),
\]

and define

\[
 K_q(z)=A(z)B(z)A(z).                                       \tag{1}
\]

Since `(I+A)^2=(I+B)^2=0`, both factors are polynomial involutions. Hence

\[
 K_q(z)^2=I,\qquad K_q(0)=I,\qquad K_q(1)=E.                 \tag{2}
\]

In particular, the row polynomial is unimodular in every binary label size.
Expanding (1) gives the explicit coefficients

\[
 K_q(z)=I+z(I+B)+z^2(AB+BA)+z^3(B+AB+BA+ABA).                \tag{3}
\]

No Yang--Baxter identity or formalization assumption is used.

For two labels, in row-major order `00,01,10,11`, the coefficient matrices as
packed binary rows are

| Degree | Packed rows | Row rank | Column rank |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | `[1,2,4,8]` | 4 | 1 |
| 1 | `[0,0,12,12]` | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | `[0,12,10,6]` | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | `[0,10,0,10]` | 1 | 1 |

Equivalently, storing polynomial coefficients as binary masks, the full row
polynomial is

```text
[[1, 0, 0, 0],
 [0, 9, 4, 12],
 [0, 4, 3, 6],
 [0, 12, 6, 11]].
```

The mask `9`, for example, denotes `1+z^3`.

## 2. The row module

The constant basis with columns represented by masks `[1,14,12,10]` changes
the two-label row polynomial into

\[
 I_2\ \oplus\
 M(z),\qquad
 M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1+z^2&z\\z^3&1+z^2\end{pmatrix}.       \tag{4}
\]

Thus there are two trivial one-dimensional summands and one nontrivial
two-dimensional summand. The last is the standard two-dimensional module of
the group generated by `A` and `B`; the explicit basis identity (4) is all
that is needed below.

Consider the coefficient of degree `3k` in `K_2(z)^tensor(t)`. Choose exactly
`k` tensor factors from the summand `M(z)` and the remaining factors from the
two-dimensional trivial part. This gives a direct-sum block with multiplicity

\[
 \binom tk2^{t-k}.
\]

Within each chosen block, degree `3k` is the highest possible degree. Its
coefficient is the tensor product of `k` copies of the rank-one top
coefficient of `M`. It has rank one. Therefore

\[
 r_{3k}^{\rm row}\ge\binom tk2^{t-k}.                        \tag{5}
\]

## 3. The column flag

In the column cut, the constant basis with columns `[6,9,1,2]` gives the
upper triangular polynomial matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z^2&z^2+z^3&0&z^2\\
 0&z^3&1+z&z+z^2\\
 0&0&z&z+z^2\\
 0&0&0&z^2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                            \tag{6}
\]

The diagonal monomials have degrees `2,3,1,2`. Tensoring retains upper
triangularity. The number of diagonal entries of degree `j` is the ordinary
integer coefficient

\[
 [x^j]\,(x+2x^2+x^3)^t=\binom{2t}{j-t}.                       \tag{7}
\]

This is a count of diagonal positions, not a binomial coefficient reduced
modulo two. Restricting to the principal submatrix on those positions gives
an upper triangular matrix with all diagonal entries equal to one. Hence

\[
 r_j^{\rm col}\ge\binom{2t}{j-t}
 \quad\text{when }0\le j-t\le2t.                            \tag{8}
\]

The local column determinant is `z^8`. The local row-infinity orders are
`0,3,3,6`, and the column-zero orders are `0,1,2,5`. The checker verifies these
orders independently through finite block-Toeplitz ranks.

## 4. An exact lower bound for every tensor exponent

Write `S_t` for the sum of the square roots of the two cut-rank products of
the ordinary coefficients of `K_2(z)^tensor(t)`. Its label count is `d=2^t`.

The constant coefficient contributes exactly `2^t`. For each `k` for which
`0<=3k-t<=2t`, combine (5) and (8). These are distinct positive degrees, so

\[
 S_t\ge M_t:=2^t+
 \sum_{0\le3k-t\le2t}
 \left\lfloor\sqrt{
       \binom tk2^{t-k}\binom{2t}{3k-t}}
 \right\rfloor.                                           \tag{9}
\]

The checker verifies with integers that

\[
 M_t^{70}>2^{99t}\qquad(1\le t\le100).                       \tag{10}
\]

Because `sqrt(2)<99/70`, every exponent in this finite range misses the
strict target `S_t<d^sqrt(2)`. Some values are:

| t | Labels 2^t | Integer lower bound M_t |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 2 | 4 | 9 |
| 3 | 8 | 22 |
| 4 | 16 | 63 |
| 5 | 32 | 186 |
| 6 | 64 | 553 |
| 7 | 128 | 1675 |
| 8 | 256 | 5157 |
| 9 | 512 | 15986 |
| 12 | 4096 | 490522 |

### All larger exponents

For `t>=100`, take

\[
 k=\left\lfloor\frac{2(t+1)}3\right\rfloor.
\]

This is a mode of the terms `binom(t,k)2^k`, whose sum is `3^t`. Thus

\[
 \binom tk2^{t-k}
 \ge\frac{3^t2^{t-2k}}{t+1}
 \ge\frac{3^t2^{-t/3-4/3}}{t+1}.                            \tag{11}
\]

Moreover, `3k-t` differs from `t` by `0`, `1`, or `2`. For `t>=2`, comparison
with the central binomial coefficient gives

\[
 \binom{2t}{3k-t}\ge\frac{4^t}{6(2t+1)}.                   \tag{12}
\]

Consequently the squared rank cost of this one coefficient is at least

\[
 \frac{(12/2^{1/3})^t}{6\,2^{4/3}(t+1)(2t+1)}
 >\frac{2^{99t/35}(4/3)^t}{16(t+1)(2t+1)}.                 \tag{13}
\]

The constants in the strict inequality have integer certificates:
`9^105>2^332` and `6^3*16<16^3`.
At `t=100`,

\[
 4^{100}>3^{100}\,16\cdot101\cdot201.
\]

The ratio `(4/3)^t/((t+1)(2t+1))` increases for `t>=100`: its consecutive
ratio exceeds one because `2t²-9t-14>0`. Thus (13) exceeds `2^(99t/35)`
for every `t>=100`. Taking a square root closes the remaining range.

Together, (10)--(13) prove that **no ordinary univariate tensor-power
coefficient decomposition of this path reaches the strict target**.

The asymptotic obstruction is stronger than a half-exponent comparison. For
`t` divisible by three, the single coefficient with `k=2t/3` has a lower
cost exponent in the label count of

\[
 \frac56+\frac12\log_2 3\approx1.625815,
\]

up to polynomial factors. This last estimate is explanatory; the all-power
statement above is proved by the exact finite checks and explicit bounds.

## 5. What remains open for this construction

The [full coefficient-space analysis](cnot-full-tensor-span.md) now gives
exact tensor block formulas, complete rank-one and column-rank-two
classifications, and an all-field exclusion of the entire third tensor power.

The obstruction isolates ordinary coefficients of one parameter. It does not
remain valid automatically after arbitrary coefficient-basis changes, because
those changes can combine the protected degrees. The full tensor coefficient
span, nonsymmetric combinations, and other fields therefore require their
own rank arguments. Any finite searches of those spaces must keep their
scope separate from the all-power theorem proved here.

## 6. Reproduction

Run the standard-library checker beside `check_nilpotent_flag_crossings.py`:

```sh
python3 tools/check_cnot_crossing_modules.py \
  --output evidence/cnot-crossing-modules.json
```

It verifies the literal crossing, both constant basis changes, both local
determinants, the local Smith profiles through independent Toeplitz ranks,
the one hundred exact inequalities, the constants for the all-large-power
proof, and endpoint controls of the same construction at four and eight
labels.
