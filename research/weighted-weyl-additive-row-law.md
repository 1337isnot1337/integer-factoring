# The exact multiplication law of the weighted Weyl coefficients

## Result and scope

Work over any characteristic-two field. For binary weighted occupation
labels of energy `h`, let `P_h(z)` be the row matrix of the weighted Weyl
curve, in row order `(alpha,y)|(beta,u)`. Define

```math
B_h(z)=P_h(z+1)=\sum_{i=0}^h z^i B_i,\qquad B_0=I.
```

The coefficient span is a commutative row-matrix algebra at **every**
energy. Its exact multiplication constants are given in (5) below.
At a power-of-two energy `h=2^R>=2`, they simplify to

```math
B_iB_j=\binom{i+j}{i}B_{i+j},\qquad
B_h(z)B_h(w)=B_h(z+w),
\tag{1}
```

where `B_n=0` for `n>h`. Equivalently,

```math
P_h(z)P_h(w)=P_h(z+w+1).
\tag{2}
```

In particular, `P_h(z)^2=I`, and `P_h(0)P_h(z)=P_h(z+1)`.
All positive normalized coefficients commute and square to zero.
The coefficient algebra is therefore generated by the coefficients
`B_{2^j}`. Its top coefficient `B_h` annihilates every positive
coefficient. These are all-size identities, not extrapolations from
finite matrices.

Equation (1) also gives an elementary proof of the common row
filtration in [weighted-weyl-row-filtration.md](weighted-weyl-row-filtration.md).
Every coefficient functional applied to `P_h(z)` is `L(1)I` plus
a nilpotent element of the commuting positive-coefficient ideal.
More precisely, every such tensor satisfies `T^2=L(1)^2 I`.
Thus a nonzero value of `L(1)` gives full row rank.

The opposite curve has its own additive normalization at **every**
energy, as proved in Section 5. In particular, its coefficients
commute and `Q_h(z)^2=z^(2h)I`.

Products from a single orientation create no tensors outside its
coefficient span, even at non-power-of-two energies. Products mixing
the two different row orientations are not covered by this statement.
This note does not claim an improved factoring exponent.

## 1. Pair creation and pair annihilation

Use modes of weights `w_j=2^j`, for `0<=j<=floor(log_2 h)`.
Let `A` be the ordinary polynomial algebra in variables `x_j`,
and let `Gamma` be the divided-power algebra in variables `y_j`.
Its basis elements satisfy

```math
y_j^{[a]}y_j^{[b]}=\binom{a+b}{a}y_j^{[a+b]}.
```

On `A tensor Gamma`, define local pair operators

```math
C_{j,k}(x^a y^{[b]})
 =\binom{b+k}{k}x^{a+k}y^{[b+k]},\qquad
D_{j,k}(x^a y^{[b]})
 =\binom{a}{k}x^{a-k}y^{[b-k]}.
\tag{3}
```

The second expression is zero if either resulting occupation is
negative. Let `C(t)` and `D(s)` be the products of these local
generating series, with local parameter respectively `t^{w_j}`
and `s^{w_j}`. Write their coefficients as `C_i,D_i`.
Both families are divided powers in characteristic two:
`C(t)C(v)=C(t+v)` and `D(s)D(v)=D(s+v)`.
This follows first in one mode from the binomial identity, and then
for all modes from `(t+v)^{2^j}=t^{2^j}+v^{2^j}`.

Restrict to the space where both weighted degrees equal `h`.
Then

```math
B_i=C_iD_i.
\tag{4}
```

Indeed, `D_i` removes a multiindex `nu` of weighted energy `i`
from both input occupations, and `C_i` adds a multiindex `kappa`
of the same energy. The coefficient is
`binom(beta,nu) binom(y,kappa)`, and
`nu=beta-alpha+kappa`. Consequently the generating matrix entry is

```math
[\alpha+u=\beta+y]\,[t^\alpha]
 \prod_j(1+t_j)^{\beta_j}(1+z^{w_j}t_j)^{y_j},
```

which is exactly `P_h(z+1)`.

## 2. The Gauss identity and the general multiplication formula

In one mode let `H` act on `x^a y^{[b]}` by the integer `a+b+1`.
The pair operators obey the formal identity

```math
D(s)C(t)
=C\!\left(\frac{t}{1-st}\right)
 (1-st)^{-H}
 D\!\left(\frac{s}{1-st}\right).
```

One elementary verification works over the rationals with
`C(t)=exp(txy)` and `D(s)=exp(s partial_x partial_y)`.
The commutators of pair creation, pair annihilation, and `H`
give the displayed Gauss factorization, or it follows by
differentiating both sides in `s` and checking their value at
`s=0`. In the divided-power basis every displayed coefficient
is integral. Thus the identity also holds after reduction
modulo two. It can alternatively be checked directly on each
`x^a y^{[b]}` by the binomial convolution in the checker.

In several modes, take the product of the local identities.
Characteristic two gives
`1-s^{w_j}t^{w_j}=(1-st)^{w_j}`. Thus the same identity holds,
with `H` acting by the **weighted** sum of the two occupation
degrees plus `W=sum_j w_j`.

On an input of weight `H_0`, coefficient extraction gives

```math
D_iC_j
=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(i,j)}
 \binom{H_0+j-i+k-1}{k}
 C_{j-k}D_{i-k}.
```

The scalar placement matters: in the Gauss product the middle
factor acts after `D_{i-k}`, so its weight is
`H_0-2(i-k)`. In `B_iB_j=C_iD_iC_jD_j`, the rightmost `D_j`
has already lowered the input weight from `H` to `H_0=H-2j`.
The divided-power laws for `C` and `D` therefore yield

```math
\boxed{
B_iB_j=
\sum_{k=0}^{\min(i,j)}
 \binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{j}
 \binom{H-n-1}{k}\,B_n,
\qquad n=i+j-k,\quad H=2h+W.}
\tag{5}
```

Here `B_n=0` for `n>h`. The scalar formula is symmetric in
`i,j`, proving both commutativity and closure of the coefficient
span at every energy. The whole proof also works when modes of
the same dyadic weight occur with multiplicity; only `W` changes.

## 3. The power-of-two simplification

Let `h=2^R>=2` and use all modes through weight `h`. Then
`W=2h-1`, so `H=4h-1`.

Every degree-`h` label has even occupation in the weight-one
mode. In (4), Lucas' identity makes the removal coefficient zero
unless its weight-one occupation is even. Consequently
`B_i=0` for odd `i`.

For even `i,j`, only even `n=i+j-k` can contribute in (5).
Write `i=2I,j=2J,n=2N,k=2K`. For a nonzero term, `n<=h`.
Lucas' identity turns the three scalar factors into the conditions

```math
I\subseteq N,\qquad J\subseteq N,\qquad
\binom{2h-N-1}{K}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\mathbin{\&}N=0.
\tag{6}
```

The last equivalence uses the fact that `2h-1` consists entirely
of one bits and `N,K<=h/2`.
Also `I+J=N+K`. If `I,J` had any common set bit, their least
common set bit would produce the first carry in their sum.
At that bit the sum `I+J` has digit zero, whereas `N+K` has
digit one: the digit lies in `N`, and `K` is disjoint from `N`.
This is a contradiction. Thus `I,J` are disjoint and
`I+J=I union J` is a bit-subset of `N`. The equality
`I+J=N+K` now forces `K=0`.

Therefore every `k>0` contribution in (5) vanishes. The remaining
coefficient is
`binom(i+j,i)^2=binom(i+j,i)` in characteristic two, proving
(1) for even indices. If one index is odd and the other even,
both sides vanish because the resulting index is odd. If both
are odd, the right-hand binomial is even. This proves (1) in
all cases, and (2) follows on replacing `z,w` by `z+1,w+1`.

## 4. Consequences for recombination

The positive coefficients form a nilpotent ideal: they commute,
each squares to zero, and there are finitely many generators.
Over an extension field, the square of any linear combination
of positive coefficients is still zero. Since

```math
P_h(z)=I+\sum_{i>0}(z+1)^i B_i,
```

any coefficient combination has scalar part `L(1)`. This scalar
is intrinsic and equals one on the crossing `P_h(1)=I`.
It recovers the full-row-rank forced summand used in the earlier
coefficient-span obstruction without using highest-weight
classification.

Any product or linear recombination of the same-oriented curves
remains inside this algebra. For example, an arbitrary odd
product of curves is another curve evaluated at the sum of
their parameters, and an arbitrary even product introduces the
corresponding extra constant shift. The general multiplication
formula (5), rather than the additive specialization, is needed
at non-power-of-two energies.

The opposite orientation changes the row product. It is not an
algebra homomorphism for this multiplication, so (1) does not
collapse mixed-orientation words or prove a barrier for their
full parameterized algebra.

## 5. The opposite curve has a reciprocal additive normalization

Let `Q_h(z)` be the crossing-preserving `y,u` relabeling of
`P_h(z)`, read as a row matrix. Its blocks are indexed by
`sigma=alpha+y=beta+u`. Let `C=Q_h,h` denote the top coefficient;
it is the row transpose operator `X mapsto X^T`, so `C^2=I`.
Define the polynomial matrix

```math
G_h(t)=C\,t^h Q_h(1/t).
\tag{7}
```

Then, for every positive energy `h`,

```math
G_h(0)=I,\qquad G_h(1)=C,\qquad
G_h(t)G_h(s)=G_h(t+s).
\tag{8}
```

Consequently the opposite coefficient span is also a commutative
row algebra, and its coefficients `Q_i` satisfy

```math
Q_i^2=0\ (i<h),\qquad Q_h^2=I,\qquad
Q_h(z)^2=z^{2h}I.
\tag{9}
```

Thus an arbitrary opposite-span tensor `T=L(Q_h(z))` obeys
`T^2=L(z^h)^2 I`. The original and opposite span statements use
different scalar functionals.

To prove (8), fix a sum block. It is the weight-zero subspace
of the actual polynomial `SL_2` module

```math
\bigotimes_j \operatorname{Sym}^{\sigma_j}(V)^{(j)}.
```

Its total weighted degree is `2h`. In this module the curve
before projection is the action of

```math
M(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1+z\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
```

The row transpose is the action of
`S=[[0,1],[1,0]]`, which preserves weight zero. The matrix identity

```math
SM(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&z\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
```

gives

```math
CQ_h(z)=\sum_{i=0}^h z^{h-i}K_i,
\qquad K_i=F_iE_i.
\tag{10}
```

Here `E_i,F_i` are the coefficient operators of the upper and
lower unipotent subgroups. The upper operator transfers energy
`i` from the second coordinate to the first. The diagonal
matrix therefore contributes `z^{h-i}`, and the lower operator
must transfer energy `i` back to reach weight zero. This proves
that the coefficients of `G_h(t)` are exactly `K_i`.

For precision, the ordinary `SL_2` Gauss identity is

```math
U(s)L(t)=
 L\!\left(\frac{t}{1+st}\right)
 \operatorname{diag}(1+st,(1+st)^{-1})
 U\!\left(\frac{s}{1+st}\right).
```

On a vector of torus weight `H_0`, its coefficient formula is

```math
E_iF_j=
 \sum_{k=0}^{\min(i,j)}
 \binom{H_0+i-j}{k}F_{j-k}E_{i-k}.
```

In `K_iK_j=F_iE_iF_jE_j`, the rightmost `E_j` starts at weight
zero and reaches weight `2j`. Hence

```math
K_iK_j=
\sum_{k=0}^{\min(i,j)}
 \binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{j}\binom{i+j}{k}K_n,
\qquad n=i+j-k.
\tag{11}
```

The first two binomials can be odd only when `i,j` are bit-subsets
of `n`. Since `i+j=n+k`, the last binomial is odd exactly when
`n` and `k` have disjoint binary supports. The same first-carry
argument used in Section 3 forces `k=0`. No halving of indices
or power-of-two assumption is needed here. Thus

```math
K_iK_j=\binom{i+j}{i}K_{i+j},
```

which proves (8). Also `G_h(1)=C Q_h(1)=C`. Thus `C` commutes
with every `K_i`, and `Q_i=C K_{h-i}`, proving (9).

One equivalent mixed-parameter identity within this orientation is

```math
Q_h(z)Q_h(w)
=C(z+w)^h Q_h\!\left(\frac{zw}{z+w}\right).
\tag{12}
```

This is interpreted as a polynomial identity: after multiplying
out, all denominators cancel because `deg Q_h<=h`. It remains
valid at `z=w`; then only the top coefficient survives and gives
`z^{2h}I`. Equations (1) and (8) therefore reduce each consecutive
run of one orientation in a mixed word. They give no relation
between the two distinct coefficient algebras.

## Verification

```sh
python3 tools/check_weighted_weyl_additive.py --output evidence/weighted-weyl-additive-row-law.json
```

The checker verifies the local integral Gauss coefficient
identity, the general coefficient multiplication law on complete
row blocks, the binary no-carry argument, and the additive law at
power-of-two energies. Energy six supplies a negative control:
the general multiplication law holds there, while the additive
law fails. The proof above supplies the all-size statement.
The checker also verifies the integral ordinary `SL_2` Gauss
coefficients and the opposite additive normalization on complete
sum blocks, including non-power-of-two energies.
