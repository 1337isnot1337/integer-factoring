# A strict module-certificate improvement for an actual crossing

## Result and scope

An explicit binary crossing has a finite module certificate

\[
B_{\rm module}=\frac{1625}{108},
\]

while its corresponding capped min-plus word certificate has rate product
at least \(425/27\). The comparison uses the same Schur factors, auxiliary
basis, precision caps, and evaluation point. It is a strict improvement
for this fixed certificate, beyond the auxiliary-only nilpotent control
in [the module theorem](cancellation-module-smith-automata.md).

This does not improve the factoring exponent. The elementary scalar
crossing already has the smaller certificate \(256/27\), and none of
these numbers passes the half-rate threshold. The result demonstrates
that the module refinement can help on a complete crossing family.
It is not an optimality claim over other channel bases, gauges, or
decompositions of this family.

## 1. The complete local crossing

The four physical labels are bits. Write

\[
I(x,y,u,v)=[x=u][y=v],\quad
E(x,y,u,v)=[x=v][y=u],\quad
P(z)=I+z(E+I).
\]

Let \(E_{01},E_{10}\) be the two auxiliary matrix units in dimension two.
Over \(\mathbb F_2\), define

\[
K(z;x,y,u,v)
=P(z;x,y,u,v)I_2+
 z(1+z)\bigl([v=0]E_{01}+[x=y=1,\ u=v=0]E_{10}\bigr).        \tag{1}
\]

Both correction terms have rank one across the physical row cut.
The complete matrix endpoint is \(K(1)=E I_2\). Therefore

\[
e_0^T K(z;x_1,y_1,u_1,v_1)\cdots
       K(z;x_t,y_t,u_t,v_t)e_0
 =\sum_{k=0}^{2t}z^k A_{t,k}                                \tag{2}
\]

is a polynomial family whose coefficients sum to the crossing on
\(2^t\) labels. No cyclic trace or free boundary is substituted in (2).

Use the row cut \((x,y)\mid(u,v)\) at infinity and the column cut
\((x,u)\mid(y,v)\) at zero. Put both auxiliary indices on the second
physical side in both local flattenings. Exact raw Schur factorization
gives exponent lists

\[
e_\infty=(2,2,1,-1),\qquad e_0=(0,-1,-1,-2).                 \tag{3}
\]

The checker reconstructs each entire rational matrix from its factors.
It does not infer these exponents solely from finite coefficient ranks.

## 2. Two exact module transfer matrices

Use precision two at infinity, precision one at zero, and \(s=3/2\).
The infinity automaton has the two modules generated by \(e_0^T\) and
\(e_1^T\) modulo \(\tau^2\). Their full lifts include \(\tau^2O^2\).
Its transfer matrix and terminal vector are

\[
T_\infty=
\begin{pmatrix}
19/6&9/4\\
9/4&19/6
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
r_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}1\\4/9\end{pmatrix}.                 \tag{4}
\]

The zero automaton has two states: the module generated by \(e_0^T\)
modulo \(\tau\), and the full module modulo \(\tau\). Its matrix is

\[
T_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
19/9&2/3\\
0&25/9
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
r_0=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}.                         \tag{5}
\]

Both initial states are the first displayed state. Row sums give exact
positive potential certificates with the all-ones vector:

\[
\beta_\infty=\frac{65}{12},\qquad
\beta_0=\frac{25}{9},\qquad
B_{\rm module}=\beta_\infty\beta_0=\frac{1625}{108}.           \tag{6}
\]

Both terminal vectors are entrywise at most one. The complete
coefficient-splitting argument from the module theorem now gives, at
every length \(t\) and coefficient \(0\le k\le2t\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
r(A_{t,k})&\le(2t-k+1)(3/2)^{-k}(65/12)^t,\\
c(A_{t,k})&\le(k+1)(3/2)^k(25/9)^t,\\
r(A_{t,k})c(A_{t,k})&\le(2t+1)^2(1625/108)^t.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{7}
\]

There are \(2t+1\) complete pieces, so their total square-root rank
sum is at most \((2t+1)^2(1625/108)^{t/2}\).
This is an all-length upper bound proved by the local identities and
positive rational potentials. The finite rank tests only check its
implementation.

## 3. The capped min-plus rate is strictly larger

For the same infinity factors and cap two, the normalized cost states,
in the order \((2,0),(0,0),(0,2)\), give

\[
W_\infty=
\begin{pmatrix}
13/6&1&9/4\\
9/4&13/6&9/4\\
9/4&1&13/6
\end{pmatrix}.                                               \tag{8}
\]

For \(h=(4/5,1,4/5)^T\),

\[
W_\infty h\ge\frac{17}{3}h,
\qquad \frac{17}{3}>\frac{65}{12}.                           \tag{9}
\]

Every entry of \(W_\infty\) is positive, and the initial state and
terminal vector have nonzero positive weights. Iterating (9) therefore
gives an exponential word-count rate at least \(17/3\). This is a lower
bound on this specified counting certificate, not on actual tensor ranks.

The opposite capped min-plus automaton is exactly (5), with positive
terminal vector one. Its row sums are \(25/9\), so its word-count rate
is exactly \(25/9\). Thus this pair of capped min-plus certificates has

\[
B_{\rm min-plus}\ge\frac{17}{3}\frac{25}{9}
=\frac{425}{27}
>\frac{1625}{108}=B_{\rm module}.                            \tag{10}
\]

In particular the gain is not merely a better numerical approximation
to the same Perron root. A rational lower potential for the old matrix
is separated from a rational upper potential for the new one.

This example compares finite capped procedures. The module method also
retains exact matrix relations when it adds and renormalizes its full
tail; the old cap may introduce paths through absent entries. No claim
is made that every part of this particular improvement is caused by
cancellation between nonzero paths. The separate dense nilpotent example
in the general theorem verifies that capability directly.

## 4. Reproduction

Run:

    python3 tools/check_cancellation_module_crossing.py \
      --output evidence/cancellation-module-crossing.json

The standard-library checker constructs (1), verifies its exact local
endpoint, reconstructs both rational Schur factorizations, and computes
the module and capped min-plus transitions. It verifies (4)--(10) with
rational arithmetic. Through length three it also compares complete
coefficient ranks with (7); literal rational channel products through
length two independently check the word-order bounds.

The recorded output is
[cancellation-module-crossing.json](../evidence/cancellation-module-crossing.json).
The implementation is
[check_cancellation_module_crossing.py](../tools/check_cancellation_module_crossing.py).

The crossed endpoints, Smith coefficient interface, and elementary
scalar family follow PR #30 as credited in the
[module theorem](cancellation-module-smith-automata.md). The coupled
auxiliary example and its exact comparison used substantial OpenAI
assistant support. The strongest complete factoring exponent remains
one half.
