# Steiner quasigroups give determinant-one cubic crossings

A swap is a product of three controlled involutions for every Steiner
quasigroup, without a coordinate-register assumption. Interpolating those
involutions gives a determinant-one cubic crossing with a rank-one starting
cut. Both bare Smith profiles can nevertheless be computed exactly,
independently of the Steiner design. They force `rho>=3/4` at every size.
This is an ordinary construction and obstruction, with exact finite
controls; the factoring exponent remains `1/2`.

## 1. Controlled involutions

Let `*` be a Steiner quasigroup on `q>1` letters:

```math
x*x=x,\qquad x*y=y*x,\qquad (x*y)*y=x.                    \tag{1}
```

Equivalently, distinct letters determine a unique third letter in a
Steiner triple system. Thus `q` is odd and `q=1 or 3 (mod 6)`.
Define permutations of ordered pairs by

```math
L(x,y)=(x*y,y),\qquad R(x,y)=(x,x*y).
```

They satisfy `L^2=R^2=I` and `LRL=RLR=S`, where `S(x,y)=(y,x)`.
Over a field of characteristic two, `A=L+I` and `B=R+I` have square zero.
Consequently

```math
K(t)=(I+tA)(I+tB)(I+tA)                                  \tag{2}
```

has determinant one, `K(0)=I`, and `K(1)=S`. Its entries are the physical
crossing tensor in the first cut `(x,y)|(u,v)`. In the second cut
`(x,u)|(y,v)`, its value at zero has rank one. Expansion gives

```math
K(t)=(1+t)I+(t+t^3)R+(t^2+t^3)(LR+RL)+t^3S.              \tag{3}
```

Each fixed design therefore supplies an explicit finite-field polynomial
crossing of degree three. The ordinary
[polynomial transfer](polynomial-crossing-interpolation.md) has `3m+1`
coefficient positions at tensor length `m` and the existing `2^O(lambda)`
construction cost. The endpoint does not require a search or a conjecture.

## 2. First-cut infinity profile

There are `q` fixed diagonal pairs. Each Steiner triple contributes the
six ordered distinct pairs on its letters, a regular orbit of the group
generated by `L,R`, isomorphic to `S_3`. Put

```math
T=\frac{q(q-1)}6.
```

The characteristic-two group algebra on one regular orbit splits as
`F[C_2] direct-sum M_2(F)`. For completeness, writing `P=LR`, its central
idempotents are `e_0=I+P+P^2` and `e_1=P+P^2`. The first summand has dimension
two and makes `L=R`. In the second summand, send `L,R` respectively to

```math
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
```

These generate `M_2(F)`, giving the four-dimensional second summand.
Its left regular module consists of two copies of the natural module.
On the first summand, (2) is a single shear `I+tN`, `N^2=0`, with infinity
orders `(-1,1)`. On each natural module it is

```math
\begin{pmatrix}1+t^2&t^3\\t&1+t^2\end{pmatrix},           \tag{4}
```

whose determinant is one and largest entry degree is three. Its infinity
orders are `(-3,3)`. Thus the complete raw infinity profile is

```math
(-3)^{2T},\quad(-1)^T,\quad0^q,\quad1^T,\quad3^{2T}.    \tag{5}
```

All these calculations remain valid over any characteristic-two extension.

## 3. Second-cut zero profile

Separate diagonal physical pairs from off-diagonal pairs in both axes of
the second cut. The realignments of `LR,RL,S` preserve these parts; each
off-diagonal Steiner triple gives a six-dimensional block. The realignment
of `R` has rows only in the diagonal part. Formula (3) therefore becomes

```math
C(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
 tI+(1+t)J&t(1+t^2)H\\
 0&t^2\bigl((1+t)(P+P^{-1})+tS\bigr)
\end{pmatrix},                                          \tag{6}
```

where `J` is the `q` by `q` all-ones matrix, each off-diagonal block has
`P^3=S^2=I, SPS=P^-1`, and `H` records the third letter of each ordered
distinct pair. The exact entries of `H` will not affect the orders.

Since `q` is odd, `J^2=J`. The upper-left block has inverse
`J+t^-1(I+J)`. Its product with the upper-right block is polynomial:

```math
(1+t^2)(tJ+I+J)H.
```

A polynomial column operation thus removes that coupling. The upper-left
block has one unit channel and `q-1` channels of order one.

In a six-dimensional lower block, `P+P^-1` is an idempotent of rank four,
commuting with `S`. On its image the inner matrix in (6) is
`I+t(I+S)`, with square-zero `I+S`, hence determinant one. On its
two-dimensional kernel the inner matrix is `tS`. Multiplication by `t^2`
gives four channels of order two and two of order three. The complete
zero profile is therefore

```math
0^1,\quad1^{q-1},\quad2^{4T},\quad3^{2T}.                \tag{7}
```

This proves design independence; the result does not rely on a sample of
isomorphism classes or on the binary rank of a Steiner incidence matrix.

## 4. The bare criterion is excluded at every size

Equations (5)--(7) give the exact partitions

```math
U(s)=2T(s^3+s^{-3})+T(s+s^{-1})+q,\qquad
V(s)=1+(q-1)s^{-1}+4Ts^{-2}+2Ts^{-3}.                    \tag{8}
```

For `q>=7` and `s>=1`, retaining only positive terms gives

```math
U(s)V(s)\ge 2Ts^3(4Ts^{-2}+2Ts^{-3})
 =8T^2s+4T^2\ge12T^2
 =\frac{q^2(q-1)^2}{3}\ge q^3.                          \tag{9}
```

The only smaller nontrivial admissible alphabet is `q=3`. There, multiplying
`U(s)V(s)-27` by `s^6` and setting `s=1+h` yields the polynomial with
coefficients, from constant upward,

```text
54, 180, 351, 533, 648, 568, 325, 113, 22, 2.
```

All are positive, proving the same bound for `h>=0`. Thus every instance
of (2), at every real scale `s>=1`, has bare parameter `rho>=3/4`.
Physical gauges, paired types, mixed-place transfers, other polynomial
paths in the permutation algebra, and collective coefficient-span
decompositions are outside this statement.

## 5. Exact replay and next question

[The checker](../tools/check_steiner_cubic_crossings.py) independently
enumerates all 923 nonempty minors of one six-dimensional orbit. Their
maximum degrees are `3,6,7,6,3,0`, giving (5) again. It also checks 44
full-cut Toeplitz ranks with both elimination orders, and 59,668 endpoint
and block entries at `q=3,7,9,15`. These are controls of the uniform proof,
not an enumeration of all Steiner systems.
[The evidence](../evidence/steiner-cubic-crossings.json) records the counts,
profiles, exact small-alphabet polynomial, and source hash.

```sh
make verify-steiner-cubic
make verify
```

The construction changes the local algebra but leaves only a six-dimensional
permutation span. A useful next question is whether tensor-power coefficient
recombinations or restricted physical types can exploit its two-dimensional
nilpotent summand while retaining the required standard-matrix endpoint.
The all-size calculation above closes the bare cubic search; it does not
settle those alternatives. No new Lean work was used.
