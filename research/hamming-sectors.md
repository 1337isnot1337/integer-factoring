# Crossing sectors on several antipodal pairs

## Results and scope

The [bounded-bit-weight deformation](hamming-pfaffian.md) extends to labels
consisting of any fixed number of antipodal mode pairs. Its crossing sum
still has scale one. For each fixed pair count, its degree is linear in
the binary label width, so complete symmetry-span optimization and all
factor generation still cost `2^O(lambda)`.

The deformation also has an exact reflected-complement identity. Its
scalar is more general than the three-term scalar of the original source
profiles. This gives an exact division rule for filled modes.

A general coefficient-span lemma strengthens both this identity and the
earlier [particle-hole identity](modular-particle-hole.md): after relabeling
by reflected complement, **the full coefficient symmetry spans and their
optimized rank-product cutoffs are identical**. This concerns complete
linear spans; it does not assert monotonicity for individual coefficients.

The separate higher-sector examples below do not improve the existing all-one
families. A joint one- and two-pair span does: at binary width two it has a
six-piece crossing witness of maximum rank product 25, below the one-pair
full-span optimum 36. This remains a finite gain.
No uniform leading rank bound below the half threshold is proved. All
statements are ordinary mathematics, with substantial OpenAI assistant
support and no new Lean formalization. The underlying folded Pfaffian
and its original source attribution remain as given in the linked notes.

## 1. Labels and crossing normalization

Let `D=2^ell`, `H=4D`, and let `t` be a positive integer with `t<=D`.
The basic antipodal pair indexed by `a<D` is

```math
mu(a)={2a+1,H-2a-1}.
```

A label is a `t`-subset `S` of these pairs, represented by the union
`L(S)=union_(a in S) mu(a)`. There are `binom(D,t)` such labels, each
containing `2t` distinct odd modes. Use the same six weighted folded
blocks as before, with `2t` vertices per block instead of two. The matrix
has `8t` vertices.

At `z=1`, only `AB` and `UY` survive. The `UY` determinant is equality of
the mode sets, hence equality of the corresponding pair subsets. To
calculate `AB`, list the first member of each pair before the second
members. After replacing `2a+1` by its non-low bit string, the first
members have leading bit zero and the second members have leading bit
one. The lower-left block vanishes. The diagonal blocks have determinants

```math
det Z(S,T),\qquad det Z(T,S),\qquad
Z(S,T)_{a,b}=[a subset b].
```

If the first determinant is nonzero, some matching of `S` to `T` uses
only subset comparisons. Consequently `sum_(a in S) popcount(a)` is at
most the corresponding sum for `T`. The reverse determinant gives the
opposite inequality. Equality of the sums forces every subset comparison
in the first matching to be equality. Thus `S=T`. Conversely, when
`S=T`, ordering by popcount makes `Z(S,S)` unitriangular. Therefore

```math
det AB(S,T)=[S=T].
```

The Pfaffian at one is the product of the two block determinants, giving

```math
P(x,y,u,v;1)=[x=v][y=u].
\tag{1}
```

No homogeneous source-operator identity is assumed for these deformed
entries. The argument is directly about the displayed polynomial matrix.

## 2. Uniform binary families and their full cost

For any target binary label width `lambda>=1`, choose the least `ell>=1`
with `binom(2^ell,t)>=2^lambda`, and take a fixed lexicographic prefix of
`2^lambda` pair subsets. For fixed `t`,

```math
ell=lambda/t+O_t(1).
```

For example, `binom(n,t)>=(n/t)^t` for `n>=t` supplies the needed upper
bound; `binom(n,t)<=n^t` supplies the lower one. The finite initial cases
are absorbed in the fixed constant. Labels are generated by combinations
of explicit integers with `O_t(lambda)` bits.

For weights `w_0=1` and `1<=w_j<=B` in the `ell+1` non-low positions,
every pair has weighted size

```math
g=2+sum_(j=1)^(ell+1) w_j.
```

A `t`-pair label has size `tg`. The endpoint-size bound from the original
deformation gives Pfaffian degree at most `4tg=O_(t,B)(lambda)`. Since
`8t` is fixed, each profile entry takes polynomial time in `lambda`.
Enumerating all coefficient tensors costs `2^O_(t,B)(lambda)`.
Their eight-symmetry span has dimension `O_(t,B)(lambda)`. Enumerating
that entire span, calculating exact ranks, selecting an optimum crossing
decomposition, and generating both rank factorizations have the same
`2^O_(t,B)(lambda)` total cost. At most `O_(t,B)(lambda)` pieces are needed.

One may select the best sector among any fixed finite set of pair counts,
or optimize their joint coefficient span: its dimension is still linear
in `lambda`. The constants then depend on the largest allowed pair count.
Allowing that count to grow without a new cost proof is outside this
statement.

The full combinatorial sector is preserved by bit permutations. A fixed
binary prefix need not map to itself under those permutations. The
one-pair multiset reduction must therefore not be applied to a multi-pair
prefix without tracking its relabeled subset.

## 3. The weighted full-core inverse

Let a complete mode core have size `K=2^a`, namely
`F={1,3,...,2K-1}`, with positive weights `w_0=1,w_1,...,w_a`.
Define

```math
T(z)=z^{2+sum_(j=1)^a w_j},\qquad
Q(z)=(1+z^2)prod_(j=1)^a(1+z^{w_j}),\qquad
D_w(z)=TQ+Q+T.
\tag{2}
```

The complete four-block weighted matrix `M` satisfies

```math
(M Rbar)^2=D_w I,\qquad
M^{-1}=D_w^{-1} Rbar M Rbar,\qquad
Pf(M)=D_w^K.
\tag{3}
```

Here `Rbar` reflects the mode order inside each of the four blocks.
To verify (3), repeat the six two-by-two digit identities in the earlier
particle-hole proof. They are polynomial identities in an arbitrary
digit variable `b`, so at digit `j` substitute `b=z^{w_j}`. All
off-diagonal blocks still cancel.

For the diagonal blocks, put
`U=prod_j z^{w_j}` and `V=prod_j(1+z^{w_j})`. Their three contributions
are `z^2(1+z^2)UV`, `(1+z^2)V`, and `z^2U`, in some order. These are
precisely `TQ`, `Q`, and `T`. The original proof's final Frobenius
simplification is unnecessary: retain their sum as `D_w`. Taking
determinants and using injectivity of squaring in `F_2[z]` gives the
Pfaffian formula, just as in that proof.

The polynomial `D_w` has constant coefficient one, degree
`2(2+sum_j w_j)`, and `D_w(1)=1`. Thus it is nonzero. In the original
bit grading `w_j=2^j`, `Q=1+T` and it reduces to the old three-term
scalar. In the all-one grading, `T=z^(a+2)` and `Q=(1+z)^(a+2)`;
usually it has more than three terms.

## 4. Reflected complement and coefficient-span invariance

For labels of cardinality `r` in the core, define
`C(L)=F minus {2K-n:n in L}`. The complementary-Pfaffian minor formula
and (3) give the polynomial identity

```math
P_{C(x),C(y),C(u),C(v)}(z)=D_w(z)^{K-2r}P_{x,y,u,v}(z).
\tag{4}
```

If the exponent is negative, the equivalent identity after multiplication
asserts exact divisibility by the corresponding positive power. There is
no inversion in an input coefficient algebra. Division is performed
exactly in `F_2[z]` before evaluating pieces. Because `D_w(1)=1`, removing
the common factor preserves the scale-one crossing sum.

For the `t`-pair sector, `K=2D`, `r=2t`, and reflection swaps the two modes
inside every pair. Hence `C(L(S))=L(S^c)`, and (4) identifies the `t` and
`D-t` sectors with exponent `2D-4t`.

**Coefficient-span lemma.** Let `V` be any vector space over a field,
let `F(z)` be a nonzero scalar polynomial, and let `P(z)=F(z)Q(z)` with
`Q` a `V`-valued polynomial. Then the coefficients of `P` and `Q` span
exactly the same subspace of `V`.

One inclusion follows from convolution. For the other, remove the lowest
power of `z` from `F` and divide by its nonzero constant coefficient.
Now `F(0)=1`. Recursively,

```math
Q_n=P_n-sum_(j>=1) F_j Q_(n-j).
```

Induction expresses every `Q_n` as a linear combination of coefficients
of `P`. This proves the other inclusion. All steps are finite.

Coordinate symmetries commute with scalar multiplication. Apply the
lemma to the polynomial with values in the direct sum of the eight
symmetry images. Equivalently, apply its coefficient expressions to
each image separately. The complete coefficient symmetry spans are
identical. Reflected complement is a bijective label map, so it preserves
both matrix ranks. Their optimized maximum rank product, and all cutoff
span-containment tests, are therefore exactly equal.

This conclusion also applies to the earlier source-profile particle-hole
identity, which uses another nonzero scalar polynomial. Individual raw
coefficient ranks can change dramatically under the convolution; no
coefficientwise rank monotonicity follows. High-occupancy sectors give no
new full-span optimum once their complementary family is available.

## 5. Fixed extension fields are compatible with the search target

The exact optimizer may also enumerate combinations over a fixed finite
extension `F_(p^k)` instead of only `F_p`; its cost remains `2^O(lambda)`.
Such a family can be descended to the prime field with only a constant
width penalty. Choose an `F_p`-linear functional `L:F_(p^k)->F_p` with
`L(1)=1` and replace each tensor `A_i` by `L(A_i)`. Their sum remains
the crossing. Expanding multiplication in a fixed `k`-element field basis
turns each rank-`r` factorization into a prime-field factorization of rank
at most `kr`. The same holds in the other arrangement. Thus each width
increases by at most `ceil(log2 k)`, before the separate trivial caps.
Factor values and indices retain the required uniform cost.

Use a coordinate projection with `L(1)=1`; the ordinary field trace need
not have that value when the characteristic divides `k`. This explains
why fixed extension fields are a legitimate asymptotic search direction,
without claiming that a finite extension search has passed the target.

## 6. Finite evidence

```sh
python3 tools/check_hamming_sectors.py
python3 tools/check_hamming_sectors.py --full --output evidence/hamming-sectors.json
python3 tools/check_hamming_complement.py
python3 tools/check_hamming_complement.py --full --output evidence/hamming-complement.json
make verify
```

The sector checker verifies the subset-Gram normalization independently,
the binary label-selection schedule, complete crossing polynomials,
coefficient ranks, and selected pair-recombination witnesses. It rechecks
that the one-pair specialization is exactly the previous implementation.
The full run includes 65,536 complete profiles on a 16-label two-pair
prefix. Its raw maximum rank product is 65,280; this is not a saving.
The small two-pair prefix has a pair-candidate witness of cutoff 63,
while the three-pair four-label example has cutoff 36. These are scoped
candidate-list witnesses, not claims of an asymptotic improvement or of
global rank optimality.

The complement checker verifies complete inverse identities with both
constant and nonconstant weights, full-core Pfaffians in small cases,
exact division of complete profiles, coefficient-span equality, and an
omitted-factor negative control. Each evidence file records the actual
counts, parameters, and source hashes. The all-size theorems rely on the
proofs above, not on extrapolating those finite checks.

## Joint-sector finite gain

The one-pair and two-pair binary families at width two have joint symmetry
span dimension 33. Six explicitly stored tensors sum to the crossing and
have rank pairs

```text
(16,1), (1,16), (4,4), (4,4), (3,4), (5,5).
```

Their maximum product is 25. Each tensor uses at most four original
coefficient symmetry images, and the witness uses only 14 such images in
total. The [evidence](../evidence/hamming-joint.json) records every sector,
coefficient degree, coordinate permutation, and both ranks.
`python3 tools/check_hamming_joint.py` reconstructs them from the polynomial
profiles, verifies ranks by independent dense elimination, and checks all
256 crossing entries. No heuristic-search program is needed for replay.

The maximum rounded binary width sum is still six, as for the earlier
rank-product cutoff 36. This finite rank improvement is therefore not a
new channel-width rate. The [separating-functional and RUP certificate](hamming-joint-optimum.md)
now proves 25 optimal in this complete 33-dimensional `F_2` span. It supplies
no extension-field lower bound. The all-size joint-span optimizer is effective
by Section 2; its uniform leading rank bound remains open.
