# A cubic parameter for phase seven and an odd-index theta candidate

The [Tate-coordinate construction](tate-coordinate-coefficients.md) at phase
seven has two coordinates after the cheap scalar trace. This note gives an
exact cubic parameter and identifies its original-nome series with a
seven-regular partition generating function. It also records a degree-four
candidate for the actual odd-index coefficients, checked through index
131,072. The proposed modular-form proof of that candidate is unfinished at
this checkpoint. No faster unrestricted factoring bound follows.

The algebraic identities use standard division polynomials, Hensel lifting
and the genus-zero modular curve X_0(7). The finite theta search and its
separating certificate were developed with OpenAI Codex assistance. No new
Lean theorem or independent proof review is claimed.

## 1. The exact cubic parameter

Work in A_7=F_8=F_2[c]/(c^3+c+1). Put

```math
D=1+u+u^2,\qquad
K(Y,u)=Y^3+uY^2+(1+u)Y+1,
\qquad x=Y/D,\quad v=u/D^4.                       \tag{1}
```

Let psi_7(x,v) be the division polynomial on E_v:y^2+xy=x^3+v,
with the normalization of the preceding compiler. There is an exact
polynomial identity

```math
D^{24}\psi_7(Y/D,u/D^4)=K(Y,u)Q(Y,u).             \tag{2}
```

The checker multiplies and divides binary polynomials without truncation.
The left side has 49 nonzero monomials; Q has Y-degree 21 and 77 monomials.
The [receipt](../evidence/q7-cubic-parameter.json) lists every coefficient
of Q as a binary polynomial mask in u, and the checker multiplies back.

The series v=u/D^4 has linear term u. Thus it has a unique inverse u(v)
with zero constant. At u=0, K(Y,0)=Y^3+Y+1, and its derivative at c is
c^2+1, a unit. Consequently there is a unique Y(u) with constant c.
Equation (2), followed by uniqueness of the nonsingular psi_7 branch,
identifies Y(u(v))/D(u(v)) with X_7(v).

The three roots of K are obtained from Y by the order-three transformation
T(Y)=1/(1+Y). Its action on c is c to c^4, with T^2(c)=c^2.
Equivalently, the three coefficient-field conjugates of Y are its roots.
Their sum is u. Therefore the scalar trace B of x is

```math
B=u/(1+u+u^2).                                    \tag{3}
```

For a second exact description, adjoin w with w^2+w+1=0. Expanding gives

```math
(u+w^2)(Y+w)^3+(u+w)(Y+w^2)^3=K(Y,u).
```

Hence r=(Y+w)/(Y+w^2) satisfies

```math
r^3=(u+w)/(u+w^2).                                \tag{4}
```

The checker verifies the cleared identity over F_4. This is an odd cubic
extension, so the order-five Artin--Schreier calculation does not transfer
directly.

## 2. The original parameter is a partition series

Write E(t)=product over n>=1 of (1-t^n). Kilford--McMurdy,
[section 2, equation (1)](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157012000095),
give the X_0(7) parameter s=(eta(t)/eta(t^7))^4 and

```math
j=(s^2+13s+49)(s^2+245s+2401)^3/s^7.
```

Reduce this integral identity modulo two and set u=1/s. Since j=1/v on
our normalized curve, it becomes v=u/(1+u+u^2)^4. Its zero-constant
inverse is unique, so at the Tate nome the parameter in (1) is

```math
u(t)=t\left(\frac{E(t^7)}{E(t)}\right)^4
    =t B_7(t^4),\qquad
B_7(t)=\frac{E(t^7)}{E(t)}.                         \tag{5}
```

Here B_7 counts partitions whose parts are not divisible by seven.
In particular, the coefficient of u at 4n+1 is the parity of b_7(n), and
all its other coefficients vanish. This identifies the series; it supplies
no fast high-index partition-parity oracle.

The finite control computes 2,049 partition parities by the ordinary
unbounded-part dynamic program, charging 1,799,314 scalar XOR updates. The
result agrees with u=Delta(t)(1+u^4+u^8), the cubic equation after multiplying
X_7(Delta(t)) by D, and the scalar trace, through index 8,193. The X prefix
comes independently from a literal odd-divisor Lambert sieve.

## 3. One scalar square series does not rationally parametrize the full X

Set

```math
V_7(t)=\sum_{n>0}\bigl(t^{n^2}+t^{7n^2}\bigr).
```

The proved trace formula gives B=V_7+V_7^2. The full series X is not a
rational function of V_7 over F_8, or over an extension of the constants.
Indeed, u has valuation one and is transcendental over the constants.
Equation K is irreducible over the rational function field in u: solving
for u gives

```math
u=(Y^3+Y+1)/(Y^2+Y),
```

whose coprime numerator and denominator define a degree-three rational
map. The numerator is nonzero at both roots 0,1 of the denominator.
Therefore X=Y/D has degree three over the field generated by u. But V_7
satisfies V_7^2+V_7=B in that field; its adjoining extension has degree at
most two. X cannot belong to it.

This excludes a particular rational representation of the full series.
It says nothing about the complexity of individual odd-index coefficients.

## 4. A bounded full-series exclusion, and a successful odd-index candidate

For scale e in {1,7} and a in {0,1,2,3}, define

```math
\Theta_{e,a}(t)=
\sum_{\substack{n>0\\\min(n\bmod7,-n\bmod7)=a}}t^{en^2}.
```

There are 3,003 monomials of total degree at most six in these eight
series, including the constant. Their coefficient vectors through 4,096
have exact binary rank 2,108. The receipt gives a 116-entry covector,
supported on indices at most 1,595, which annihilates every monomial and
pairs to c+c^2 with X. This is a finite exact certificate excluding every
full-series polynomial in that bounded ansatz, even with extension-field
coefficients. It excludes neither larger degree nor other series.

The desired factoring coefficients have odd indices. The preceding
covector uses even indices, and its exclusion does not apply to that task.
Restricting the fit to odd indices produces a degree-four candidate.
Put a=Theta_(1,1), b=Theta_(1,2), d=Theta_(1,3), and define

```math
P_1=a+ab+ad+a^3+a^2d+ab^2+abd+b^3
       +a^3b+a^3d+ab^2d+abd^2+bd^3,
```

```math
P_2=d+ad+bd+a^3+a^2d+abd+bd^2+d^3
       +a^2bd+ab^3+ab^2d+ad^3+bd^3.                \tag{6}
```

Writing O for the projection onto odd exponents, the candidate is

```math
O(X)=(1+c+c^2)O(V_7)+cO(P_1)+c^2O(P_2).           \tag{7}
```

The fit used indices through 4,096. An independent larger prefix confirms
all three coordinates at 65,536 odd indices through 131,072. The known
phase-five degree-three identity is a positive calibration at every
coefficient through 16,384. These finite checks do not by themselves prove
(7) at all indices.

A possible proof uses weight-two integral modular lifts. Homogenize each
monomial of (6) to degree four by the full theta series
h=sum over n in Z of t^(n^2), whose reduction modulo two is 1. The resulting
products are rank-four lattice-coset theta series. Torsion-coordinate
differences have weight-two elliptic lifts, and the scalar odd-square trace
has an Eisenstein lift. The subgroup, integrality and congruence bound must
all be justified before applying Sturm. This proof is the next checkpoint.

## 5. Scope and replay

Run `make verify-q7-parameter`. The checker emits the versioned receipt,
including source hashes, the full polynomial quotient, the separating
covector and the odd-index candidate. Its finite prefix providers enumerate
divisors or partitions and are explicitly separate from a fast coefficient
engine. No stronger complete factoring exponent follows from this note.

Even if (7) is proved, direct counting for its degree-four products would
still be exponential in the input bit length. It would give a concrete
nonlinear representation for another phase, and would leave the uniform
phase family and a sufficiently fast evaluator unresolved.
