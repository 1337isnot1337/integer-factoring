# A complete finite norm sieve and an exact arithmetic factoring prototype

The short-norm route now has a concrete search implementation. Given a
primitive positive binary quadratic form, a smoothness cutoff, a norm
cap, and a prime-multiplicity budget, it either returns an admissible
primitive represented norm or exhausts every such norm below the cap.
The returned vector is converted into a signed prime-form word by the
[direct converter](smooth-norm-short-words.md).

An exact prototype uses these searched words to build a strictly
diagonally dominant relation matrix. Its determinant supplies an
annihilator, and the [polynomial ambiguous extractor](polynomial-ambiguous-extraction.md)
returns a proper factor. No class-group enumeration or search for
generator orders occurs in this prototype.

The finite runs include a 92-bit input. Their purpose is to make the
remaining search problem executable and inspectable. They do not bound
the worst-case search cost or improve the complete factoring exponent.

## 1. Enumerate exactly the represented-norm ellipse

Write

$$Q(x,y)=Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2,\qquad D=4AC-B^2>0.$$

The identity

$$4A Q(x,y)=(2Ax+By)^2+Dy^2\tag{1}$$

gives exact integer bounds for all vectors satisfying `Q(x,y)<=H`.
Identify `(x,y)` with `(-x,-y)` and take `y>=0`. For `y=0`, only
`x=1` can be primitive modulo this identification. For positive `y`,
set

$$
0<y\le\left\lfloor\sqrt{4AH/D}\right\rfloor,\qquad
r_y=\left\lfloor\sqrt{4AH-Dy^2}\right\rfloor.
$$

The allowed integers in that row are exactly

$$
-\left\lfloor\frac{r_y+By}{2A}\right\rfloor
\le x\le
\left\lfloor\frac{r_y-By}{2A}\right\rfloor.\tag{2}
$$

All square roots in the implementation are integer square roots. Each
row is generated by the finite differences

$$
Q(x+1,y)-Q(x,y)=A(2x+1)+By,\qquad
\Delta_x^2 Q=2A.
$$

Discard vectors with `gcd(x,y)>1` and norms with `gcd(Q(x,y),D)>1`.
The remaining candidates have exactly the primitiveness and
coprimality required by the direct prime-word converter.

## 2. Sieve the row using its modular roots

Precompute the unramified split primes `p<=Y` and their square roots
with the [deterministic root constructor](prime-form-root-construction.md).
This setup depends only on `Delta=-D` and `Y` and is reused for every
target form. Its bounded nonresidue search and Tonelli--Shanks steps
cost `Y` times a polynomial in the bit sizes under the pinned analytic
input; the actual root identities are checked exactly.

For an odd prime `p` not dividing `A`, let `s_p^2=Delta mod p`. The
roots in the `x` coordinate of one row are

$$
x\equiv \frac{(-B+s_p)y}{2A},\quad
\frac{(-B-s_p)y}{2A}\pmod p.\tag{3}
$$

When `p|A`, unramifiedness implies `p` does not divide `B`. If `p`
does not divide `y`, the unique root is `x=-Cy/B mod p`; if `p|y`,
every `x` is a root. The prime two is handled by checking both residues.

For a fixed target form, the coefficients of `y` in these root formulas
are constant. The implementation precomputes these slopes once per
prime, including the two parity cases at prime two. Every later row
uses only multiplication and reduction modulo the prime. The schedule
is retained across that target's expanding norm caps; only the most
recent target's schedule is kept in memory.

Along these progressions, divide the stored norm by every power of
`p` and increment its prime multiplicity. Reject a candidate once that
multiplicity exceeds the supplied budget `L`. A final remainder one
is precisely an accepted smooth norm.

No inert prime can divide a primitive norm coprime to `Delta`: modulo
an inert odd prime, a nonzero projective zero of `Q` would give a square
root of `Delta`; a zero vector would contradict primitiveness. The
prime-two case follows directly from the three possible coefficients
modulo two. Thus the split-prime list contains every possible prime
factor of an admissible norm. Skipping ramified and inert primes does
not weaken completeness.

Together, (1)--(3) prove the finite contract:

> An exhausted call certifies that no primitive vector of norm at most
> `H`, coprime to `Delta`, has a `Y`-smooth norm with multiplicity at
> most `L`.

It certifies nothing about larger norms. A successful call need not
return the smallest admissible norm.

### The search region remains an explicit cost

Let `P` be the number of coordinate points traversed and `R_0` the
number of nonempty rows. The elementary rectangle bounds give

$$
P=O\left(\frac H{\sqrt D}+\sqrt{\frac HA}
              +\sqrt{\frac{AH}D}+1\right).\tag{4}
$$

The point tests and finite differences take polynomial bit cost per
point. For a prime not dividing `A`, at most `2P/p+2R_0` progression
positions are visited. The all-root exception adds at most
`P omega(A)` positions. Actual successful prime divisions number at
most `P(L+1)`, because the budget test stops further division. Hence
one valid coarse bound, using the
[deterministic prime-root constructor](prime-form-root-construction.md)
and its pinned analytic input, is

$$
O\bigl(Y+R_0\pi(Y)
          +P(1+\log Y+\log A+L)\bigr),\tag{5}
$$

times a polynomial in `bits(D)+bits(H)+bits(Y)`. Only one row of
remainders is held in memory. The maximum row width is at most
`2 sqrt(H/A)+1`.

The implementation uses deterministic Tonelli--Shanks roots for every
known split prime. It chooses the smaller of the two roots modulo
the prime, so this setup improvement preserves the previous sieve
order and every recorded relation witness. The finite completeness
argument itself uses only exact root identities and does not depend
on the analytic bound for the nonresidue search.

The `H/sqrt(D)` term in (4) is the substantive remaining issue. The
known existence arguments can allow norm caps near `D` times a
subexponential factor. Exhausting that whole region then incurs an
exponential cost in `bits(D)`. The sieve is an exact baseline and a
platform for testing stronger extraction ideas; no uniform small
search region follows from its successful finite runs.

## 3. Build and use actual dominant relations

The implementation
[arithmetic_short_norm_factor.py](../tools/arithmetic_short_norm_factor.py)
chooses one orientation for each split prime at most a supplied finite
cutoff. It checks their gcds with the target integer first. For every
remaining generator `g_i`, it computes `g_i^(L+1)` using the
[polynomial-bit HNF arithmetic](direct-discriminant-short-norms.md).

Starting at `H=4 max(A,C)` for the resulting reduced form, it applies
the complete sieve. An unsuccessful attempt multiplies `H` by four,
up to the specified expansion budget. A successful vector is verified
again by the direct converter, giving an exponent vector `v_i` with
`||v_i||_1<=L`. The row

$$a_i=(L+1)e_i-v_i$$

is a class-group relation and is strictly diagonally dominant. The
prototype replays every row with HNF group operations. Exact
fraction-free elimination computes

$$0<E=|\det(a_i)_i|\le(2L+1)^m.$$

The adjugate identity proves that `E` annihilates all supplied
generators. The implementation also directly verifies these
annihilations. It then calls the polynomial early ambiguous extractor
at discriminant `-4N` and checks any returned divisor by integer
division.

The practical finite cutoff is explicitly **not** asserted to be the
large uniform full-generation cutoff in the analytic theorem. If the
generated subgroup or search budget does not produce a proper factor,
the prototype returns `unresolved`. A successful divisor remains an
exact certificate without any generation assumption. Complete
factorization would recurse on the two factors and handle primes and
perfect powers separately; this prototype records one splitting step.

## 5. A norm gap forces a growing word budget

The multiplicity budget cannot be reduced to an arbitrary small
constant while keeping a subexponential smoothness cutoff. This can
be proved directly, without a heuristic about random smooth norms.

Suppose the reduced representative of `P_p^K` has leading coefficient
`A=p^K`, for an unramified split prime `p`. Every primitive vector with
`y=0` represents `p^K` and therefore has prime multiplicity `K`. Every
vector with `y!=0` satisfies, by (1),

$$Q(x,y)\ge\frac D{4p^K}.$$

A `Y`-smooth integer with multiplicity at most `K-1` is at most
`Y^(K-1)`. Consequently

$$D>4p^K Y^{K-1}\tag{6}$$

rules out every desired witness, irrespective of the search algorithm
or norm cap. Equivalently, existence for this target requires

$$
K\ge\frac{\log(D/4)+\log Y}{\log p+\log Y}.\tag{7}
$$

There are explicit families where the required prime powers remain
reduced. Take `N=2*3^J-1`, `Delta=-4N`, and

$$P=(3,2,(N+1)/3).$$

For `1<=K<=J/2`, the class `P^K` has the reduced representative

$$Q=(3^K,2,2\cdot3^{J-K}).$$

This follows from the unramified root `1 mod 3^K` of `z^2=-N` and
the usual prime-ideal power description; `N+1` is divisible by
`3^K`. Thus when `log Y` tends to infinity but is `o(log D)`, (7)
forces `K>=(1-o(1)) log D/log Y` on suitable targets in this family.

A small exact fixture has

$$
N=10000001,\quad \Delta=-40000004,\quad
P_3^4=(81,8,123457).
$$

At `Y=31` and multiplicity budget three, every admissible norm would
be at most `31^3=29791`. A nonzero second coordinate has norm at
least `N/81>123456`, and the primitive zero-coordinate norm 81 has
multiplicity four. The sieve exhausts this instance after its single
possible primitive coordinate point.

The [powered-prime construction](powered-prime-smooth-norms.md)
uses one final cutoff throughout and proves existence with
`L log Y=(1+o(1)) log D` and `K=L+1`. Thus, when
`log p=o(log Y)`, its leading multiplicity budget matches (7) on
this explicit family. At its balanced choice
`log Y=Theta(sqrt(log D))`, both the sufficient budget and the
necessary budget have order `sqrt(log D)`, with the same leading
constant when expressed as `log D/log Y`.

This comparison concerns the target powers and their short-word
contract. It does not supply the still-missing deterministic norm
selection algorithm, and it is not a lower bound for factoring.
