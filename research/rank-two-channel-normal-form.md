# Regular pencils and the complete normalized rank-two channel form

## Statement and scope

Let `F` be any field. Suppose an invertible linear map on `Mat_q(F)` has
row reshuffling rank at most two, so it can be written

\[
U(X)=A_1X^{\mathsf T}B_1+A_2X^{\mathsf T}B_2.
\tag{1}
\]

Each of the two matrix pencils `span(A_1,A_2)` and `span(B_1,B_2)` is
regular: its determinant polynomial is not identically zero. After a
finite scalar extension, the decomposition can therefore be chosen with
an invertible first left factor.

If `U` also fixes the identity and preserves trace, then over that
extension it has the form

\[
\boxed{\quad
U(X)=P\left[X^{\mathsf T}(I-AB)+AX^{\mathsf T}B\right]P^{-1},
\qquad AB=BA.
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]

Conversely, (2) fixes the identity, preserves trace, and has row
reshuffling rank at most two. Invertibility or nilpotence of `I-U` remains
a separate condition.

The normalizations are assumptions. An arbitrary regular nilpotent
`S=I-U` need not fix the matrix identity or have trace as a left kernel
functional. Such nonunital channels remain a separate construction
space. The theorem also does not restrict commuting `A,B` to polynomials
in one regular nilpotent matrix; noncyclic commuting modules and
semisimple components remain available.

## 1. A singular pencil cannot give an invertible two-term tensor sum

Suppose `A,C` are square `q` by `q` matrices and

\[
\det(A+tC)=0\quad\text{in }F[t].
\tag{3}
\]

Then for any square `r` by `r` matrices `B,D`,

\[
\det(A\otimes B+C\otimes D)=0.
\tag{4}
\]

Here is a direct proof, including the case that `B` is singular. Introduce
an indeterminate `epsilon` and work over `F(epsilon)`. The matrix
`B+epsilon I` is invertible. Multiplying the deformed tensor sum on the
right by `I tensor (B+epsilon I)^(-1)` produces

\[
A\otimes I+C\otimes M,
\qquad M=D(B+\epsilon I)^{-1}.
\]

Over an algebraic closure, triangularize `M`. Reordering the tensor
basis makes the last display block triangular, with diagonal blocks
`A+lambda_j C`, one for each diagonal entry of that triangularization.
Every such block is singular by (3). Hence

\[
\det(A\otimes(B+\epsilon I)+C\otimes D)=0
\]

as an element of `F(epsilon)`. The left side is a polynomial in
`epsilon`, so it is the zero polynomial. Evaluating at zero proves (4).
No hypothesis on the characteristic or on the size of `F` is used.

The ordinary matrix of (1), after removing the invertible transposition
permutation, is a two-term Kronecker sum of this kind. Thus a singular
left pencil would make `U` singular. Exchanging the tensor factors proves
the same assertion for the right pencil.

This argument is also a short specialization of the regularity question
for tensor products of matrix pencils; see
[Matrix pencil generated by a tensor product from two matrix pencils](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379595003649).
The proof above is sufficient for the stated lemma.

## 2. Choosing an invertible factor over a finite extension

Regularity says that `f(t)=det(A_1+tA_2)` is a nonzero polynomial of degree
at most `q`. If `F` has more than `q` elements, some `t` in `F` has
`f(t)!=0`. If `F` is finite, pass to any finite extension with more than
`q` elements and choose such a `t` there. Infinite fields need no
extension.

Replace the first factor by `P=A_1+tA_2`, and rewrite (1) exactly as

\[
U(X)=PX^{\mathsf T}B_1+A_2X^{\mathsf T}(B_2-tB_1).
\tag{5}
\]

This changes the factorization, not the operator. All original matrix
entries of `U` stay in `F`. Extending the scalar field preserves every
matrix rank and every local Smith order of an existing polynomial matrix.

An extension can be necessary. Over `F2`, take

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,1),\qquad
C=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1).
\]

All three nonzero binary combinations `A,C,A+C` are singular, while
`det(A+tC)=t(1+t)` is not the zero polynomial. If `D` is a companion
matrix of `t^3+t+1`, then

\[
A\otimes I_3+C\otimes D
 =\operatorname{diag}(I_3,D,I_3+D)
\]

is invertible. Thus a rank-two decomposition of an invertible operator
can have no invertible left factor in its original binary pencil.
Over `F4`, any `t` outside `{0,1}` makes `A+tC` invertible.

## 3. Identity and trace normalization force commutation

Use (5), rename its second right factor `B_2'`, and put

\[
A=P^{-1}A_2,\qquad B=B_2'P.
\]

The identity condition `U(I)=I` gives

\[
B_1P=I-AB.
\]

Substituting this equation into (5) yields (2), without yet assuming
commutation. Cycling factors in the ordinary trace gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(U(X))
 =\operatorname{Tr}\left(X^{\mathsf T}(I-AB+BA)\right).
\]

The pairing `(X,Y) -> Tr(X^T Y)` is nondegenerate over every field.
Consequently trace preservation for every `X` is equivalent to `AB=BA`.
This proves necessity and the converse. Each summand in (2) is a
transpose channel with row reshuffling rank one, so the row rank is at
most two.

## 4. Exact finite controls

Run

```sh
python3 tools/check_rank_two_channel_normal_form.py \
  --output evidence/rank-two-channel-normal-form.json
```

The standard-library checker exhausts all binary two-by-two left pencil
pairs satisfying (3), and all binary two-by-two right factors, checking
that every resulting tensor sum is singular. It separately checks the
three-dimensional extension example and every normalized binary
`q=2` choice of `P,A,B`, verifying that trace preservation is equivalent
to commutation. The all-field conclusion comes from the proof, not from
these finite controls.

There is no improved factoring exponent. The lemma removes singular
matrix pencils from the invertible rank-two search and identifies the
remaining assumptions exactly.
