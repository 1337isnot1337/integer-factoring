# Polynomial-cost prime forms from a fixed Hecke zero-free strip

## Result and scope

The generator obligation in the [arithmetic framework](arithmetic-framework.md)
can be removed using a newer analytic input. For every negative quadratic-order
discriminant `Delta`, the full proper ideal class group is generated by the
prime forms of odd prime norm

$$p\le C\bigl(1+\log |\Delta|\bigr)^{32},\qquad p\nmid\Delta,$$

where `C` is one absolute effective constant. The generator list is constructed
deterministically in a polynomial number of bit operations in
`b = bits(abs(Delta))`. The construction does not need a factorization of
`Delta`, the fundamental discriminant, the conductor, or the class number.

There is also a direct biased-sample construction. For rational
`0 < epsilon <= 1/2`, one can construct a rational probability distribution
on prime forms with

$$\left|\sum_g\mu(g)\chi(g)\right|\le\epsilon$$

for every nonprincipal character of the full class group, with support size
`poly(b, 1/epsilon)` and `poly(b, bits(epsilon), 1/epsilon)` bit operations.
For the dyadic choices used in the factoring framework, the latter is simply
`poly(b, 1/epsilon)`. Each support element is
one of the supplied generators, so its word has length one. This avoids the
powering seed and expander-amplification dependency of the earlier
[biased-word construction](arithmetic-biased-words.md).

These are ordinary deductions from the external theorem identified below.
They are not new Lean formalizations or a complete factoring improvement.
Fast smooth represented-norm search remains open, and the proved factoring
score stays `1/2`.

## 1. Analytic input and exact scope

Cureton and Thorner, *A quasi-Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions*,
[Hexagon 2610.00200v2](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00200v2), Theorem 1.1 and
Corollary 1.2, state that every finite-order Hecke L-function over every
number field has no zeros in `Re(s) > 31/32`, wherever it is holomorphic.
The source text is available as
[DedekindZeta.tex](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2610.00200v2/source?path=DedekindZeta.tex).

The associated repository is pinned here to
[`09afbb4ce6570c1615d58509dc197fc9f0956b7d`](https://github.com/AxiomMath/DedekindZetaZeroFree/tree/09afbb4ce6570c1615d58509dc197fc9f0956b7d).
Its [solution declaration](https://github.com/AxiomMath/DedekindZetaZeroFree/blob/09afbb4ce6570c1615d58509dc197fc9f0956b7d/Solution/DedekindZetaSolution.lean)
`DedekindZetaZeroFree.heckeCorollary` supplies the finite-order ray-class
character statement, including `s = 1` for nonprincipal characters.
The challenge file is a statement template; its `sorry` placeholders are not
the solution proof. This work inspected the paper statement and solution
wrapper. It did not rebuild that external Lean project.

The quantification over every base field is needed here. A result only over
`Q(sqrt(-3))`, or only for Dirichlet characters, does not by itself cover all
ring-class characters used below.

The classical analytic facts used in the deduction are the completed
Hecke L-function, its functional equation, its Hadamard product, and the
contour shift for a smoothed logarithmic derivative. One convenient primary
reference is Thorner and Zaman, *An explicit bound for the least prime ideal
in the Chebotarev density theorem*, Algebra & Number Theory 11 (2017),
1135--1197, [publisher PDF](https://msp.org/ant/2017/11-5/ant-v11-n5-p04-p.pdf),
Section 2B and Lemma 9.4. Their proof of Theorem 1.2 also records the
ring-class conductor inequality used next.

## 2. Ring-class characters have controlled conductor

Write, for the proof only,

$$\Delta=f^2D_K,$$

where `D_K` is the fundamental discriminant of the imaginary quadratic
field `K`. A character of the proper ideal class group of the order of
conductor `f` lifts to a finite-order Hecke character modulo `f O_K`.
The primitive character inducing it has conductor `mathfrak f` dividing
`f O_K`; hence its analytic conductor satisfies

$$Q_\chi=|D_K|\,\mathrm N\mathfrak f\le |D_K|f^2=|\Delta|.$$

All these L-functions have degree two and the same complex-place gamma
factor. A nonprincipal ring-class character stays nonprincipal as a Hecke
character. Prime ideals away from `f` correspond to proper invertible
order ideals. In particular, when an odd prime `p` does not divide `Delta`
and splits in `K`, its two prime ideals correspond to the two oriented
prime forms of leading coefficient `p`.

The unknown decomposition `Delta = f^2 D_K` is used to prove the conductor
bound. The algorithm never computes it.

## 3. A smoothing that keeps the bound uniform

Put `theta = 31/32` and let `chi` now be a primitive degree-two character.
For `x >= 2`, define

$$W_\chi(x)=\sum_{\mathrm N\mathfrak a\le x}
 \Lambda_K(\mathfrak a)\chi(\mathfrak a)
 \frac{\mathrm N\mathfrak a}{x}
 \left(1-\frac{\mathrm N\mathfrak a}{x}\right).$$

Here `Lambda_K(p^j) = log Np`, and it is zero on other ideals.
The Mellin kernel of `w(t)=t(1-t)` on `[0,1]` is

$$\int_0^1 w(t)t^{s-1}\,dt=\frac1{(s+1)(s+2)}.$$

Shifting the logarithmic-derivative integral from `Re(s)=2` to
`Re(s)=-1/2` gives

$$W_\chi(x)=\delta_\chi\frac{x}{6}
 -\sum_\rho\frac{x^\rho}{(\rho+1)(\rho+2)}
 -\frac{r_\chi}{2}+I_\chi(x).$$

The nontrivial zeros are counted with multiplicity, `delta_chi` is one for
the principal character and zero otherwise, and `r_chi` is the order of
vanishing at zero, at most two. The final integral satisfies

$$I_\chi(x)=O\bigl(x^{-1/2}\log(Q_\chi+3)\bigr).$$

Indeed, the functional equation moves the logarithmic derivative on this
line to `Re(s)=3/2`, where the Euler product is absolutely convergent.
The remaining terms are `O(log(Q_chi+3)+log(|Im(s)|+3))`; the kernel is
integrable and is `O((1+|Im(s)|^2)^(-1))`. The horizontal contours can be
taken through the usual zero-avoiding heights and then sent to infinity.

For completeness, uniformity of the zero sum does not use a hidden
class-number factor. The Hadamard logarithmic-derivative identity at
`s=2`, together with the degree-two Euler-product and gamma-factor bounds,
gives

$$\sum_\rho\frac1{1+(\mathrm{Im}\rho)^2}
 \ll\log(Q_\chi+3).$$

One can obtain this directly by comparing each summand with
`Re(1/(2-rho))`, since `0 < Re(rho) < 1`. Also

$$|(\rho+1)(\rho+2)|\ge 1+(\mathrm{Im}\rho)^2.$$

The fixed zero-free strip therefore implies the uniform estimate

$$W_\chi(x)=\delta_\chi x/6
 +O\bigl(x^{31/32}\log(Q_\chi+3)\bigr).\tag{1}$$

The kernel has no pole at zero. In particular, this argument does not
silently require a uniform bound for `1/rho` at a low-lying zero, or a
constant that depends on the varying quadratic field.

## 4. Prime forms of polynomial norm generate the whole group

Let `H` be the subgroup generated by the two orientations of every split
odd prime `p <= x` with `p` not dividing `Delta`. If `H` is proper, choose
a nonprincipal character `chi` of the quotient by `H`.

Away from the rational primes dividing `2 Delta`, every prime-ideal power
of norm at most `x` has character value one. A degree-one prime ideal has
class in `H`; a degree-two prime ideal over an inert rational prime is a
principal ideal. Thus the principal and nonprincipal smoothed sums can
differ only at the excluded primes.

There are at most `2 omega(2 abs(Delta))` prime ideals above those primes.
For any one such prime ideal, the sum of the absolute contributions of
its powers is at most `(log x)/4`. Consequently

$$|W_1(x)-W_\chi(x)|
 \le\frac{\log(2|\Delta|)}{\log 2}\log x.\tag{2}$$

The primitive character can have additional Euler factors at the excluded
primes. They are included in this bound. Equation (1) gives instead

$$W_1(x)-W_\chi(x)=x/6+
 O\bigl(x^{31/32}\log(|\Delta|+3)\bigr).\tag{3}$$

Taking `x = C (1+log(abs(Delta)))^32` with one sufficiently large
absolute effective `C` contradicts (2). Enlarge `C` once to cover the
finite small-discriminant range. This proves generation.

For the actual algorithm use the integer cutoff `ceil(C') (b+1)^32`,
where one absolute effective `C'` dominates the preceding constant and
the change of logarithm base. This removes real logarithms from the
cutoff computation. The constant can be fixed once from the effective
degree-two explicit-formula bounds; it is independent of `Delta`.

To construct the list, enumerate primes up to this integer bound, discard those
dividing `2 Delta`, and enumerate roots `B` modulo `2p` with

$$B^2\equiv\Delta\pmod{4p},\qquad B\equiv\Delta\pmod2.$$

The corresponding primitive form is

$$\left(p,B,\frac{B^2-\Delta}{4p}\right).$$

Reduce it by the ordinary deterministic binary quadratic-form algorithm.
Even trial primality testing and exhaustive root enumeration take only
`poly(b)` bit operations at this bound. Thus no randomized modular
square-root routine is needed.

## 5. Direct small-bias distributions

For every split odd prime `p < x`, `p` not dividing `Delta`, assign the
same positive weight

$$\log p\,\frac{p}{x}\left(1-\frac p x\right)$$

to each orientation. Write `A_chi(x)` for the weighted character sum.
Removing prime powers and inert prime ideals from `W_chi(x)` costs
`O(sqrt(x) log(x))`: each rational prime at most `sqrt(x)` contributes
`O(log x)`, and there are at most `sqrt(x)` of them. Removing the excluded
primes costs `O(log(abs(Delta)+3) log x)`. Therefore

$$A_\chi(x)=\delta_\chi x/6+
 O\!\left(x^{31/32}\log(|\Delta|+3)
 +\sqrt{x}\log x+\log(|\Delta|+3)\log x\right).\tag{4}$$

Normalize by `A_1(x)`. For

$$B=b+\lceil\log_2(1/\epsilon)\rceil+2,\qquad
 x=\left\lceil(CB/\epsilon)^{32}\right\rceil,$$

and one sufficiently large absolute effective `C`, the resulting
distribution has nonprincipal bias at most `epsilon/2`, and
`A_1(x) >= x/12`.

These logarithmic weights can be replaced by exact rational ones. Compute
an integer `ell_p` with

$$0\le\log p-2^{-k}\ell_p\le 2^{1-k}.$$

A deterministic construction is to write `p=2^e u`, `1 <= u < 2`, and
truncate

$$\log u=2\sum_{j\ge0}
 \frac{((u-1)/(u+1))^{2j+1}}{2j+1},$$

using the same expansion with ratio `1/3` for `log 2`. The tails are
positive and decrease geometrically. Exact rational arithmetic followed
by rounding down gives the displayed inequality in polynomial time in
`log x+k`.

Use integer weights `ell_p p(x-p)` for each orientation. The error in
the unnormalized total mass, after restoring the common scale, is at
most `x 2^(-k)`, since there are at most `2x` oriented primes and
`w <= 1/4`. Normalization changes the distribution in `l1` norm by at
most `24 2^(-k)`. Taking `2^k >= 48/epsilon` spends at most the other
`epsilon/2` of the bias budget.

All probability weights are now exact rationals with
`O(log x+log(1/epsilon))` bits. Prime/root enumeration, form reduction,
and logarithm approximation cost `poly(b,bits(epsilon),1/epsilon)` bit operations.
Repeated reduced forms simply have their weights added. Inverse
orientations receive equal weights, so the distribution is symmetric.

The support itself generates the group: otherwise a nonprincipal
character trivial on that subgroup would have bias one.

## 6. Consequence for relation search

If a smooth-form density lower bound is
`delta = exp(-O(sqrt(b log b)))`, choose `epsilon <= delta/2`.
The generators and biased distribution above then cost
`exp(O(sqrt(b log b)))` bit operations and encoded size. All support
words have length one in the enlarged prime-form list.

The relation coordinate list must include the smooth factor base as
well as the sampling primes. Take their union, including the prime forms
over `2` when relevant. Adding those generators does not change the
distribution or its character-bias bound. If the smooth bound `Y` is
also `exp(O(sqrt(b log b)))`, deterministic enumeration of this union
has the same asymptotic cost. Thus the smooth words already supplied by
the earlier arithmetic notes retain their coordinates without an
unproved change-of-generators procedure.

The [positive relation kernel](positive-relation-kernel.md) still proves
the existence of a smooth pair for every translated sample. The new
construction does not locate that pair or a represented smooth norm.
It removes full generation and biased sampling from the list of missing
arithmetic primitives. Deterministic relation-witness search, with its
complete uniform bit bound, remains the obstacle to a stronger factoring
theorem.

## 7. Exact finite controls

Run:

```sh
python3 tools/check_prime_form_bias.py
```

The [constructor](../tools/prime_form_bias.py) accepts a finite prime
cutoff and uses exact rational series bounds, integer weights, both
prime-form orientations, and discriminant gcd tests. It does not know
the class number, factorization of the discriminant, or its conductor.
The [checker](../tools/check_prime_form_bias.py) separately enumerates
small class groups and bounds every nonprincipal character bias by the
exact `l1` distance between the sample and the uniform distribution.
It also checks inverse symmetry and generates the full group from the
sample support.

The [record](../evidence/prime-form-bias.json) checks 48 logarithm
intervals and 39 discriminants, including nonmaximal orders. Its largest
class group has order 90. There are 506 oriented prime records across
the accepted controls, with prime cutoffs at most 1021. Every displayed
sample has certified bias at most `3/4`.

For discriminant `-41612`, the sample at cutoff 1021 has 178 oriented
prime records and 80 distinct support classes. The exact character-bias
upper bound is

$$\frac{4715672063392}{7983469691865}<\frac34.$$

Its support generates all 90 classes even though it omits ten of them.
This is a finite constructor and sample certificate, not a verification
of the Hecke theorem or the uniform analytic cutoff. The small form
package's composition search is used only by the checker and carries
no polynomial-bit-cost claim.
