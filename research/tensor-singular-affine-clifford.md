# Singular local maps still have compact nonzero Smith profiles

## Result and scope

The auxiliary-mode Clifford method extends to **singular** one-mode maps.
For a nonzero circuit on `n` physical modes, the rank is a power of two and
the complete nonzero Smith multiset has the form

\[
 \left\{\mu+\sum_{i\in S}b_i:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\right\},
 \qquad b_i\ge0,\quad \mu\in\mathbb Z.
 \tag{1}
\]

Its rank is `2^r`, with `0 <= r <= n`. The zero operator is a separate
possibility. A `2(n+1)`-row Clifford calculation determines the rank,
minimum order and gaps without constructing the `2^n`-row Fock matrix.

The allowed circuits consist of Gaussian gates and arbitrary one-mode
linear maps over a characteristic-two Laurent-series field. Even Gaussian
gates commute with the auxiliary change of basis; a particle-hole gate is
itself a one-mode map. Equivalently, one may use exterior mode changes,
creation/annihilation pair shears, particle-hole maps, scalars, and arbitrary
one-mode maps in any order. Singular maps may act on different modes and
may be separated by coupling gates. No simultaneous diagonalization of
the local maps is assumed.

This is a classification and an exact computation method. It does not
provide a pressure improvement, a pressure lower bound for the family,
or a new worst-case factoring exponent. A smaller rank alone is not a
tensor improvement: physical type partitions, both cuts, and every
clearing must still be included in the score calculation.

## 1. Regularize, then use one auxiliary mode

Let `k` have characteristic two, let `K = k((t))`, and let `G_0` be the
given circuit on the full exterior space of `n` modes. Replace each local
two-by-two matrix `X(t)` by

\[
 X_\varepsilon(t)=X(t)+\varepsilon I_2.
 \tag{2}
\]

The determinant is

\[
 \det X_\varepsilon=\det X+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{tr}(X)+\varepsilon^2.
 \tag{3}
\]

It is a nonzero polynomial in `epsilon`, even if `X` is zero. Thus the
regularized circuit `G_epsilon` is invertible over `K((epsilon))` and
specializes to `G_0` at `epsilon = 0`. Every matrix entry has nonnegative
epsilon order; its t order may be negative.

Add one auxiliary mode, write `R = C_* + A_*`, and define the integral
permutation

\[
 U(|S\rangle\otimes v)=|S\rangle\otimes R^{|S|\bmod2}v.
 \tag{4}
\]

Then `U^2=I`, and conjugation by `U` sends `C_i` and `A_i` to `C_i R`
and `A_i R`. It fixes every even Gaussian factor. An invertible local
matrix becomes Gaussian on its mode and the auxiliary mode. For example,
the transformed linear shear factors as

\[
 I+bA_iR=(I+bA_iC_*)(I+bA_iA_*),
 \tag{5}
\]

since the cross term vanishes. Diagonal factors, the opposite shear and
particle-hole exchange complete the two-by-two factorization. Consequently

\[
 \widetilde G_\varepsilon=U(G_\varepsilon\otimes I_2)U
 \tag{6}
\]

is Gaussian. Its Smith multiset is exactly two copies of the multiset of
`G_epsilon`. These identities are the
[invertible affine Clifford construction](tensor-affine-clifford-profiles.md);
regularization makes that construction applicable to every local factor.

## 2. The Clifford theorem over the lexicographic valuation ring

For nonzero `f` in `K((epsilon))`, define

\[
 v(f)=\bigl(v_\varepsilon(f),\;v_t(\text{leading epsilon coefficient of }f)\bigr)
       \in\mathbb Z^2,
 \tag{7}
\]

ordered lexicographically. Let `V` be its valuation ring. This ring is
not being treated as a discrete valuation ring. The following direct
argument is sufficient.

The [elementary quadratic Clifford proof](clifford-gaussian-smith-profiles.md)
uses only finitely many valuation comparisons and integral divisions.
Here are the needed details for this ring.

1. For any two nonzero field elements, `a/b` belongs to `V` exactly when
   `v(a) >= v(b)`. A finite set of entries has a least valuation, attained
   by an entry. An ideal generated by finitely many entries is therefore
   generated by such an entry. These facts also give finite matrix Smith
   elimination over `V`; no infinite elimination is used.
2. A primitive isotropic vector has a unit coordinate. Integral mode
   changes, a possible particle-hole exchange, and alternating shears
   send it to the first creation vector. The same explicit Fock lifts
   and their inverses lie in `V`.
3. For an orthogonal Clifford matrix, choose its least entry valuation
   `-a`, where `a >= (0,0)`. This sign follows from its unit determinant.
   Every value pair is realized by a monomial in `epsilon` and `t`.
   Rescale the selected isotropic column by a monomial of value `a`,
   normalize it, and use the integral Eichler map to split off the
   hyperbolic pair with values `-a,a`. The coefficients of that map have
   nonnegative value because they divide only by the chosen minimum.
4. The remaining block has no entry value below `-a`. Induction splits
   off all pairs in a finite number of steps. The integral Fock lifts
   reduce the Gaussian operator to an exterior diagonal times a scalar.
   The scalar conclusion follows because the creation and annihilation
   operators generate all matrix units.

Thus the Clifford theorem holds with value pairs in place of integers.
For `m` modes, write its paired values as `+/-a_1,...,+/-a_m`, where all
`a_i >= (0,0)`. The Fock values are

\[
 \left\{\mu+\sum_{i\in S}a_i:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\right\},
 \qquad
 \mu=2^{-m}v(\det G)-\tfrac12\sum_i a_i.
 \tag{8}
\]

The divisions in (8) describe the determinant mean; they do not assert
divisibility of an arbitrary pair. The diagonal Fock reduction proves
that its resulting minimum `mu` belongs to `Z^2`.

Apply this result to (6), with `m=n+1`. Its minimum-value multiplicity is
`2^z`, where `z` is the number of **exact zero pairs** among the gaps.
Since every value of (6) occurs with even multiplicity, `z >= 1`. Remove
one exact gap `(0,0)`. The remaining `n` gaps and the same minimum give
the complete regularized profile of `G_epsilon`. Removing an arbitrary
gap with zero first coordinate would be wrong: its second coordinate
can be positive.

## 3. Specialization selects exactly the epsilon-zero part

Let `P` be the ideal of `V` consisting of elements with positive epsilon
order. Constant-coefficient reduction gives

\[
 V/P\simeq k[[t]].
 \tag{9}
\]

An invertible integral matrix over `V` has determinant value `(0,0)`.
Its reduction modulo `P` is therefore invertible over `k[[t]]`.
Multiply the original circuit by a common power of `t` if necessary so
that all its epsilon-zero coefficients are integral. Apply the integral
Smith reduction over `V` and reduce it modulo `P`. A diagonal entry of
positive epsilon order becomes zero; an epsilon-zero entry becomes a
nonzero Laurent series of the recorded t order. Removing the common
t clearing proves that these and only these entries give the nonzero
Smith profile of `G_0`.

Write the regularized minimum as `(u,mu)` and the remaining gaps as
`(e_i,b_i)`. Because the circuit is epsilon-integral, `u >= 0`. Since
the gaps are lexicographically nonnegative, `e_i >= 0`.

- If `u > 0`, every diagonal entry disappears. The specialized circuit
  is zero.
- If `u = 0`, an epsilon-zero subset cannot contain any gap with
  `e_i > 0`. It may contain any of the gaps with `e_i = 0`; each of
  those has `b_i >= 0`. The selected multiset is exactly (1).

This proves both the power-of-two rank statement and the complete
nonzero profile formula. The claim concerns the full exterior space,
not a separately selected parity summand.

Two small examples show why both coordinates and the minimum matter.
For

\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}0&t^a\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad(a>0),
 \tag{10}
\]

the regularized minimum is `(0,a)` and the physical gap is `(2,-2a)`.
Its second coordinate is negative, but its lexicographic value is
positive. The specialization has rank one and its sole order is `a`.
Conversely, the consecutive projectors `diag(1,0)` and `diag(0,1)` have
zero product. Their regularized product is `epsilon(1+epsilon) I`:
the gap is zero, but the minimum first coordinate is one.

## 4. A finite exact computation by bounded substitution

For polynomial input factors, assemble the regularized Clifford matrix
as a common rational matrix

\[
 B(t,\varepsilon)=N(t,\varepsilon)/d(t,\varepsilon),
 \qquad s=2(n+1).
 \tag{11}
\]

Let `D_N` bound the t degree of every entry of `N`, and let `D_d` bound
the t degree of `d`. Degrees here ignore the epsilon exponent. All entries
have nonnegative t and epsilon powers after the explicitly charged local
clearings. Set

\[
 L=sD_N+D_d,\qquad W=2L+1.
 \tag{12}
\]

Every `j`-minor of `N` has t degree at most `j D_N`. Substituting
`epsilon=t^W` preserves its lexicographic leading value: distinct
epsilon-degree bands cannot overlap, and cancellation within each band
is unchanged. The same statement applies to `d`. Thus every
determinantal minimum and every signed Smith value is preserved under
the encoding `(a,b) -> Wa+b`.

For consecutive determinantal minima, their second coordinates lie in
`[0,j D_N]` and `[0,(j-1)D_N]`. After subtracting the denominator value,
the second coordinate of each individual signed order has absolute
value at most `L`. Since `W > 2L`, it is recovered uniquely as the
balanced remainder modulo `W`. The quotient is its epsilon coordinate.
This bound handles negative second coordinates, including (10).

The determinant mean is computed as a pair directly from the circuit.
Each local factor contributes `v(det X_epsilon)` to **twice** the mean;
equation (3) computes it without an encoded-value ambiguity. Pair and
mode shears contribute zero, an exterior diagonal contributes the sum
of its integer mode weights, and a scalar `t^c` contributes `(0,2c)`.
All local and global clearings are included.

The implementation uses exact fraction-free polynomial elimination on
`N(t,t^W)`. At each step it chooses the nonzero entry of least t order.
The Bareiss division by the preceding pivot is exact. Its pivot is a
minor of the original matrix; the difference of successive pivot orders
is the next Smith order. Equivalently, the current block divided by the
previous pivot is the ordinary Schur complement, so the pivot choice is
the usual integral local Smith elimination. An exactly zero remaining
block ends the rank calculation. No finite-series cutoff or externally
promised rank supplies this conclusion.

This is a polynomial algorithm in the matrix order, number of factors,
and expanded polynomial degrees. The encoded degree is at most
`D_N + W E_N`, where `E_N` is the epsilon-degree bound obtained by
charging each local factor. Bareiss minors have at most the matrix order
times that degree. Exact polynomial arithmetic therefore has polynomial
cost. This is **not** a polynomial bound in the logarithms of arbitrary
sparsely supplied exponents. Explicit expansion of the output multiset
also costs its output size; the default result retains only rank,
minimum and gaps.

## 5. Implementation and independent controls

`tools/tensor_singular_affine_clifford.py` implements the procedure over
`F4`. Its circuit interface supports polynomial local matrices, Gaussian
creation and annihilation pair shears, exterior mode shears, exterior
Laurent diagonals, and Laurent scalar powers. Factors act successively
on the left. The small Clifford construction reuses the established
local four-by-four numerator formula; polynomial elimination is new and
exact.

The independent checker builds literal Fock matrices in the original
subset basis. It uses separate coefficient-list field arithmetic and
reduced rational-function elimination, rather than the Clifford
construction or its bit-plane Bareiss engine. Toeplitz-prefix ranks give
an additional finite-field check of singular multiplicities. The receipt
records every circuit, complete result and source hashes.

The initial suite covers all 256 constant `F4` local matrices, nilpotent
maps with negative second-coordinate gaps, opposing projectors,
negative Laurent scalars, and coupled two-, three- and four-mode circuits with
singular and invertible maps. Separate rectangular polynomial controls
check the rank-revealing Bareiss routine against rational elimination.

The retained receipt contains 322 literal circuits, 6,100 literal matrix
entries, 665 independently computed nonzero orders, and 192 independent
Toeplitz-prefix ranks. Arithmetic controls include 128 exact divisions,
96 rectangular Smith comparisons and two nonexact-division rejections;
eight malformed circuit inputs are rejected.

Three larger controls use 8, 16 and 32 physical modes. Disjoint singular
local factors have a directly known tensor profile; integral unimodular
Gaussian factors at both ends couple modes while preserving that profile.
The Clifford matrices have orders 18, 34 and 66 and the computed ranks are
64, 16,384 and 1,073,741,824. Their exponentially large Fock matrices and
expanded profiles are never constructed. These are algebraic compactness
controls, not factoring instances or favorable tensor pressure results.

```sh
python3 tools/check_tensor_singular_affine_clifford.py \
  --output evidence/tensor-singular-affine-clifford.json
```

The proof is the uniform argument above. The finite controls check its
implementation; they are not a substitute for that argument and do not
establish a pressure claim.
