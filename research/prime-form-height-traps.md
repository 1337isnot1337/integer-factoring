# Why strict leading-coefficient descent can stop

## Scope

The [conic denominator analysis](conic-denominator-descent.md) shows that
changing a rational conic parameter does not automatically change the
known square-root class. One possible next step is to compose that class
with small prime forms and try to decrease the leading coefficient of its
reduced representative. The elementary product-norm calculation below
obstructs this rule on an entire bounded word neighborhood.

The obstruction persists when the prime forms generate the full class
group and when the readily available class at the prime 2 is included.
It concerns this specific monotone potential. It is neither a lower bound
for general class-group navigation nor a new factoring complexity bound.

## 1. Products remain reduced below the geometric threshold

Let \(N>1\) be odd, let \(\Delta=-4N\), and let \(P\) be a set of
odd split rational primes coprime to \(N\). For each \(p\in P\), fix a
prime-form class \(g_p\). Let \(q\notin P\) be another odd split prime
coprime to \(N\), and let \(g_q\) be either of its two orientations.

For any integers \(e_p\), define

\[
n=\prod_{p\in P}p^{|e_p|},\qquad
h=g_q\prod_{p\in P}g_p^{e_p}.
\]

**Proposition.** If \((qn)^2<N\), then the leading coefficient of the
canonical reduced form for \(h\) is exactly \(qn\).

For each \(p\), all \(|e_p|\) ideal factors have the same orientation.
Their product is a primitive invertible ideal of norm \(n\). It is
coprime to the chosen ideal above \(q\), so the further product is
primitive and has norm \(A=qn\). Equivalently, ordinary coprime
composition and root lifting produce a primitive form

\[
\left(A,B,\frac{B^2+4N}{4A}\right),
\]

where \(B\) has the prescribed prime-power orientations. Replace \(B\)
by its congruent representative in \((-A,A]\). Its third coefficient
satisfies

\[
C\ge\frac NA>A.
\]

The form is already reduced. The middle coefficient is even and \(A\)
is odd, so the negative boundary \(B=-A\) cannot occur. This proves the
claim using only ideal factors coprime to the order discriminant. In
particular, the proof does not require the order to be maximal or \(N\)
to be squarefree.

Thus every such word has leading coefficient at least \(q\). A
nonidentity word with \(n>1\) strictly increases it. A class can have
very small leading coefficient while still lying at a large weighted
word distance from every class with a smaller leading coefficient.

## 2. The explicit class at 2 does not remove the obstruction

Suppose that \(E\) is any fixed primitive form class of leading
coefficient \(a_E\in\{1,2,4\}\). Its ideal norm is coprime to \(qn\),
so the same argument gives

\[
\operatorname{lead}\!\left(Eg_q\prod_{p\in P}g_p^{e_p}\right)
=a_E qn
\qquad\text{whenever }(a_E qn)^2<N.
\tag{1}
\]

This includes the following explicit finite groups, for \(N\) large
enough that their displayed representatives are reduced:

- If \(N\equiv1\pmod4\), the group generated by
  \(U=(2,2,(N+1)/2)\) consists of the identity and \(U\). Direct
  composition gives \(U^2=1\).
- If \(N\equiv3\pmod8\), the group generated by
  \(F=(4,2,(N+1)/4)\) consists of the identity, \(F\), and
  \(F^{-1}\). The trailing coefficient is odd, so these forms are
  primitive; direct composition gives \(F^2=F^{-1}\).

These small classes are important to include in a descent check. The
class \(F\) alone would be an easy local minimum for an odd-prime move
set, but explicitly adding \(F\) would immediately remove that example.
Using an omitted odd prime \(q\) gives the stronger obstruction (1).

Allow arbitrary use of the appropriate finite group at 2. If

\[
4qn<\sqrt N,
\]

then every resulting reduced leading coefficient is at least \(q\).
Consequently, if every factor-base prime is at most \(Y\), no word of
at most \(\ell\) odd-prime moves can decrease the leading coefficient
when

\[
4qY^\ell<\sqrt N.
\tag{2}
\]

For \(q,Y\) bounded by powers of \(\log N\), this can force an
\(\Omega(\log N/\log\log N)\) lookahead before the first decrease is
even possible. It does not say that enumerating all words is necessary:
an algorithm using a different potential, a structured global search,
or an explicit relation can leave this neighborhood efficiently.

## 3. A complete finite example

Take

\[
N=1022117,\qquad \Delta=-4088468,\qquad P=\{3,7,11\}.
\]

The three prime forms generate all 928 proper classes, as verified by
independent reduced-form enumeration and group closure. The next split
prime is \(q=17\), with

\[
g_q=(17,10,60126),\qquad U=(2,2,511059).
\]

The reduced leading coefficients of its immediate neighbors are:

| Move | Inverse orientation | Positive orientation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(g_3\) | 51 | 51 |
| \(g_7\) | 119 | 119 |
| \(g_{11}\) | 187 | 187 |
| \(U\) | 34 | 34 |

All are larger than 17. To measure the obstruction beyond one move,
assign weight \(p\) to either orientation of \(g_p\), and weight 2 to
\(U\). A path's weight is the product of its edge weights. Since the
group is abelian, an optimum path has no opposite-orientation
cancellations; two occurrences of \(U\) can also be removed. Thus this
path cost is the oriented norm of an optimum word, including the
possible factor 2.

An exact multiplicative-weight Dijkstra search over this finite group
finds the first smaller leading coefficient at weight

\[
33957=3^2 7^3 11,
\qquad
g_q g_3^{-2}g_7^{-3}g_{11}^{-1}=(14,6,73009).
\]

The recorded path has leading coefficients

\[
17,\ 51,\ 153,\ 1071,\ 1078,\ 154,\ 14.
\]

There are 480 class values at strictly smaller minimum path weight than
33957, and none has leading coefficient below 17. The minimum path
weight from \(g_q\) to the identity is 475398.

The minimum is certified, not just inferred from the first returned
path. For all 928 vertices, the evidence records an integer distance
label and an equality predecessor. Every noninitial vertex has a
strictly smaller predecessor label whose edge realizes its label.
Every directed edge satisfies
\(d(v)\le p\,d(u)\). The predecessor chains give paths attaining the
labels; the edge inequalities prove that every path has at least the
labelled weight. There are 927 predecessor checks and 7424 directed
edge checks.

This exhaustive finite computation is not proposed as an efficient
general navigation algorithm. It establishes that full generation and
the known class at 2 do not make strict height descent work.

## 4. Large arithmetic control and reproduction

The checker also takes

\[
P=\{3,5,7,11\},\quad q=13,\quad
N=(8\cdot2^{180}+4)\prod_{p\in P\cup\{q\}}p-1.
\]

This is a 197-bit odd input. Every selected splitting root is 2, and
\(N\equiv3\pmod8\), so both nonidentity conductor classes above are
available. It checks all 2401 signed words with \(|e_p|\le3\), and all
three choices from the group generated by \(F\): 7203 products in total.
For every one, the reduced leading coefficient is exactly \(a_E qn\).
The maximum oriented norm in the box is 1540798875, well below the
threshold in (1).

This large control uses no factorization or class-group enumeration and
makes no full-generation claim. It checks the product and reduction
identities directly at a substantially larger discriminant.

```sh
python3 tools/check_prime_form_height_traps.py
```
