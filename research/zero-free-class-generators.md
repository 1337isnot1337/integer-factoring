# A fixed zero-free strip would supply small generators for every quadratic order

This note proves a **conditional implication**: a fixed zero-free half-plane
for the relevant Dedekind zeta functions gives prime-form generators of
polynomial size in log|Delta|. It applies to nonmaximal quadratic orders,
does not require the factorization of Delta as algorithm input, and avoids
a class-field degree in the final bound. Under this analytic input, the
global generator and missing two-torsion direction obligations in the
arithmetic program disappear. Efficient smooth-norm or fixed-square
representation search remains unproved.

Recent manuscripts assert analytic results strong enough to supply the
input. Their statements are recorded below with versions. This repository
note does not independently verify those analytic proofs, and it does not
promote a claimed external theorem or a conditional implication to a new
unconditional factoring score.

## 1. The explicit analytic input

Fix a rational constant 0 < eta <= 1/2. Write ZF(eta) for the assertion that every
Dedekind zeta function needed below has no zero in Re(s)>1-eta, with the
principal pole at s=1 permitted. It suffices to assume this for the
quadratic field K and the finite abelian extensions of K arising from
quotients of its ring class groups.

We use the standard uniform smoothed explicit-formula consequence. Fix
one explicit nonzero nonnegative smooth function phi supported in (1,2),
with computable derivative bounds,
and define

```math
c_\phi=\int_1^2\phi(t)\,dt>0,
\qquad
\Psi_F(x)=\sum_{\mathfrak p}\sum_{j\ge1}
  \log N\mathfrak p\,\phi((N\mathfrak p)^j/x).
```

For a number field F of absolute discriminant D_F and degree n_F, the
required estimate is

```math
\left|\Psi_F(x)-c_\phi x\right|
\le C_{\phi,\eta}\,x^{1-\eta}(\log D_F+n_F),
\qquad x\ge2,                                      \tag{1}
```

where C_{phi,eta} is effectively computable and independent of F. The
field independence is essential. A separate unspecified constant for each
ring class field would not give the claimed uniform generator bound.

For clarity, the usual proof of (1) uses Mellin inversion for
-zeta_F'/zeta_F. Shifting the contour gives the pole term c_phi x, a sum
over nontrivial zeros, and an error bounded by a constant times
log D_F+n_F. The Mellin transform of phi decays faster than any fixed
power of the imaginary part, while the uniform zero count in each unit
height interval is

```math
O(\log D_F+n_F\log(|t|+2)).
```

The sum of the absolute Mellin weights of the zeros is therefore
O_phi(log D_F+n_F). ZF(eta) bounds their factors x^Re(rho) by x^(1-eta).
The functional equation bounds the remaining contour uniformly. Fixed
computable smoothing and explicit zero-counting constants give effective
constants in (1). This is the smoothed explicit-formula argument stated
in Lemma 11.2 of the primary finite-field manuscript cited in Section 6.
The analytic zero-free assertion itself is a separate input.

## 2. A ring-class extension witnesses failure of generation

Let Delta<0 be an order discriminant and write

```math
\Delta=f^2D_K,
\qquad
\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K,
\qquad
G=\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f),
```

where D_K is the fundamental discriminant of the imaginary quadratic
field K. This decomposition is used in the proof; the algorithm is not
given f, D_K, or their prime factors.

Let H be the subgroup generated by the two conjugate prime-form classes
at every rational prime p<=Y with p not dividing Delta and Delta a square
modulo 4p. Choosing one of the conjugate classes suffices because a
subgroup contains inverses. Suppose H is proper. Ring class field theory
gives a finite abelian extension L/K with

```math
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong G/H,
\qquad d=[L:K]\ge2.
```

Its conductor divides f O_K. Every character of Gal(L/K) has conductor
norm at most f^2. The conductor-discriminant formula therefore gives

```math
D_L
 =|D_K|^d\prod_\chi N\mathfrak f_\chi
 \le |D_K|^d f^{2(d-1)}
 \le |\Delta|^d,
\qquad n_L=2d.                                   \tag{2}
```

We never construct L or enumerate its characters. It is a hypothetical
extension used to contradict the assertion that H is proper.

Subtract the normalized prime-ideal sums:

```math
V(x)=\Psi_K(x)-d^{-1}\Psi_L(x).
```

Equations (1) and (2) show that

```math
V(x)=(1-d^{-1})c_\phi x
 +O_{\phi,\eta}\!\left(x^{1-\eta}(\log|\Delta|+2)\right).       \tag{3}
```

The factor d has cancelled from both the logarithmic discriminant and
the degree in the error. Applying a least-prime estimate to L while
leaving log D_L unnormalized would lose this useful cancellation.

## 3. Small prime forms force the same sum to be tiny

Take Y>=2x. Every prime ideal of K lying over a rational prime
p<=2x with p not dividing Delta splits completely in L:

- If p splits in K, its two prime-ideal classes lie in H by construction.
  Their Artin symbols in G/H are trivial.
- If p is inert in K, the prime ideal is (p), so its ring ideal class is
  principal. Its Artin symbol is trivial as well.

The assertion also handles prime powers in the smoothed sums. If a prime
ideal P of K splits completely in L, there are d primes above it with
the same norm. Its entire contribution cancels in V(x), at every power.

At any remaining prime ideal P, let e and r be its ramification and
residue degrees in the Galois extension L/K. There are d/(er) primes
above it. At the base-field norm power (NP)^j, its contribution to V(x)
has coefficient

```math
\log N P\left(1-e^{-1}\mathbf1_{r\mid j}\right).             \tag{4}
```

This coefficient lies between zero and log NP. Consequently V(x) is
nonnegative, and only rational primes dividing Delta can contribute to
its support interval when all the small prime-form classes lie in H.

Because phi is supported in (1,2), at most one positive power of NP lies
in its support interval (x,2x). For any rational prime p, the sum of
log NP over primes P of the quadratic field K above p is at most
2 log p. Hence

```math
0\le V(x)
\le 2\|\phi\|_\infty\sum_{p\mid\Delta}\log p
\le 2\|\phi\|_\infty\log|\Delta|.                        \tag{5}
```

There is no class-field degree, list of ramified primes, or higher-power
count in this bound. Those quantities are used only in the proof of the
inequality, not as computational inputs.

Put b=max(2,ceil(log_2(|Delta|+1))). Choose a fixed effectively computable
constant C_eta sufficiently large, and take

```math
x=(C_\eta b)^{1/\eta}.
```

Then x^eta=C_eta b makes the error in (3) at most c_phi x/4. Since
d>=2, equation (3) gives V(x)>=c_phi x/4. Enlarging the same fixed
constant makes this larger than the right side of (5), for every b>=2.
This contradiction proves the following theorem.

**Conditional generator theorem.** Under ZF(eta) and the effective
uniform estimate (1), the proper class group of every imaginary
quadratic order of discriminant Delta is generated by the prime forms
at unramified split rational primes

```math
p\le 2(C_\eta b)^{1/\eta}.
```

An extra rounding or an exponent larger than 1/eta can be used to make
the bound an integer formula. Only a single fixed numerical constant is
involved, so this is a uniform algorithmic statement.

## 4. Descent from the cyclotomic Hecke version

The stronger all-number-fields input is not necessary for this
implication. Suppose instead that all finite-order Hecke L-functions
over cyclotomic fields containing the twelfth roots of unity satisfy one
fixed zero-free strip.

For a quadratic field K, choose a cyclotomic field F containing both K
and those roots of unity. For example, the conductor-discriminant theorem
for quadratic fields permits F=Q(zeta_lcm(12,|D_K|)). For any finite-order
Hecke character chi of K, abelian Artin formalism gives

```math
L_F(s,\chi\circ N_{F/K})
 =\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)}}L_K(s,\chi\psi).
                                                                  \tag{6}
```

Each factor on the right is holomorphic away from a possible principal
pole at s=1. Thus a zero of L_K(s,chi) in the stated half-plane away from
s=1 would force a zero of the left side. It cannot be cancelled by a pole
of another factor there. Nonprincipal Hecke L-functions are nonzero at
s=1 by the standard nonvanishing theorem. This descends the strip to
every finite-order Hecke character of K.

Factoring zeta_L as the product of the Hecke L-functions corresponding
to the characters of an abelian extension L/K then gives the strip for
the ring class fields needed above. Imprimitive Euler factors cause no
new issue in this half-plane: their possible zeros have real part zero.

The possibly enormous degree and discriminant of F do not enter the
generator bound. Equation (6) is used solely to infer a qualitative
zero-free statement. Estimate (1) is subsequently applied to K and L,
with their own discriminants, followed by the normalization in (3).

## 5. Constructive consequences and limits

For fixed eta, the prime bound is polynomial in b. One can enumerate the
rational primes up to that bound, compute gcd(p,Delta), and find every
splitting root by deterministic finite search modulo 4p. This has a
polynomial bit cost in b, with a large fixed exponent. A fast modular-root
algorithm, a factorization of Delta, and a class-number oracle are not
assumed.

Thus the factor base in the existing arithmetic program can be enlarged
to the maximum of its chosen Y and this generator bound. A fixed
polynomial in b is eventually below exp(c sqrt(b log b)) for every c>0.
The enlargement therefore leaves the arithmetic subexponential scale
unchanged. Small inputs can be handled by a fixed finite fallback.

Conditional on this analytic input, H=G. A full-rank relation matrix on
the factor base gives a multiple of the full class number directly, and
the existing [two-group extraction](arithmetic-torsion-extraction.md)
can access every global two-torsion direction. In particular, the proper
subgroup obstruction in [density index transfer](arithmetic-density-lift.md)
would no longer persist once the factor base reaches the stated bound.

The finite example Delta=-348 illustrates the distinction. At Y=7 the
factor base contains only (7,4,13), which generates a subgroup of order
three. At Y=11 it also contains the reduced form (8,-2,11) of the prime
ideal above 11, and the two forms generate all six classes, including
(3,0,29). These exact small computations illustrate completion of a
subgroup; they do not test or prove the analytic bound.

The [fixed smooth-square theorem](universal-smooth-square.md) still
requires an efficient representation oracle. Knowing that the factor
base generates G does not find a bounded word for a target, compute a
relation matrix, or supply a class-number multiple. The analytic input
alone therefore yields no improved factoring score through this route.
