# A finite square-zero ideal in the full parameterized Weyl algebra

## Results and scope

Let `A_h^(2)` be the row-matrix algebra generated by every coefficient
of the binary weighted Weyl curves `P_h(z)` and `Q_h(z)`, where
`Q_h` is the opposite `y,u` orientation. Let `A_h^(sym)` also
include the raw transposed coefficient matrices. As shown below,
this is precisely the algebra generated by all eight
crossing-preserving external symmetry images. At energy 32 the label
dimension is 202 and the full row dimension is 40804.

Exact finite orbit closure proves that the two-sided ideal
generated by the rank-one top pairing `R=P_h,h=v v^T`, with
`v=vec(I)`, is square-zero even in `A_h^(sym)`. This statement
allows arbitrary word lengths, arbitrary linear recombinations,
and coefficients in every characteristic-two extension field.

The proof is a finite certificate. It is **not** an all-energy
radical theorem. The same property is separately replayed at
energies 2, 4, 8, and 16. The corresponding natural affine
search `P_h(0)+A_h^(sym) R A_h^(sym)` is ruled out asymptotically by the
independent parity argument in Section 3.

Neither result improves the factoring exponent. The rest of the
full parameterized algebra remains open.

## 1. The orbit certificate

### External symmetry scope

Use external order `(alpha,y,u,beta)`. The eight permutations
preserving the crossing `delta_(alpha,beta) delta_(y,u)` are
generated by `a=(alpha beta)`, `b=(y u)`, and
`s=(alpha y)(beta u)`. The first four images are

| Permutation | Polynomial row matrix |
|---|---|
| identity | `P` |
| `a` | `Q^T` |
| `b` | `Q` |
| `ab` | `P^T` |

The permutation `s` acts on any tensor row matrix by conjugation
with the row-swap matrix `C`. Thus the remaining four images are
`CPC`, `CQ^T C`, `CQC`, and `CP^T C`. The matrix `C=Q_h,h`
already belongs to the four-family algebra. All eight images
therefore belong to that algebra, and all four listed families
are among the eight images. Their generated algebras are equal.
No assumption that external relabeling preserves row
multiplication is used.

### Complete left and right orbit closure

Both orientations preserve the vector of coordinate parities
of the row-label sum `alpha+y`. For `P_h`, this follows from
its difference conservation; for `Q_h`, it is sum conservation.
The vector `v` lies in the parity-zero block.

Let

```math
W=\operatorname{span}\{Tv:T\in A\},\qquad
Z=\operatorname{span}\{v^{\mathsf T}T:T\in A\},
```

where `A` is either of the two algebras just defined.

The checker starts with `v` and applies **every raw coefficient**
of both curves to every newly independent orbit vector. For the
right orbit it applies the transposed coefficient matrices.
Binary elimination continues until both spaces are stable.
Thus closure verifies all words, not a bounded word sample.

It then computes the complete bilinear pairing `Z times W`.
For `A_h^(2)`, the exact results are:

| Energy | Labels | Parity-zero dimension | `dim W` | `dim Z` | Pairing rank | Middle quotient |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 4 | 6 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| 8 | 10 | 26 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 14 |
| 16 | 36 | 214 | 74 | 77 | 0 | 63 |
| 32 | 202 | 4154 | 1912 | 1894 | 0 | 348 |

At energy 32, basis vectors are generated by linear combinations
of words of length at most seven. This is a witness for the
closed orbit bases, not a word-length restriction on the theorem.
Most resulting vectors are nonsymmetric and have nonconstant
diagonal entries; ordinary alternating-matrix symmetry alone
does not explain the vanishing pairing.

For the transpose-closed algebra `A_h^(sym)`, the left and right
orbits coincide. Applying all four coefficient families gives:

| Energy | Parity-zero dimension | Common orbit dimension | Self-pairing rank | Middle quotient |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 6 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| 8 | 26 | 10 | 0 | 6 |
| 16 | 214 | 99 | 0 | 16 |
| 32 | 4154 | 2051 | 0 | 52 |

The energy-32 orbit closes with basis-word length at most six.
This gives a 52-dimensional middle quotient for the full
crossing-symmetry algebra inside its parity-zero block.

## 2. What the finite certificate proves

Every element of the two-sided ideal `J=A R A` is a linear
combination of the outer products

```math
(Tv)(v^{\mathsf T}U),\qquad T,U\in A.
```

Conversely, every outer product from `W times Z` is such a
linear combination. Choosing bases of `W` and `Z` identifies
the ideal vector space with all `dim W` by `dim Z` matrices.
This map is injective because the left basis map is injective
and the right basis map is surjective. Therefore

```math
\dim J=(\dim W)(\dim Z).
```

The zero pairing gives `z(w)=0` for every `w in W,z in Z`.
Products of two displayed outer products consequently vanish:

```math
J^2=0.
```

At energy 32 the two-family ideal has dimension 3621328, and each of its
row matrices has rank at most 1894. Since both orbit spaces are
invariant, the parity-zero module has the invariant flag

```math
0\subset W\subset Z^\perp\subset V_0,
```

with successive dimensions 1912, 348, and 1894. The ideal acts
only from the last quotient into the first subspace. This gives
a precise finite quotient for further searches.

For the full symmetry algebra, the same argument gives ideal
dimension `2051^2=4206601`, maximum ideal row rank 2051, and
successive invariant-flag dimensions 2051, 52, and 2051.

All certificate matrices have entries in `F_2`. Extending the
field preserves their ranks, the spanning relations, and the
zero pairing. This proves the stated extension-field scope.

## 3. An all-size obstruction for parity-zero-image perturbations

This section is independent of the finite orbit dimensions.
Let `h=2^R>=4`, put `d=b(h)`, `a=b(h/2)`, and let `K` be **any**
row matrix whose image is supported on parity-zero label pairs.
It need not belong to the generated ideal or to the Weyl algebra.

The parity-zero pair-space dimension is

```math
n_0=\sum_{k=0}^{h/2}b(k)^2\le a d.
\tag{1}
```

To count it, a common individual parity vector `p` has weighted
energy `e`, and a label is `p+2 alpha'` with lower energy
`k=(h-e)/2`. Each even `e=0,2,...,h` specifies one binary parity
vector. There are `b(k)^2` pairs in that sector. Also
`sum_{k<=h/2}b(k)=b(h)=d`.

Since `P_h(0)` is invertible,

```math
\operatorname{rank}_{\rm row}(P_h(0)+K)\ge d^2-n_0.
\tag{2}
```

There is also a protected column minor of size `d-a-1`.
Choose all labels `beta` of energy `h` having at least one odd
occupation, except the singleton part of weight `h`. There are
`d-a-1` such labels. The sum `sigma=2 beta` has no single bit
of weighted size `2h`: the excluded singleton is the only
single-bit label with an odd occupation. As in the no-carry
split lemma, its occupation bits split into two groups of
energy `h`. Write the resulting no-carry split as
`alpha+u=2 beta`. Both `alpha` and `u` have every occupation
even, since every bit of `2 beta` has positive bit position
within its mode.

In the column cut select this row `(alpha,u)` and the column
`(beta,beta)`. All selected row sums are distinct. Difference
conservation gives an identity submatrix for `P_h(0)`: its
diagonal values are one by the no-carry condition, and its
off-diagonal values vanish because the sums differ.

Every entry of `K` on this entire submatrix is zero. Its
original row-label pair is `(alpha,beta')`, with `alpha`
coordinatewise even and `beta'` having a nonzero parity vector.
Such a pair is outside the row support allowed for `K`.
Therefore

```math
\operatorname{rank}_{\rm col}(P_h(0)+K)\ge d-a-1.
\tag{3}
```

The recurrence `d=b(h-2)+a` and monotonicity give `a<=d/2`
for `h>=4`. Equations (1)--(3) consequently force a rank-sum
contribution at least

```math
\sqrt{(d^2-n_0)(d-a-1)}\ge d^{3/2}/\sqrt8.
```

At energy 32 the exact elementary bounds are row rank at least
36650 and column rank at least 165. Their product is 6047250,
which already exceeds the finite target. Thus the affine coset
`P_h(0)+J` cannot supply the sought better piece. This does not
exclude arbitrary tensors or decompositions in the full
parameterized algebra.

## Reproduction and provenance

```sh
python3 tools/check_weyl_rank_one_ideal.py --output evidence/weyl-rank-one-ideal-h32.json
```

The standard-library driver compiles the existing
`explore_weighted_binomial_crossing.cpp`, regenerates both
energy-32 coefficient dumps, compiles both orbit engines, and
verifies the exact closure and pairing counts for both algebras.
It records source
and dump SHA-256 hashes. It uses no precomputed orbit vectors.
Pass `--energy 2`, `4`, `8`, or `16` for the other finite rows.
An optional `--dump-prefix` reuses existing dumps; the report
then explicitly records that generation was not rerun.

The dump generator uses the original occupation-conservation and
binomial coefficient formula, with coefficient polynomials as
bit masks. The row dump is already in row order. The opposite
dump has column pairs in the other order, so the orbit engine
explicitly swaps those column-pair labels before applying it as
`Q_h`. This convention is also checked against the independent
small Python algebra exploration.
